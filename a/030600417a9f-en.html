Nintendo DSi Remains Playable Thanks to Game Streaming

A hack turns the DSi into a streaming terminal for retro games without hardware modification.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Nintendo DSi, released in 2008, is often seen as obsolete for modern gaming. Yet, the RNDS-Stream project by gavff64 offers an unexpected workaround: streaming games in real-time from a Linux server. The system decodes an MJPEG stream (a lightweight video format) displayed in an X11 or xWayland window, with a latency of 15 to 20 frames per second. Sufficient for casual games like Counter-Strike, but not for competitive titles requiring high responsiveness.

The project leverages RubyNDS, a homebrew framework written in Ruby, specifically designed for the DSi. The author cites personal preference for the language and console as the reason, without technical justification. While audio streaming is not yet implemented, the hack remains functional and open source, available under the MIT license on GitHub. A demonstration is visible in an embedded video, though its editing is tailored for a TikTok audience.

The DSi has a rich history of modifications, including the release of the first flash carts in its launch year. However, homebrew projects for this dual-screen device remain scarce, with a few exceptions like augmented reality applications.
