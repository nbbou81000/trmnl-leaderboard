A Raspberry Pi Modem to Relive Dial-Up and BBS Era

An open-source project recreates dial-up modem experience with a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, BBS-compatible and Hayes command-ready.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Xavi Paz has created RetroBox Modem, a Wi-Fi modem compatible with Hayes commands that emulates the behavior of old dial-up modems, allowing both vintage and modern computers to connect to active Bulletin Board Systems (BBSs). Unlike basic serial-to-Wi-Fi adapters, this project replicates AT commands, dialing sequences, status indicators, and even the iconic connection sounds.

The system is built around a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, which handles Wi-Fi connectivity, network communication, and the Hayes command interpreter. Computers interact with the modem via a serial connection, sending commands like AT or ATD, while RetroBox translates them into TCP/Telnet connections over Wi-Fi. Modern computers connect via USB serial, while older machines use a MAX3232 level converter for RS-232 compatibility. The prototype has been tested with 486 and Pentium-era PCs, as well as modern computers.

The hardware features a 128×64 OLED display for connection details, an I2S audio amplifier with a speaker to reproduce modem sounds, and status LEDs. The entire setup is housed in a 3D-printed enclosure styled after external modems from the 1980s and 1990s. The firmware supports multiple modem generations, with profiles ranging from V.22bis to V.90 and configurable speeds from 300 bps to 56,000 bps.

RetroBox Modem is still under active development, with planned hardware refinements and compatibility improvements. The working prototype already delivers a full retro experience of early online communication without requiring a phone line.
