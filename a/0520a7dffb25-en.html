OpenArm 2.0: An Open-Source Robot to Escape Proprietary Cobot Traps

An open-source dual-arm robot inspired by abandoned cobots like Baxter avoids the risks of proprietary tech lock-in.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The OpenArm 2.0 is a dual-arm partial humanoid robot designed to avoid the pitfalls of proprietary cobots like Rethink Robotics’ Sawyer or Baxter, which vanished after 2018. While these robots were powerful, their reliance on closed software and hardware made them unusable once the company folded. The OpenArm 2.0 embraces open source to ensure long-term viability without manufacturer dependency.

Each arm features 7 degrees of freedom, a 633 mm reach, a nominal payload of 4.1 kg (6 kg peak), a parallel gripper, and an integrated camera. An optional z-axis actuation allows vertical movement. Communication runs over a 1 kHz CAN-FD bus for high responsiveness. The robot can be built from scratch, purchased as a kit, or bought fully assembled.

The base setup costs around $6,500. Additional options include the OpenArm Cell (a smart enclosure with z-axis actuation) and the OpenArm KER, a passive wearable harness with arms that mirror the robot for intuitive teleoperation and training. All hardware, software, and firmware are available on GitHub, with implementations for ROS2, Isaac Lab, and MuJoCo.
