JBR-001: DIY Robot with Vision and Interactive Responses

An open-source robot powered by Arduino UNO Q integrates a camera, sensors, and an animated display for autonomous interactions.

The JBR-001 is a DIY robot designed to explore the intersection of robotics and computer vision. Its core is built around an Arduino UNO Q board housed in a 3D-printed frame. Three servomotors control its movements: one rotates its head, while the other two operate its arms. Unlike many projects, the servomotors are powered by an external supply to avoid overloading the Arduino.

The robot’s perception system combines a front-facing USB-C camera and a Modulino distance sensor located at the back of its head. The camera streams real-time images, while the sensor continuously measures proximity to nearby objects. These inputs enable the robot to react to its surroundings by turning its head, moving its arms, changing its display, or emitting sounds via a Modulino buzzer.

Object Recognition and Custom Reactions

The JBR-001’s standout feature is its ability to identify objects using a computer vision model. Trained via Edge Impulse on a synthetic dataset (images of Arduino Modulino boards in various angles and lighting conditions), the model is deployed on the Arduino UNO Q. Once active, the camera continuously scans the space in front of the robot. When a recognized object is detected, the JBR-001 triggers a predefined action, such as raising an arm or playing a melody. This system can be adapted to recognize other objects (tools, electronic components) and assign specific behaviors to each.

This project demonstrates a modular approach to integrating embedded AI (edge AI) with physical hardware. Design files, code, and detailed instructions are available as open source to encourage further experimentation.
