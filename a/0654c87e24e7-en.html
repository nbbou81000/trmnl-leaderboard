The Rise of Hidden Software-Defined Radios in Common Chips

Hackers are repurposing hidden modes in WiFi and LoRa chips to turn everyday components into versatile software-defined radios.

WiFi chips repurposed as software-defined radios

Hackers have independently uncovered hidden debug modes in ESP32 chips that grant direct access to raw IQ data from the 2.4 GHz radio module. Normally, this data is processed by the MAC hardware for WiFi operations, but these modes allow it to be used for other radio applications.

Enthusiasts quickly expanded this discovery by adding transmission capabilities. Some integrated these features into existing tools like osmosdr, making the chips compatible with standard SDR software. Parallel projects confirmed the feasibility of transmission, enabling applications such as digital TV broadcasts, voice signals, and radio protocols like FM Hell.

Other radio chips revealed

This trend isn’t limited to ESP32. A user identified SDR capabilities in the LR2021 LoRa chipset, designed for long-range communications. This component covers frequencies from 150 MHz to 960 MHz with 16-bit resolution, offering an alternative to traditional SDR solutions. These discoveries echo the early days of RTL-SDR projects, where USB TV tuner dongles were repurposed for radio use, sparking a wave of software and hardware innovations.

Experts note that these breakthroughs drastically reduce the barrier to entry for software-defined radio. With chips already present in many devices, the technical hurdles are lowering, promising a surge of DIY radio projects in the coming months.
