A Fire-Powered Absorption Fridge, No Electricity Needed

A DIY project turns a flame into an absorption cooling solution, ditching the electric compressor.

Unlike traditional refrigerators that rely on electric compressors, this DIY project leverages a century-old principle: absorption refrigeration. Developed by Greenhill Forge, the system uses heat from a fire to drive a thermochemical process. It consists of two main modules: a generator and an absorber, connected by stainless steel pipes.

In the generator, a 38% solution of calcium chloride and water is heated by a flame. The water evaporates due to the heat and travels to a condenser, where it condenses back into liquid. This liquid then passes through an adjustable valve into the evaporator. With pressure maintained at around six millibars (high vacuum), the water boils at a low temperature, absorbing heat from the surroundings and creating a cooling effect.

To prevent vapor buildup and stop the process, a concentrated calcium chloride solution is sprayed into the absorber through a perforated shower head. This solution absorbs the water vapor, maintaining the vacuum required for continuous cooling. Electric pumps, consuming 30 to 50 watts, circulate the fluid between the two modules, while fans assist the system. An Arduino monitors temperatures, pressures, and flow rates, triggering alarms if any issues arise.

The entire structure, made entirely of stainless steel, was assembled using welding and silver brazing to prevent leaks. After assembly, the system was pressurized and evacuated with a vacuum pump to detect and seal even microscopic leaks before being filled with the calcium chloride solution. While it still requires a small amount of electricity for the pumps and fans, this method could provide a practical solution in off-grid areas where firewood or other heat sources are more accessible than electricity.
