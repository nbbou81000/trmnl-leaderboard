Custom Displays Made with Airbrush and Laser Cutter

Researchers simplify DIY electronics with a method to build custom electroluminescent displays using accessible tools.

Building your own displays remains technically challenging, as industrial methods require expensive equipment and specialized skills. Researchers at the University of Bath have developed an accessible alternative with Spray and Etch, a technique combining an airbrush and a laser cutter to create custom electroluminescent displays.

The process starts by spraying a copper-based conductive ink onto a substrate. A standard CO2 laser cutter then etches electrodes by precisely removing sections of this layer. Additional layers of dielectric material, electroluminescent phosphor, and a transparent conductive coating are applied, followed by another laser etching step to finalize the electrode structures. A key innovation is depth-selective laser etching, which allows removing one layer without damaging underlying layers by adjusting the laser’s power, speed, and focus. This enables the construction of complex multilayer electronic structures.

The team demonstrated the feasibility by fabricating a functional 32×32-pixel display with 0.4 mm pixels (50.8 PPI), a resolution far exceeding previous DIY electroluminescent prototypes (16 to 30 PPI). The method also allows experimenting with multicolor pixels by stacking different electroluminescent phosphors, though brightness and color separation still need improvement. Spray and Etch can even be applied to gently curved surfaces, as shown by illuminated features integrated into a game controller, glasses, or an artificial fingernail.

Despite limitations in laser calibration, material compatibility, and reliable multicolor displays, this approach paves the way for custom displays in DIY electronics projects without relying on off-the-shelf panels.
