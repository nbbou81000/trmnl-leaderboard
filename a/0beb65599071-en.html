Saving a Failing EGA CRT with a Software Hack

A retro assembly hack forces a failing EGA CRT into 640×350 mode to bypass a defective tube.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

In the early 1990s, a computer enthusiast inherited a PC clone with an EGA graphics card and a 14-inch CRT monitor. The CRT soon showed signs of failure: the image warped and bulged after just three minutes of warming up, but only in 320×200 mode. The sharper 640×350 mode worked perfectly. However, most games of the time, including Civilization, relied on the low-resolution mode. Replacing the tube was not an option due to cost.

The solution came in the form of a TSR (Terminate and Stay Resident) program written in assembly. This software intercepted any attempt to switch to 320×200 mode and forcibly switched the display to 640×350 instead. The game continued to run as if nothing had changed, though with visual artifacts: odd lines appeared in the top left, even lines in the top right, and text became unreadable in part of the screen. Despite these compromises, the hack allowed playing for months until a new graphics card and monitor could be afforded.
