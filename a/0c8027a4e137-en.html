16 Critical Checks Before Sending a PCB to Fabrication

A 16-point checklist to prevent costly mistakes before sending a PCB to production.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Before sending a printed circuit board (PCB) to fabrication, a critical step is verifying its design to ensure it meets Design for Manufacturing (DFM) requirements. Poor preparation can lead to delays, extra costs, or even production failure. PCB manufacturer JLCPCB shared a 16-point checklist on X to review before submitting files to production.

The checks cover technical aspects such as trace width, component spacing, Gerber file compatibility, and short-circuit prevention. Other points include layer management, component orientation, and manufacturer-specific standards. For instance, a trace that is too narrow may break during soldering, while insufficient spacing between components can make assembly impossible.

According to JLCPCB, these preventive checks are the most cost-effective: an error caught early only requires a few minutes to fix, compared to days or weeks if detected after production. The full list is available on X, where the manufacturer explains each point in detail.
