DSPi Turns Raspberry Pi Pico into USB Sound Card with Built-in DSP Engine

An open-source firmware for RP2040/RP2350 adds advanced audio processing over USB without extra hardware.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The DSPi project offers a full-featured firmware for Raspberry Pi RP2040 and RP2350 microcontrollers, including the Pico and Pico 2 boards. It turns these platforms into a USB sound card with an integrated digital signal processing (DSP) engine. The firmware enables advanced audio tools like room correction, active crossover filters, parametric EQ, and time alignment, all accessible directly from a computer.

Technical Features

DSPi uses a dual-core architecture to split audio processing across both cores of the RP2040 and RP2350. As a USB sound card, it supports 16-bit and 24-bit PCM streams at 44.1 kHz, 48 kHz, and 96 kHz, compatible with macOS, Windows, Linux, and iOS. It also includes an S/PDIF input (24-bit, stereo, 44.1 or 48 kHz) and digital audio outputs (S/PDIF or I2S) with up to four independent stereo slots (eight channels on RP2350, four on RP2040). A dedicated subwoofer output, using a high-performance 24-bit delta-sigma modulator, is also available.
