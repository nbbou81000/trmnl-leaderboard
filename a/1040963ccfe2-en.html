Inside a 1980s Switched-Capacitor Filter IC: The Harris HF10

A mystery 1980s IC uncovers a dual switched-capacitor filter, a clever design for tunable analog signal processing.

A mystery integrated circuit (IC) dated the 47th week of 1985 and marked with an unrecognized logo was handed to Ken Shirriff by CuriousMarc. After opening the ceramic package with a hammer and chisel, microscopic analysis revealed a Harris HF10, a variant of the National Semiconductor MF10, a dual switched-capacitor filter.

This type of filter uses capacitors whose switching mimics variable resistors, allowing dynamic adjustment of frequency response (low-pass, band-pass, or high-pass). Compared to traditional resistors, this method is more compact and offers unmatched flexibility for its time, especially in analog synthesizers where filter modulation was essential. Modern versions, such as the TI MF10-N, are still available in PDIP or SOIC packages, but no longer in CDIP.

The original IC, designed for applications requiring analog precision, reflects a period where space and power optimization were major challenges. Its use in 1980s audio or electronic equipment, such as synthesizers, highlights its utility. However, its availability is dwindling, raising concerns about its gradual disappearance.
