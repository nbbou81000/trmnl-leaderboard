A Squishy Cow Udder Game Controller Made of Silicone

Karina Chow turns a cow udder into a soft alternative game controller for a Rhythm Heaven Fever-inspired rhythm game.

Karina Chow, a creative technologist and maker, designed a squishy cow udder-shaped game controller for her rhythm game Udder Mayhem, inspired by Rhythm Heaven Fever. Born from a class project at NYU, this controller uses pressure on four silicone teats to trigger in-game actions, with each teat functioning as a button or key press.

Chow experimented with various pressure detection methods, including Hall effect sensors embedded in metal tubes, but ultimately chose force-sensitive resistors (FSR) for their reliability and comfort. These sensors avoided alignment issues and the unpleasant tactile feedback of earlier solutions. The udder is molded from Smooth-On Dragon Skin 20 silicone, a soft material (Shore hardness 20A) commonly used for prosthetics and masks, dyed in fluorescent pink for a striking visual effect.

Mold fabrication and assembly

The project relies on a two-part mold, available as a single block (166 × 209 × 146 mm) or in four parts for smaller printers. The mold includes a central insert and four rods for the teats. After applying a mold release spray (like Mann Ease Release 200), the silicone is poured into each quadrant and rotated to distribute the material evenly. Once cured, the rods are removed by twisting, and the insert is carefully demolded. Internal channels in the mold guide the silicone to the correct areas.

Chow advises using vinyl gloves and clear mixing containers, along with a vacuum chamber to remove bubbles. The Smooth-On Silc Pig Electric Pink pigments provide a vibrant color under ambient or UV light, ideal for dark environments like arcade cabinets.
