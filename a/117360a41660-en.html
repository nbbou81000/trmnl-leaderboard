Hack the North 2026’s Custom ESP32 Badge: Design Insights and Challenges

A programmable electronic badge designed for a hackathon faced major technical hurdles before mass production.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

To replace traditional paper badges, the Hack the North 2026 team designed a customizable electronic badge featuring a screen, buttons, LEDs, a speaker, wireless connectivity, and a battery. The initial goal was to keep costs under $5 per badge, but this proved unachievable.

The first prototype, hexagonal in shape, included an ESP32 microcontroller, a color display, addressable LEDs, and an NFC chip. Early testing revealed multiple issues: a component mix-up led to a capacitor being soldered where an inductor should have been, and directly soldering the display’s flexible ribbon cable to the PCB proved impractical for manufacturing. These flaws were addressed by adding an FPC connector and redesigning the board to resemble a miniature handheld gaming console. The NFC chip, initially selected, could only function as a tag, not a reader, requiring a hardware change.

On the software side, the badge ran custom firmware with a Lua runtime for app development, a browser-based code editor, and a shell for file management. Wireless interactions were possible via Bluetooth and an accelerometer. After testing 50 units, assembly errors (incorrectly oriented ICs) and risks of smoke in case of reverse battery insertion were identified. Reverse-polarity protection was added, and production began in June, delivering 1,750 badges by late August at a unit cost of about $11.60.

During the hackathon, attendees used the badges to create multiplayer games, a rhythm game, a remote-control vehicle interface, and even a mesh-networked version of Among Us. Hardware designs, firmware, and a browser-based emulator are now available as open-source projects.
