Clean Up Your Home Assistant Before HACS’s Official Marketplace Transition

HACS is becoming an official Home Assistant marketplace: time to remove abandoned integrations for better performance and security.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

As the Home Assistant Community Store (HACS) moves toward an official, curated marketplace model, users are urged to audit their installations and remove abandoned integrations. These outdated components—often installed for testing or long-discarded devices—clutter the system, degrading performance and introducing risks.

Unmanaged custom_components can slow down boot times, bloat databases (SQLite or PostgreSQL) with unnecessary logs, and spike CPU usage. Some may trigger timeout errors or poll dead endpoints, compounding the issue. More critically, unmaintained integrations pose security risks, as they lack the automatic patches provided by official Home Assistant updates.

To clean up, the author suggests checking startup logs (*Settings System Logs*) for warnings or errors tied to custom integrations. A manual review of the config/custom_components folder helps identify remnants of old devices or abandoned projects. These should be removed via the HACS interface, followed by deleting leftover folders to prevent conflicts.

The shift to an official HACS marketplace improves component validation, reducing compatibility and security risks. Popular integrations are increasingly absorbed into Home Assistant’s core, reducing reliance on custom solutions. After cleanup, restarting the system reveals noticeable performance and stability gains.
