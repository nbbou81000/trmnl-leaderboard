3 Weekend Projects to Turn Dumb Devices into Smart Ones

Three DIY tutorials to automate everyday devices with Home Assistant and microcontrollers for under $10 each.

A standard mechanical water meter can be turned into a smart monitoring tool without replacing it. A Home Assistant user connected a proximity sensor to an ESP8266 and placed a magnet on the rotating dial of the meter. Each full rotation, representing one liter of water consumed, is recorded by the sensor. The hardware costs around $10 and requires no soldering for the sensor. A detailed guide is available on the creator’s blog.

A classic ceiling fan can also gain smart features. Another project uses an ESP32 wired to the fan’s remote buttons. With ESPHome, the microcontroller simulates button presses, allowing remote control via Home Assistant. The total cost is estimated at $9, and this method remains useful for fans using non-standard RF frequencies (such as 403 MHz).

A basic washing machine can be transformed into a smart appliance without physical modifications. Several low-cost sensors are sufficient: a smart plug measuring power consumption or a vibration sensor detecting the machine’s movements. A motion sensor in the laundry room triggers a voice alert on a smart speaker when the user enters, while a contact sensor on the door signals when it’s opened. These solutions work locally, even without internet access, and offer features superior to some commercial "smart" appliances.
