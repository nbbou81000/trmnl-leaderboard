M-Vave FM-1: An Open-Source Synth for Tinkerers

A $60 portable FM synth evolves into an open-source platform thanks to a thriving community and an official SDK.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The M-Vave FM-1, a $59.72 portable FM synth inspired by the Yamaha DX7, was repurposed by a passionate community within months. Originally featuring 27 soft-touch keys and a 1.54-inch TFT screen, the device’s stock firmware was reverse-engineered by users as early as July 2026. Custom builds emerged in September before the manufacturer responded in October by releasing FM1 Open.

This open-source kit includes an SDK, a browser-based development workspace, and the FM-1’s firmware v15 under GPLv3. While the application layer is now modifiable, the synthesis engine remains a closed binary. Several community builds have already been tested: Melodee adds emulations of the CZ-1, DX7, drums, and a Commodore 64 SID, while Felucca runs over a dozen engines at once with a sampler and pattern sequencer. SLOOP even turns the unit into an eight-track battery-powered groovebox capable of running Doom.

One user encountered issues after skipping the v15 firmware update before flashing a custom build, but recovered the device using the online workspace’s force-flash tool.
