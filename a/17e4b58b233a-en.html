Lunar Lander Recreated on the Legendary 1959 PDP-1

A developer ports Lunar Lander to the PDP-1, one of DEC’s earliest computers, with a working graphical version.

The PDP-1, the first computer in Digital Equipment Corporation’s (DEC) PDP line, made computing history in 1959. Yet developer Michael Gardi has managed to run a graphical version of Lunar Lander, an iconic game, on this early machine with limited resources.

While the original game, developed on a PDP-8 in 1969, is often linked to Atari’s 1979 arcade version, its text-based predecessor dates back to 1959. Unlike later BASIC versions, this implementation leverages the PDP-1’s graphical capabilities, despite the machine being less powerful than the PDP-8. The result echoes SpaceWar, another legendary game created for the PDP-1, which inspired generations of developers.

The project is available online as an emulation, allowing users to test the game without physical hardware. Michael Gardi hopes to run it one day on one of the few surviving working PDP-1 units, a goal aligned with efforts to preserve computing heritage. This work is part of a retrocomputing contest, offering participants a chance to win prizes while exploring even older machines.
