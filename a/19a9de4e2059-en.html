Space Invaders Displayed on a Retro VFD Screen

A DIY project brings Space Invaders to life on a vintage vacuum fluorescent display (VFD).

Vacuum fluorescent displays (VFDs) were once common in consumer electronics, from microwave clocks to stereo systems, before LCDs took over. Their distinctive glow and retro aesthetic make them a compelling target for hardware hackers. [José Luis Monteiro] and his father have breathed new life into a salvaged VFD by adapting it to run Space Invaders.

The project highlights the technical challenges of working with VFDs, particularly their limited vertical resolution, which slightly impacts gameplay. However, the game’s sprites blend seamlessly with the VFD’s glowing phosphor display, enhancing its vintage charm. The team provides detailed documentation and source code, offering a valuable resource for anyone interested in retro hardware hacking. A companion video further explains the process, making the project engaging even for those not actively seeking to port DOOM to a microwave.

This work is part of a broader trend of reviving obsolete technologies for creative projects. VFDs, though less common than LCDs, offer a unique visual appeal with their dynamic glow and energy efficiency. Retro computing enthusiasts see them as a way to recreate the aesthetics of the 1980s, as seen in similar projects like Snake on a seven-segment display.
