New STM32H7P4/P5 520 MHz MCUs with AI and Signal Accelerators

STMicroelectronics unveils STM32H7P4 and STM32H7P5 520 MHz MCUs with hardware accelerators for AI and signal processing.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

STMicroelectronics has launched two new microcontrollers (MCUs) in the STM32H7P family: the STM32H7P4 and STM32H7P5, both running at 520 MHz with an Arm Cortex-M7 core. Designed for high-performance applications such as motor control, power conversion, industrial sensing, and edge AI, these MCUs integrate advanced hardware accelerators.

Both models feature a Hardware Signal Processor (HSP), a dedicated accelerator for mathematical and signal-processing tasks like FFT, filtering, and convolution. ST claims the HSP can perform these operations up to five times faster than a software implementation on the Cortex-M7. The STM32H7P5 adds a Neural-ART NPU (Neural Processing Unit) capable of delivering up to 85 GOPS (0.085 TOPS) for AI inference, including TinyML models like ST’s arc-fault circuit interrupter (AFCI), which runs up to 40 times faster than a Cortex-M7-based software solution.

The MCUs offer up to 2MB of dual-bank flash with error correction (ECC), 736KB of SRAM, and external memory interfaces such as Octo-SPI (supporting PSRAM, NOR flash, HyperRAM) and the Flexible Memory Controller (FMC) (for SRAM, SDRAM). Peripherals include up to 134 GPIOs, four 12-bit ADCs at 5 MSPS, three CAN FD interfaces, a USB OTG Full-Speed controller, and three advanced motor-control timers. Security features include code readout protection, a NIST-compliant TRNG, and cryptographic accelerators (HASH, SHA).

ST provides a full development ecosystem: STM32CubeH7P with HAL2, middleware, and examples, STM32CubeMX for peripheral configuration, STM32CubeIDE for development and debugging, and STM32CubeProgrammer for firmware flashing. Two development boards are available: the NUCLEO-H7P5II (with integrated ST-LINK) and the STM32H7P5I-DK (featuring an OLED display, MEMS microphone, microSD slot, and motor-control expansion connector).

The MCUs are currently in sampling for OEM customers, with mass production scheduled for January 2027. Pricing starts at $4.43 for a 1MB flash model in an LQFP100 package (10,000-unit quantity).
