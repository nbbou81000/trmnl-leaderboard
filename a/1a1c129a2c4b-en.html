Crescent City Dev Board: A Compact ESP32-S3 with OLED Display and Battery Support

A new all-in-one ESP32-S3 development board integrates a 0.96" OLED display, LiPo battery support, and Qwiic connectors for compact projects.

Engineer Hao Wen is preparing a crowdfunding campaign for the Crescent City Dev Board, a compact development board built around an Espressif ESP32-S3 module. The board integrates a 0.96" monochrome OLED display (128×64 pixels, SSD1306 controller), lithium-polymer battery support rechargeable via USB Type-C, and a Qwiic/STEMMA QT connector for solderless expansion.

The core of the board features an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N8 module with dual Tensilica Xtensa LX7 cores running up to 240 MHz, 512 KB of static RAM, an additional 16 KB from the real-time clock, and 8 MB of external flash memory. Connectivity includes Wi-Fi 4 and Bluetooth 5 Low Energy (BLE), though the stock firmware documentation does not explicitly mention BLE support. The board offers 12 GPIO pins, seven of which can function as analog inputs, though three share connectivity with the Wi-Fi module, limiting their availability during Wi-Fi operation.

The open-source stock firmware includes a Wi-Fi weather clock example, displaying local time, real-time weather conditions, daily high and low temperatures, and indoor humidity via a Qwiic sensor. A battery level indicator is also included. Configuration is handled directly through a web browser, requiring no app or account. The board is programmable via the Arduino IDE (ESP32 board package) or ESPHome, with ready-made examples for a Qwiic temperature/humidity sensor or a battery-powered data logger.

Prior to its official launch on Crowd Supply, Hao Wen has released the board’s schematic and 3D model, though these files are source-available with all rights reserved, preventing private or commercial reproduction. The firmware and code examples, however, will be distributed under the permissive MIT license. The Crescent City Dev Board targets projects requiring an integrated display, battery management, and Qwiic connectivity without additional modules.
