Bluetooth Extended to 12 Kilometers: A Surprising Technical Feat

A maker pushes Bluetooth’s limits to 12 km using a parabolic antenna and an ultra-sensitive receiver, without altering transmit power.

Bluetooth, typically confined to short-range uses like peripherals or trackers, has seen its range pushed far beyond the usual few meters. Maker nktbuilds demonstrated that 12 kilometers is achievable by combining two technical levers: a parabolic directional antenna and an ultra-low-noise receiver. Unlike standard dipole antennas, this setup captures far more signal and minimizes interference.

The trick lies in optimizing reception rather than increasing transmit power. The device used remains a standard Bluetooth tracker, with no hardware modifications to the transmitter. This approach highlights that a protocol’s range depends as much on reception quality as on transmission power. However, Bluetooth remains unsuitable for long-distance communications: technologies like LoRA, capable of covering hundreds of kilometers in line of sight, are far better suited.

A key technical limitation remains: the speed of light imposes a maximum delay of 10 microseconds for Bluetooth Classic exchanges. This restricts the distance between synchronized devices to about 1.5 km, even with optimized reception. Commenters also noted legal constraints related to directional antennas, which may violate EIRP (equivalent isotropic radiated power) limits depending on local regulations.
