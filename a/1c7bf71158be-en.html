DIY Smart Thermostat Without Subscription Using ESP32 and Home Assistant

A YouTuber builds an open-source smart thermostat to bypass mandatory subscriptions from commercial models.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Frustrated by mandatory subscriptions for smart thermostats, a creator built an open-source alternative without recurring fees. The project uses an ESP32 microcontroller, a temperature sensor, an OLED display, and a rotary encoder for input. Power is supplied via a buck converter, stepping down the HVAC system’s 24 V to the required 5 V.

Unlike traditional thermostats that simply complete a circuit (for heating, ventilation, or cooling), this digital version includes a precise temperature sensor and a microcontroller. Basic functions like automatic regulation or scheduling are handled locally by the microcontroller. For example, the system can activate the heater if the temperature drops below 20 °C and the last heating cycle ended over five minutes ago.

For advanced features like remote control or complex scenarios, the creator relies on Home Assistant (via the Matter protocol), a free and open-source platform. This solution integrates the thermostat into an existing smart home ecosystem without relying on a proprietary app or subscription. The entire project is documented and freely accessible, providing an autonomous alternative to locked-down commercial solutions.
