Chili Pepper: A Single-Board Solution for DIY Electronic Instruments

An open-source board combining microcontroller, audio, and connectivity to build digital musical instruments without complex assembly.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Building a DIY electronic musical instrument often requires assembling multiple modules—microcontroller, audio hardware, input interfaces, storage, and connectivity—before even playing a single note. Chili Pepper streamlines this process by consolidating everything onto a single open-source board. Powered by an Espressif ESP32-S3, it includes 16 MB of flash storage and 8 MB of PSRAM, providing the processing power needed for synthesizers, samplers, grooveboxes, and MIDI controllers.

Connectivity is handled via Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy, enabling wireless communication with other devices. For user input, Chili Pepper offers 24 capacitive touch inputs, managed by two MPR121 controllers, suitable for creating electronic keyboards or unconventional instruments like digital handpans. Audio output is delivered through a Texas Instruments PCM5102 DAC, providing stereo, 32-bit line-level audio, alongside a microSD card slot for storing samples and other data.

The board also features native USB MIDI support and a CH343 USB-to-serial interface. Measuring 93.3 × 59 mm and supporting an input voltage range of 1.8 V to 5.5 V, it is well-suited for portable, battery-powered projects. Two optional controllers are available: Panorythe, an ammonite-shaped interface with 16 tactile pads, and French Baguette, designed for sampling and MIDI performances.

The project emphasizes openness, with software licensed under Apache 2.0 and hardware/mechanical designs under CERN-OHL-S-2.0. Hardware design files will be released before fulfillment, while firmware and example projects are already publicly available. A crowdfunding campaign is planned, though pricing and launch date remain undisclosed. Interested makers can sign up to receive updates when the campaign goes live.
