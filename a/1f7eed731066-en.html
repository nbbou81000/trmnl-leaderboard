56k.rip: Relive the 1996 Web Experience with a Virtual Dial-Up Modem

An online simulator recreates the 1996 dial-up internet experience, complete with Windows 95 and a miniature retro web.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

56k.rip offers a full dive into the pre-broadband era by simulating a dial-up internet connection from the 1990s. Upon loading, users are greeted by a fake BIOS screen followed by a Windows 95 desktop, complete with the Start button and a Dial-Up Networking icon. Connecting requires following an 11-step process, including classic AT commands (ATZ, ATDT 555-0156) and a 25-second wait before receiving a CONNECT 50666/ARQ, accompanied by the iconic dial-up sound.

Once online, the experience enforces a byte budget that mimics the real-world speeds of a 28.8 or 56K modem. Web pages load progressively, image by image, replicating the slow, compressed JPG loading of the era. The simulator features a miniature retro web, limited to fictional content such as personal pages (like Gary’s vacation photos), a guestbook, classifieds, weather updates, an email client, a chat room, Minesweeper, and a drawing tool. Users cannot browse freely; they are confined to this curated retro environment.

The simulation also includes nostalgic touches like a warning not to pick up the phone to avoid disconnection. While 56K modems didn’t arrive until 1997 (with the V.90 standard officially approved in 1998), the experience captures the essence of the 1990s, before social media and instant connections took over.
