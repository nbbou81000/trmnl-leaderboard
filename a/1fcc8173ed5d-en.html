Rouse: A DIY ESP32 Alarm Clock Without Subscriptions or Apps

A customizable ESP32-based alarm clock, built from scratch without online dependencies.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Rouse is a DIY bedside alarm clock built around an ESP32 development board and a 3D-printed enclosure, featuring a 3.5-inch touchscreen. It includes standard alarm clock functions such as up to ten independent alarms, each configurable by time, sound, volume, and snooze duration. A gradual volume increase and a sunrise simulation slowly brighten the screen before the alarm triggers, while an extra-loud mode targets heavy sleepers. A physical snooze bar on top of the enclosure allows users to silence or snooze the alarm without touching the screen.

The clock displays current weather conditions, sunrise and sunset times, and upcoming alarms. Its interface can be customized with fonts, colors, backgrounds (including personal photos), and built-in sleep sounds like rain, ocean waves, and white noise. Three compatible ESP32 touchscreen boards are supported: Freenove FNK0104N, Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-3.5 (with built-in motion sensor), or CYD E32R35T. Assembly involves 3D printing the case, installing the display board, connecting a small speaker, and wiring two tactile switches for the snooze bar. An optional LiPo battery ensures operation during power outages.

Firmware is installed via a browser-based flashing tool, requiring no complex setup. Once connected to Wi-Fi, the clock is configured through a local web interface accessible from any device. The project provides free firmware, 3D-printing files, and a detailed build guide.
