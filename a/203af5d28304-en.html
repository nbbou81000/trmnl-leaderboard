ESP32 Now Transmits as an SDR: Hellschreiber and ZigBee Ahead

An experimental firmware lets the ESP32 transmit as an SDR on 2.4 GHz, paving the way for protocols like Hellschreiber and ZigBee.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Until now, ESP32-based software-defined radio (SDR) projects were limited to receiving signals in the 2.4 GHz band. This limitation has now been overcome thanks to firmware developed by Murmel, which enables the chip to transmit as well. The early code, compatible with the gqrx tool, was successfully tested between an ESP32-S3 and an ESP32-C6 using a custom protocol inspired by Hellschreiber, a visual telegraphy transmission mode.

The author is also working on 802.15.4 modulation, which could allow the ESP32 to communicate using ZigBee. These advancements remain confined to the 2.4 GHz band, but Espressif's API suggests the hardware could theoretically go as low as 100 MHz. However, the effectiveness of this lower range remains uncertain due to built-in filter limitations.

These developments build on earlier work that exploited the raw radio capabilities of the ESP32, initially limited to listening. The goal now is to expand the possibilities of DIY SDR, much like what happened with RTL-SDR dongles. Commenters, however, highlight the risks of interference, particularly with reserved bands for aviation or critical communications, where unauthorized use could prove problematic.
