ESP32-S3: Internal Memory, the Real Culprit Behind Bluetooth/Wi-Fi Crashes

An overloaded ESP32-S3 running Bluetooth and Wi-Fi simultaneously exhausts its internal RAM, leading to silent lockups without rebooting.

ESP32-S3 devices share a single antenna for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, managed via time-division multiplexing. This method splits radio access between the two protocols but doesn’t directly affect memory. The real issue lies in the 512 KB of internal SRAM, shared between both protocol buffers and software components like ESPHome.

By enabling a Bluetooth proxy on an ESP32-S3 Sense with 8 MB of PSRAM, the author observed an immediate consumption of 90 to 110 KB of internal SRAM by the proxy. Memory fragmentation (measured at 86 % by ESPHome, compared to 73 % without Bluetooth) reduced available contiguous memory blocks, critical for allocating new data. Even with 6 KB of free SRAM, the module couldn’t handle network connections, causing silent radio failure without apparent errors.

Home Assistant continued displaying the last sensor values for two minutes before marking the data as stale. The module eventually rebooted automatically after 15 minutes of inactivity, due to a timeout configured in ESPHome. Recommended optimizations, such as reducing Bluetooth connection slots or enabling use_psram (to move buffers to PSRAM), provided only a marginal gain of 100 KB—insufficient to resolve the issue.
