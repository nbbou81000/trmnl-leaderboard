Radxa Q8B: A $209 SBC Outperforming the Pi 5

The Radxa Q8B, powered by a Snapdragon 8cx Gen 3, outperforms the Raspberry Pi 5 in nearly every aspect, yet its supply remains uncertain.

The Radxa Q8B, a $209 single-board computer (SBC), stands out with its Qualcomm Snapdragon 8cx Gen 3 processor, already used in Microsoft’s Windows Dev Kit 2023. This chip delivers significantly better performance than the Raspberry Pi 5, featuring double the CPU cores, a faster GPU, a dedicated NPU, and a more advanced manufacturing process for improved energy efficiency.

On paper, the Q8B outperforms the Pi 5 across all metrics: connectivity (two 2.5 Gbps Ethernet ports, multiple M.2 slots for NVMe or WiFi), I/O capabilities (extended PCIe, full GPIO), and software compatibility (ARM64 Linux, including Radxa’s official distributions). However, its setup and configuration are less straightforward than the Pi 5’s. The OS selection follows a choose-your-own-adventure guide, with installation methods varying by storage medium. The author highlights the inability to boot a generic ARM64 ISO from a USB drive to install the system on an NVMe drive.

Once configured with Radxa OS, the Q8B performs like an N150 mini-PC, featuring a smooth interface and lag-free 4K video playback. Benchmarks confirm its advantages: in multicore tasks, it is over twice as fast as the Pi 5, while maintaining decent energy efficiency despite higher power draw under load (over 20 W). The PCIe ports and M.2 slots offer expansion potential, though ARM64 peripheral compatibility remains unproven.

The main obstacle to adopting the Q8B is its availability. In the U.S., delivery times stretch to three months, with inconsistent stock levels. The author had to order in June to receive their unit in September, a delay incompatible with urgent projects. This shortage echoes past supply chain issues, despite competitive pricing for an SBC of this class.
