ESP32 Chips Repurposed as Hidden SDR Receivers by DIY Projects

Hackers leverage an undocumented ESP32 feature to turn these microcontrollers into SDR receivers spanning 2.2 to 6 GHz.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Several open-source projects have uncovered an undocumented ESP32 feature that bypasses the built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth radio limitations. By enabling this hidden function, users can capture raw IQ baseband samples directly from the processor, effectively turning the microcontroller into a standalone software-defined radio (SDR).

The frequency ranges vary by model: most ESP32 chips cover 2.2 to 2.7 GHz, while the ESP32-C5 extends this to 6 GHz. Sample rates reach up to 80 MS/s (mega-samples per second), with analog bandwidths ranging from 13 to 54 MHz depending on the chip. However, most models can only export these samples as snapshots to a PC, restricting their use to spectrum analysis rather than continuous reception.

The ESP32-S31 stands out by enabling continuous streaming at up to 16 MS/s over Gigabit Ethernet, offering a more capable real-time solution. A project called ESP-WebSDR simplifies deployment: flashing the firmware from a browser onto most ESP32 dev boards provides a live spectrum analyzer and waterfall display.

Two other projects leverage this discovery in different ways. One pairs an ESP32-S3 with an FPGA as a USB 3.0 front end for continuous IQ streaming. The other, C5VRX, uses an ESP32-C5 as a 5.8 GHz FPV video receiver, demodulating the signal onboard and outputting composite video via a resistor DAC.
