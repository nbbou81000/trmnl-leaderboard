A DIY 500W Power Station That Fits in Your Palm

A YouTuber builds a compact portable power station that can run a desktop PC while fast-charging multiple devices.

To bridge the gap between portability and power, Gadget Industry built a DIY 500W power station small enough to fit in one hand. Unlike standard power banks, which typically power only laptops, this prototype runs a desktop PC via an integrated AC outlet while offering two USB-C ports (140W and 65W) for fast-charging phones or laptops.

The system’s core uses four 3.2V, 15Ah LiFePO4 cells wired in series to form a 12.8V battery (14.6V when fully charged). These cells support a 3C discharge rate, delivering up to 45A continuously. A 100A Battery Management System (BMS) handles cell balancing, overcurrent, and short-circuit protection. Integrating a 300W inverter into such a compact space required drastic modifications: disassembly, PCB cutting, component relocation, and moving MOSFETs to a large aluminum heat sink. A thermal switch at 55 °C activates a cooling fan only when needed, avoiding unnecessary power drain and noise.

The USB-C modules were also adapted: their connectors were desoldered and extended with wires to optimize space, while the 140W module, being bidirectional, doubles as the primary charging input. A boost converter and voltage regulator allow slow battery charging via a standard 5V phone charger when a suitable USB-PD adapter isn’t available. The entire setup is encased in a custom dark green epoxy resin enclosure, with a matching lid featuring openings for ports, ventilation, indicator lights, and a power button.

During testing, the power station successfully powered a desktop PC while fast-charging two smartphones simultaneously. The creator provides a detailed breakdown of the build in a dedicated video.
