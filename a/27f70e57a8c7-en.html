Adafruit Unveils MCP2222 Breakout Board to Control Displays and Sensors Directly from a PC

Adafruit introduces a USB interface board to let a PC communicate directly with OLED displays, sensors, or LEDs without an intermediate microcontroller.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Adafruit has replaced its MCP221A-based interface board with a new version built around Microchip’s MCP2222 chip. This integrated circuit enables direct communication between a PC and electronic peripherals via standard protocols like SPI, I2C, or UART, along with configurable GPIO pins supporting 3.3V or 5V logic levels.

The compact board features a USB Type-C connector on one end and a Qwiic/STEMMA QT port on the other, allowing quick connections to compatible modules. GPIO pins along the edges expand interfacing options. Adafruit highlights that this solution eliminates the need for an intermediate microcontroller like an Arduino when testing or controlling devices directly from a PC, streamlining development and prototyping.

However, the new version comes with two key limitations compared to its predecessor. First, it no longer includes analog-to-digital (ADC) and digital-to-analog (DAC) converters. Second, the SPI bus experiences inter-byte gaps, preventing its use for NeoPixel-style commands that require MOSI signal modulation.

The MCP2222 breakout board is now available on Adafruit’s store for $10.95, a notable price increase from the $6.50 of the original model.
