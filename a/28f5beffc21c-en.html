VoltGuard: An Open-Source Circuit to Protect Your Car Battery

An open-source project to prevent dash cams from draining car batteries, even in 12 V systems.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Dash cams are useful for recording incidents even when parked, but they can drain a car’s 12 V battery if left powered. Commercial hardwire kits are supposed to cut power when the battery voltage drops too low, but they don’t always work as intended. Dieter Santy tested a dash cam kit and found its protection circuit failed to activate even as the voltage dropped dangerously low.

An open-source, configurable solution

To address this, Santy developed VoltGuard, an open-source power board that monitors battery voltage and automatically disconnects the dash cam when it falls below a configurable threshold. The system uses an Infineon TLE8366EV50 automotive buck converter to step down the 12 V supply to 5 V for the dash cam, along with a resettable fuse, transient voltage suppression diodes, and a P-channel MOSFET to prevent electrical faults and reverse polarity. A Texas Instruments TPS3700 voltage detector continuously checks the battery via a resistor divider and triggers the MOSFET to cut power when the voltage drops below a set level. Santy set a cutoff voltage of 12.2 V for his setup.

The circuit also includes an optocoupler to monitor the vehicle’s ignition-switched accessory line, keeping the dash cam powered while driving. A separate accessory-status signal is generated so the camera can distinguish between driving and parking conditions. The PCB was designed in KiCad, hand-assembled, and housed in a custom 3D-printed enclosure made from PETG, chosen for its heat resistance (up to 135 °F during operation).

The complete KiCad project files, bill of materials, and 3D-printable enclosure designs are available under the CERN-OHL-P open hardware license, allowing for modifications and community-driven improvements.
