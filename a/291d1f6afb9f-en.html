A Miniature, Functional Cray-1A Built on FPGA

An enthusiast recreates a Cray-1A supercomputer at 1/10 scale on FPGA, with binary compatibility and cycle accuracy.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A personal project spanning about a year and a half resulted in a functional replica of the Cray-1A, one of the first vector supercomputers from the 1970s. The goal was to build a 1/10-scale version that is fully binary-compatible and cycle-accurate. The system’s core runs on a Xilinx Spartan-3E 1600 FPGA board, utilizing roughly 75% of the available logic resources and all of the embedded block RAM.

The prototype operates at a clock speed of 33 MHz, with 4 kilowords of memory, compared to the original machine’s 80 MHz and up to 32 MB. The Cray-1A’s architecture is fully pipelined, featuring 12 execution units and vector registers capable of holding 64 values each. Notably, there is no dedicated divide instruction—instead, division is performed by computing a reciprocal approximation in exactly 13 cycles, followed by a multiplication.

The biggest challenge was obtaining compatible software. Historical archives, including those from the Computer History Museum, were incomplete, and requests to access SGI’s Cray archives (which had taken over Cray’s software) proved unsuccessful. Early programs were hand-assembled in octal. A later update introduced a CAL assembler rewritten in Python, context-switching exchange packages, a 64-bit UART, and optimized multipliers capable of reaching 50 MHz. The build also includes a wooden and MDF replica case with a pleather couch seat around the base.
