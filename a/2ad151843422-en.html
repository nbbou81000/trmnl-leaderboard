Arduino Drives Sony Car Radio LCD Display

A hacker reverse-engineered a Sony car radio LCD to drive it with Arduino by decoding the SANYO LC75826W driver protocol.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Jose Luis Monteiro has published a project on Open Electronics detailing how he reverse-engineered the detachable LCD screen of a Sony CDX-A250 car radio from the 2000s. The display is controlled by a SANYO LC75826W driver, labeled IC901 in Sony’s service manual. This chip uses a proprietary serial protocol called CCB (Custom Communication Bus), sending data over three wires: data, clock, and chip select.

The LCD is powered by an external bench supply (+B), with ground shared with the Arduino. The CCB protocol requires a device address (41H) followed by four 72-bit data groups, each covering specific segments (D1 to D208). The first group also includes control bits for bias, segment count, frame frequency, oscillator mode, and display on/off. Since the datasheet does not document which visible symbol corresponds to which bit position, Monteiro used a searchOfSegments() routine to cycle through test states via a button connected to D2, printing results in the serial monitor.

The technical notes, available on GitHub, may apply to other displays using the same driver. The project is fully documented on Open Electronics.
