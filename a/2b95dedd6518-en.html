3D-Printed Einstein-Szilard Refrigerator Cools with Fire

A transparent Einstein-Szilard absorption refrigerator, built with a 3D printer, cools using a flame as its energy source.

Absorption refrigerators, while less efficient than compressor-based models, remain useful for their ability to run on waste heat alone. Their operation relies on fluids like ammonia and water, but their inner workings can be hard to grasp. To clarify this process, [Ben Krasnow] built a transparent Einstein-Szilard refrigerator using a 3D-printed clear resin body.

Unlike traditional designs, this refrigerator uses a third liquid—nonflammable perfluoropentane—as the refrigerant, avoiding risks from an open flame. The system separates an ammonia-water solution through boiling, then ammonia gas lowers the refrigerant’s partial pressure, causing it to evaporate. The vapors mix with water, where ammonia dissolves, leaving the refrigerant to condense and restart the cycle. A copper block transfers heat from the flame to the boiler chamber, though ammonia eventually corrodes copper over time.

The prototype reached 5 °C below ambient temperature, but its operation is finicky. A misplaced bubble can airlock the system since pressures equalize quickly. Despite challenges, the experiment shows that drinks can be cooled by burning a portion of their alcohol—a clever trick for off-grid scenarios.
