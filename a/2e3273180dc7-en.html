TwinX: Two Modular Robots That Transform On Demand

Japanese researchers develop TwinX, a system where two robots connect or separate via a retractable wire to change shape and function on the fly.

The TwinX system, developed by the University of Tokyo, consists of two identical robots called WiXus, each equipped with articulated limbs ending in wheels. These robots can connect or cooperate at a distance using a 2 mm Dyneema wire, controlled by a motorized mechanism capable of generating up to 313 newtons of tension.

Unlike traditional modular robots that lock rigidly, TwinX uses the wire for two purposes: pulling the robots together and transmitting forces between them. When retracted, the wire draws the robots close until four docking pins engage corresponding holes, ensuring a stable mechanical connection. The system offers three main configurations: a quadruped for obstacle navigation, an omnidirectional traction vehicle for sideways movement, and a mobile manipulator where one robot stacks on top of the other to form a platform with two articulated arms.

Demonstrations highlight TwinX in action: crossing grassy terrain, descending stairs, picking up objects, or collaboratively transporting items using the wire. Even when separated, the robots can cooperate by using wire tension to move loads or assist each other on slopes. Researchers note the system remains experimental, with challenges ahead, including automating cooperation between distant robots and scaling up to larger groups with multiple connecting wires.
