A Desktop OS for the ESP32-P4: A 1990s Throwback

A minimalist operating system inspired by DOS interfaces has been developed for the ESP32-P4, a powerful and affordable RISC-V processor.

The ESP32-P4, Espressif’s high-end processor featuring two fast RISC-V cores at a surprisingly low price, now runs BMDS (Bare Metal Do Stuff), a new operating system created by Jthwho. Built on the FreeRTOS kernel, widely used in embedded projects, BMDS offers a GUI reminiscent of 1990s DOS interfaces.

The OS supports ELF binaries and includes partial POSIX compliance, with a port of BusyBox for additional functionality. Its minimalist, single-user design delivers a clean, functional desktop experience without unnecessary overhead. The project aligns with a growing trend of powerful, hacker-friendly machines, where the ESP32-P4 is carving out a niche for creative and technical applications.

The article highlights the retro appeal of BMDS, comparing it to systems like Amiga, 68k Mac OS, or Windows 3.x, while questioning its relevance in 2026. Despite this uncertainty, the project is praised as an impressive technical achievement with room for future development.
