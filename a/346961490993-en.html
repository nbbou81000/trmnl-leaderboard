frank-dino: RP2350B Emulating a Z80 and Its Clones

An RP2350B microcontroller emulates a Zilog Z80 and its variants, with extended hardware support.

Mikhail Matveev, also known as rh1tech, has released frank-dino v1.00 on GitHub, a firmware designed for the dino_z80 board. This project uses an RP2350B microcontroller to emulate a Zilog Z84C00 CPU while handling additional functions such as memory, video, sound, and storage. The real Z80 remains dedicated to computations, while the RP2350B manages the rest of the system.

The firmware supports a wide range of Z80-compatible machines, including classic ZX Spectrum models (16K, 48K, 128K, +2, +2A, +3) and nearly thirty variants like the Pentagon 128/512/1024SL, Scorpion ZS-256, and Timex TC2048. It also includes support for hardware expansions such as the Beta Disk (four drives), DivMMC/esxDOS, Z-Controller, and IDE interfaces. Peripherals like USB keyboards, mice, gamepads, and HDMI displays are supported, along with advanced video modes such as ULA+ or Gigascreen.

Program loading works in real time or instantly via a real cassette connected to port J8. Snapshots, disk images, and ROMs are stored on a FAT32 microSD card. The firmware is released under the GPL-3.0-or-later license. Flashing requires no debug probe: simply hold button S3, press S4, then copy the .uf2 file to the drive that appears. The project is open source and available on GitHub.
