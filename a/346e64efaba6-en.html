An ESP32 Revives an Old AM Radio with a DIY Transmitter

An ESP32-S3 microcontroller broadcasts an online radio stream as AM to power a 1930s tube radio.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

As AM broadcast bands fade away, many vintage tube radios risk becoming obsolete. To keep these devices functional, one maker built a tiny AM transmitter using an ESP32-S3 microcontroller. The system streams an online radio feed and converts it into an AM signal via pulse-width modulation on a GPIO pin.

The tricky part was transmitting the signal effectively. A 3D-printed enclosure holds a loosely wound wire coil, which acts as an antenna strong enough for the vintage radio to pick up the transmission. The creator notes that while the setup may not comply with regulations, its low power output makes it unlikely to cause interference.

The current configuration targets the European longwave band at 200 kHz, but the maker suggests it could be adjusted for mediumwave frequencies. This project highlights a clever way to repurpose old electronics using open-source tools and DIY techniques, though it remains a hobbyist experiment rather than a commercial solution.
