Arduino Unveils DIY Synth Kit to Build Your Own Musical Instrument

A complete kit to build a polyphonic synthesizer and drum machine with gestural controls, no coding experience required.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Arduino has launched the UNO Q DIY synth kit, a complete solution for building electronic musical instruments without prior coding experience. The bundle includes a UNO Q 4GB board, four Modulino modules (Buttons, Movement, Knob, and Distance), Qwiic cables, and a step-by-step guide on the Arduino Project Hub. The goal is to enable anyone to assemble a polyphonic synthesizer, drum machine, or gestural controller with minimal effort, without sourcing individual components.

The kit leverages the UNO Q’s dual architecture, combining an STM32 microcontroller with an embedded Linux system. Modulino modules connect directly to the board via Qwiic connectors, simplifying wiring. The preconfigured software, based on Pure Data, maps sensor inputs to sound parameters in real time: tilting the Movement module changes pitch, turning the Knob module adjusts settings, pressing Buttons triggers drum hits, and the Distance module affects the audio filter. A detailed tutorial guides users through assembly and sensor integration, with 3D-printable enclosure files available on Printables.

Designed for beginners, the kit requires no programming knowledge. Qwiic connectors ensure secure, mistake-proof connections. Once assembled, users can expand the instrument’s capabilities by integrating visual control (via Processing or TouchDesigner), RFID scene switching, or generative sequencing. The Modulino ecosystem supports additional sensors (temperature, LEDs, etc.) for further customization.

The kit is available at a 35% discount on the Arduino Store until November 30, 2026. If out of stock, it can be found on Amazon, provided the product is verified as an original Arduino item.
