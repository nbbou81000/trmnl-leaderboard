A Platinum-Titanium Cathode Display for Retro Computing

A prototype five-segment display uses platinum and titanium cathodes in a vacuum chamber for a retro-style glow.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Segmented displays like Nixie tubes or Panaplex panels were once staples of digital readouts before LEDs took over. [Witchcraft and Alchemy] has revived this retro approach with a prototype five-segment display, but replacing neon with argon gas. The anode is made from an indium-tin oxide layer on a glass substrate, while the cathode is sputtered in two stages: first titanium, then platinum. This platinum-titanium combo prevents the platinum from flaking off, a common issue caused by mismatched molecular structures between glass and platinum.

The initial prototype, still basic, uses a 74141 Nixie driver IC to power the segments, producing a distinctive purple glow inside a vacuum chamber. A follow-up self-contained prototype was built but failed shortly after, likely due to sealing issues. Future iterations will ditch adhesive methods (tape or epoxy) in favor of a gasket, while conductive vias will help maintain a stable vacuum. These changes should also allow adding two more segments, turning the display into a seven-segment format. Additionally, a Penning mixture (argon and krypton) will replace pure argon to enhance performance.
