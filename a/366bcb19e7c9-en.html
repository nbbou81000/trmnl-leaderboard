A Hidden Magnetic Gesture Lock for an Invisible Safe

A wooden box locked by an invisible magnetic pattern, with no visible key or button.

Stephen Cass has built a wooden box that unlocks only when a specific magnetic pattern is traced across its surface, with no visible keyhole or keypad. Unlike traditional magnetic locks, which activate as soon as a magnet is detected, this system requires a precise, memorized gesture. To achieve this, Cass relies on a magnetic imaging tile (an 8×8 array of Hall-effect sensors) that tracks the position and polarity of a magnet in real time. Connected to a Teensy 4.1 microcontroller, the tile can detect gestures at high frequencies, accurately distinguishing between complex patterns.

The box features a servo-operated latch that secures the lid until the correct gesture is performed. A button allows users to record new gestures, while an LED indicates the system’s status. Power is supplied by a battery, but Cass points out a critical flaw: a dead battery would permanently lock the box. A potential fix involves adding a hidden inductive charging coil to prevent this issue.

To conceal the sensor location, Cass engraved a Celtic knotwork pattern on the wood. This design doubles as a subtle reference grid, guiding the magnetic stylus without revealing the sensitive area. While the lockbox works as intended, it remains experimental and requires further refinements, particularly for battery failure scenarios.
