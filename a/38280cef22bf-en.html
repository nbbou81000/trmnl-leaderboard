NXP Unveils Bluetooth 6.x MCUs for IoT and Automotive with Secure Localization

NXP expands its MCX W70 and KW43 families with Bluetooth 6.x Channel Sounding for accurate and secure distance measurements in IoT and automotive applications.

NXP has launched two new wireless microcontroller families, the MCX W70 and KW43, both supporting Bluetooth 6.x Channel Sounding. This feature uses phase-based ranging (PBR) and round-trip time (RTT) to deliver precise and secure distance measurements for industrial IoT and automotive applications.

The MCX W70 targets smart home and industrial IoT devices, while the KW43 is designed for automotive use cases such as digital car keys and secure vehicle access. Both families feature a 96 MHz Arm Cortex-M33 application core, a dedicated radio subsystem with a second Cortex-M33 core, up to 1.5 MB of flash, and 256 KB of SRAM. A dedicated Localization Compute Engine (LCE) accelerates localization calculations, while EdgeLock security features (secure boot, cryptography, true random number generation) protect sensitive data.

The MCX W70 adds support for Zigbee, Thread, Matter, and IEEE 802.15.4 networks, with a claimed localization accuracy of 20–50 cm. The KW43 includes CAN and LIN interfaces for automotive applications and supports up to 10 simultaneous Bluetooth LE connections.

Development boards like the FRDM-MCXW70 and FRDM-KW43, along with evaluation kits MCXW70-LOC and KW43-LOC, are available to streamline prototyping. These boards include debug probes, sensors, Arduino-compatible headers, and CAN/LIN transceivers. Software development is supported through the MCUXpresso environment, which provides wireless protocol stacks and code examples.

The MCX W70 and KW43 are currently in pre-production and sampling phases, with mass production expected in Q2 2027. Official pricing has not been announced, but a KW43 variant is listed at $4.52 on Digi-Key for orders of 2,000 units.
