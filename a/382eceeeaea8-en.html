DIY Autonomous Boat Maps Lakebeds with Open-Source Tech

A maker built a 3D-printed autonomous boat using ArduPilot and sonar to map lakebeds autonomously for under $725.

Mapping the bottom of a lake is challenging, especially in murky water or over large areas. Instead of relying on expensive commercial equipment, one maker spent two years developing an autonomous robotic boat to survey lakebeds. The project features a custom 3D-printed hull designed for modularity, allowing easy modifications without rebuilding the entire vessel. The hull also includes foam compartments for added structural rigidity and emergency flotation in case of water ingress.

Propulsion and Autonomous Navigation

The boat is powered by electric motors driving propellers, controlled by electronic speed controllers. Steering is handled via differential thrust, similar to a drone. Navigation relies on ArduPilot, an open-source autopilot platform, paired with a GPS/GNSS receiver. Users can program a series of waypoints, and the boat follows the route autonomously without manual control.

Data Collection and Results

Bathymetric data is gathered using a sonar depth sounder, which measures the distance to the lakebed. Each reading is recorded with GPS coordinates, creating a set of georeferenced depth measurements. This data is processed to generate a 3D representation of the underwater terrain. Field tests in canals and reservoirs, including Llyn Celyn in North Wales, revealed calibration and precision issues, which were addressed through iterative adjustments.

The total cost of the project is around $725 in parts, significantly less than commercial alternatives. The maker was able to customize the system to meet specific needs.
