An IP Packet Transmitted by RFC 1149 Pigeon Goes Up for Auction

A historic IP packet, sent via pigeon as per the RFC 1149 April Fool’s joke, is now up for auction at Christie’s.

In 1990, David Waitzman published an April Fool’s RFC titled RFC 1149, jokingly proposing a protocol to transmit IP datagrams via homing pigeons. The document described these "avian carriers" as offering high latency, low throughput, and low altitude service, with a variable MTU (Maximum Transmission Unit) often around 256 milligrams. The datagram was printed in hexadecimal on a scroll wrapped around the bird’s leg and secured with duct tape.

The joke became reality in 2001 when the Bergen Linux User Group printed nine IP datagrams on slips of paper and attached them to racing pigeons. After a three-mile flight, four replies were received from a second loft, proving the concept. One of these packets, framed and signed by Waitzman, is now up for auction at Christie’s. The lot includes a 41x210 mm scroll with creases from being attached to the pigeon’s leg, along with an inscription on the back of the frame confirming its origin.

As of now, the bidding has reached $2,600 with eight days left before the auction closes. This packet stands as a playful yet tangible reminder of how technical humor can turn into real-world tinkering.
