Heathkit H8 Mini: A Retro DIY Microcomputer Replica

A palm-sized DIY kit inspired by the 1977 Heathkit H8, built with vintage-style components.

In 1977, the Heathkit H8 was a landmark DIY microcomputer kit with through-hole components to solder. To celebrate its 50th anniversary, Lee Hart and the Society of Eight-Bit Heath Computerists have unveiled the H8 Mini, a palm-sized functional replica built with period-appropriate technology.

Vintage components, modern size

The H8 Mini retains the original’s architecture but with subtle upgrades. Its core is an 80C85 processor running at 2 MHz, paired with 32 KB of RAM and a 32 KB EPROM. An optional flash ROM can expand storage to 512 KB. The six-by-three-inch board features nine 0.3-inch seven-segment displays and a 4x4 keypad, mirroring the original. Power comes from a USB-to-serial adapter or three AAA batteries.

The I/O stays true to the retro spirit: serial communication uses the 80C85’s SID and SOD pins without a dedicated UART. An 82C55A chip handles the displays, keypad, speaker, and other onboard I/O. LEDs are multiplexed like the original H8, and a piezoelectric speaker adds basic sound capabilities.

Firmware in progress, but already usable

The current firmware runs a modified monitor combining elements of Heath’s PAM-8 and GW-MON-80. Programs load via a 2400-baud serial connection, with demos including LED patterns, musical scales, and a startup message. Future plans include support for patched versions of PAM-8 and Benton Harbor BASIC, while the flash ROM could serve as a disk to run CP/M or HDOS. An optional 3D-printed enclosure, complete with custom keycaps, is available for a more polished look.
