A WiFi 7 Router Turned into a Software-Defined Radio Receiver

The MediaTek MT7996 chip in a Gemtek W1700K WiFi 7 router enables SDR reception across 2.4, 5, and 6 GHz bands via its SoC.

The Gemtek W1700K WiFi 7 router, powered by the MediaTek MT7996 chipset, has been repurposed as a software-defined radio (SDR) receiver using open-source code. The project by h0m3us3r configures the router’s SoC to extract raw I/Q data streams from radio signals and stream them over Ethernet to a Linux machine. This approach bypasses the need for dedicated SDR hardware like the RTL-SDR, which is often restricted to lower frequency bands.

The MT7996 chip supports 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz bands, depending on the router version. However, it does not cover frequencies below 2.4 GHz, limiting its use for certain radio protocols. Enthusiasts have already expressed interest in capturing signals around 2 400–2 412 MHz, or even as low as 2 300 MHz. A hardware modification, such as a frequency up-converter, could expand its capabilities.

This project aligns with a growing trend where radio chips embedded in everyday devices reveal unexpected functionalities. Developers hope that other WiFi or IoT chipsets will be similarly exploited, paving the way for new low-cost and accessible SDR solutions.
