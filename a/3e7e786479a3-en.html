3D-Printed Lab Instruments to Democratize Biological Research

A PhD student designs 3D-printable lab instruments to cut costs and delays, making biological research more accessible.

Daniel Nilsson, a PhD student at Umeå University in Sweden, has developed a method to produce low-cost biological research instruments using consumer-grade 3D printers. His work, part of his doctoral thesis, aims to make high-precision lab tools more accessible, particularly for research on microorganisms.

Traditional instruments are often expensive and difficult to manufacture, slowing progress and excluding some researchers. Nilsson’s solution offers customizable alternatives that can be printed in hours using standard 3D printers. These devices also incorporate open-source electronics to meet specific needs, such as detecting or manipulating bacteria with lasers, or studying biofilms — the slimy layers of bacteria that colonize surfaces.

The project includes a practical guide for assembling these tools, along with real-world examples of applications. The thesis, available as a PDF on the university’s website, will be publicly defended this Friday at 9 AM in the Biology Building. The goal is to change how labs design equipment by promoting a collaborative and adaptive approach.
