LTO-5: The Return of Magnetic Tapes for Low-Cost Storage

Despite their age, LTO-5 tapes remain an economical solution for storing data in a homelab, with costs far lower than hard drives.

In a context where hard drive prices remain high, LTO-5 (Linear Tape-Open) magnetic tapes are emerging as an economical alternative for homelabs. Though outdated in professional settings, these cassettes offer 1.5 TB of uncompressed storage per unit, roughly the size of a palm. Commercial listings often cite 3 TB, but this figure assumes a 2:1 compression ratio, rarely achieved with files like photos or videos.

LTO-5 drives come in SAS or fiber channel versions. The first uses a SCSI-like interface, while the second relies on an optical connection. Installation requires a PCIe adapter and careful attention to cabling: without a switch, a crossover cable is essential to connect TX and RX ports. This often-overlooked detail was highlighted by a user who shared their experience.

On the second-hand market, LTO-5 tapes trade around $2 per TB, compared to $16 per TB for new hard drives. A used drive costs a few hundred dollars, making the investment quickly worthwhile for large data volumes. However, their use requires software and hardware compatibility, as newer drives (LTO-9 or LTO-10) no longer read older generations. LTO-5 tapes remain readable up to LTO-7, but backward compatibility is shrinking over time: LTO-9 drives only read LTO-8 tapes, and LTO-10 drives only their own.
