Exoplanets: Videos Showing Their Orbits in Fast Motion

Animations based on years of observations reveal the slow movements of giant exoplanets orbiting their stars.

Exoplanets, planets located outside our solar system, were once considered impossible to detect directly. Today, thanks to years of observations and advanced techniques, it is possible to visualize their orbits around their host stars. Jason Wang, a researcher, has created several videos compiling images taken over periods of up to 17 years to reconstruct these trajectories.

The method used relies on motion interpolation, a technique commonly found in video editing and modern TVs. It estimates the position of an object between two images taken at different dates, based on Kepler’s laws that describe planetary orbits. Although observations are not continuous, as planets move slowly (their orbital periods often exceed several decades), this approach provides a realistic representation of their path.

The data comes from the Keck Observatory, which captured images over 12 years for some exoplanets. This work has contributed to scientific publications, offering an unprecedented glimpse into these distant star systems. While the idea of flying over these worlds from the bridge of a starship remains fictional, these animations are the closest approximation available today.
