Meeting Recorder: Transcribe Meetings Locally with Omarchy

An open-source tool to record and transcribe meetings locally, without cloud or specific hardware.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Meeting Recorder is a meeting recorder designed for Omarchy, a Linux distribution based on Arch and Hyprland. It captures the user's microphone and computer audio separately, then transcribes the entire meeting locally using the Whisper model (large-v3-turbo). No data is sent to the cloud, ensuring full privacy.

The tool uses Nemotron 3 Diarization, a local NVIDIA model, to identify up to eight distinct voices. Speakers are automatically labeled (e.g., "Remote 1", "Remote 2"), and their names can be customized. At the end of the call, a structured transcription is generated, with timestamps and speaker attribution. An integrated audio player allows easy navigation through the recording.

The software is available in Omarchy's package repository, currently only on the edge channel. A one-line command also enables installation on other systems, though some features (like automatic chaptering) are exclusive to Omarchy. For Windows or macOS users, alternatives like Meetily or Kassis provide similar solutions, but without native integration with a local agent.

Note: Version 1.1.1 has a bug on processors without AVX-512 (e.g., Ryzen 5 5500), preventing transcription. A fix has been proposed by a contributor, but manual compilation may be required in the meantime. The Whisper model, around 1.6 GB, is downloaded once. Meetings are saved in ~/Documents/Meetings, with audio and a modifiable transcript.md file.

The tool is licensed under MIT and its code is available on GitHub. Installation is straightforward, but participants must be informed before use, in compliance with France's Article 226-1 of the Penal Code.
