FilaLoom: Merging 3D Printing and Knitting with Thread

A hybrid tool integrates textile threads directly into 3D prints, creating novel hybrid structures.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

FilaLoom, a project by Joram de Haas, bridges the gap between 3D printing and knitting by embedding textile threads directly into printed structures. Unlike traditional 3D printing, which relies solely on layered plastic, this system uses a dedicated tool head to lay down thread, akin to a tool-changer on a multi-extruder 3D printer. The result merges the mechanical strength of plastic with the flexibility of textile, enabling hybrid structures with novel properties.

The method leverages two extruders simultaneously: one for plastic filament and another for thread. This automation allows for precise textile integration, far beyond manual insertion. Demonstrations include 3D-knitted patterns where thread forms web-like structures or textile reinforcements, expanding beyond typical point reinforcements.

While the project doesn’t target a specific use case, it highlights a growing trend in additive manufacturing: exploring hybrid techniques. For digital fabrication enthusiasts, FilaLoom offers a fresh approach to designing objects with combined properties, balancing rigidity and flexibility.
