End of Production for eZ80, Last Heir of the Z80

The eZ80, an iconic microcontroller, is being discontinued after 25 years. Final orders are accepted until December 2026.

Littelfuse, the owner of the Zilog brand, has announced the discontinuation of the eZ80 and other microcontrollers in its lineup via PCN ZAC26-0074. This component, the successor to the iconic Z80 processor, remained available even after the original chip and its peripherals were discontinued years ago.

Final orders can still be placed until December 21, 2026, with deliveries scheduled throughout 2027. For industrial customers, this marks the end of the road for circuits still using the eZ80 or similar models. The move aligns with Littelfuse’s strategy to focus on other areas of its business.

The Z80, introduced in 1974, left a lasting mark on computing by powering machines like the ZX Spectrum and the Game Boy. Its successor, the eZ80, expanded the 8-bit architecture to 24 bits while maintaining software compatibility. Though production is ending, alternatives already exist: open-source Z80-compatible cores are available, and projects like TinyTapeout enable the fabrication of custom silicon chips.
