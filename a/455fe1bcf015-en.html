3D Printing Meets Electronics: Embedding Components Without Extra Tools

An open-source method embeds electronic components during FDM 3D printing without printer modifications or custom tool heads.

Researchers led by Oliver Child have developed Print & Place, an open-source technique to embed electronic components directly into 3D-printed structures during FDM printing, without requiring manual assembly or printer modifications. Unlike traditional hybrid manufacturing methods that often need custom tool heads, this approach leverages the temporary adhesive properties of molten thermoplastic to pick up and place parts.

The process begins with a parametrically generated parts tray, printed alongside the main model. This tray holds components such as pre-populated PCBs, conductive wires, braided strips, or rivets before they are integrated. During printing, the printer’s firmware—via added G-code commands—moves the extruder over the required parts. A small amount of molten plastic is deposited on the component, which is then lifted and placed into position within the growing structure. Subsequent layers of filament cover and secure the component in place.

While the method successfully demonstrates embedding a variety of components, the authors note several challenges inherent to an open-loop system. Common issues include pick failures, misalignment, and inconsistent electrical contacts. They propose integrating a camera system, similar to industrial pick-and-place machines, to provide real-time feedback for adjustments and operator alerts. Currently a research prototype, Print & Place offers a promising path toward more automated hybrid manufacturing workflows.
