Winning Projects from Arduino UNO Q Competition: AI, Robotics and Home Automation

Fifteen winning projects leverage the UNO Q for edge AI, home automation, and robotics.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Invent the Future competition, organized by Arduino, Qualcomm Technologies, and Edge Impulse in partnership with Hackster, awarded 15 projects out of 1,202 submissions. All winning projects rely on the UNO Q board, a platform designed for edge AI and connectivity, and the Arduino App Lab, a streamlined development tool.

Category winners

Social Impact: KOCHONI Biaou David Barthélémy’s Smart Water Guardian AI uses sensors to monitor water quality in real time (temperature, pH, turbidity, dissolved solids). The goal is to detect anomalies indicating pollution, with potential lifesaving applications.

Home Automation: Coders Cafe’s Smart Coop 2.0 automates chicken coop maintenance using the UNO Q. The system dispenses food, heats the space, and integrates a local AI to detect eggs or predators (coyotes, foxes).

Gaming: Kevin Loeffler’s RCT IRL turns a theme park visit into a game experience. A portable device with a 9DoF IMU (inertial measurement unit) and a UNO Q records roller coaster movement data. Three Edge Impulse models analyze excitement, intensity, and nausea, generating a comparative report.

Industrial IoT: pasquale887’s HaloSense V1 explores neuroplasticity through a haptic feedback headset. Thirteen vibration motors convey environmental data captured by sensors connected to the UNO Q, allowing users to learn to interpret these patterns.

Robotics: Mohammed Akramuddin’s Curie is a desktop robot with an OLED screen displaying facial expressions. Designed to assist users, including those with ADHD, it manages tasks via APIs like Groq, breaks down complex projects, and alerts when distractions occur.

Best in Show: Jorge Eldis González’s Edge-AI Spectrophotometer combines a AS7343 spectrometer and embedded AI to analyze light and detect contaminants in samples. The system simplifies testing and expands spectrophotometry applications.
