Marelleo: An Open-Source Story Box for Kids Using RFID

A father built a story box for his kids, triggered by RFID figurines, with no screen or subscription.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Marelleo project was born from frustration with commercial story boxes like the Toniebox. While these devices are user-friendly, they rely on proprietary figurines or cards to access content, restricting user freedom. The creator aimed to build an open-source alternative without subscriptions or closed catalogs to share stories with his kids.

To bring this idea to life, he used an ESP32 microcontroller, capable of decoding MP3 audio files while remaining easy to program like an Arduino. A breadboard allowed quick prototyping without risking damage, a crucial advantage for an electronics beginner. After a few hours of work, a functional prototype was ready: a box that plays a story when an RFID tag is placed on top.

Technical challenges soon arose. A wiring mistake on a potentiometer (a volume control component) caused a short circuit and a burnt ski wax smell. Managing the lithium battery, including its charge controller, highlighted the need to secure connections to prevent fire hazards. The final version required switching to a perfboard to replace the breadboard, ensuring durability and resistance to children’s handling.

The 3D-printed enclosure had to be redesigned after tests with children aged 2 to 7. The volume knob, for example, was torn off by enthusiastic hands, prompting the creator to rethink the design to avoid fragile parts. The project demonstrates learning through trial and error, where each technical issue led to a more robust and user-friendly solution.
