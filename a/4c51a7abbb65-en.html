A Magnetic Lockbox That Opens with a Secret Gesture

A wooden lockbox unlocks only when a specific magnet gesture is traced over a sensor grid.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Stephen Cass has built a wooden lockbox that unlocks only when a specific gesture is traced with a magnet over a sensor grid. The system relies on SparkFun’s magnetic imaging tile, an 8-by-8 grid of Hall-effect sensors that detect both magnetic field strength and polarity. This small-run board, based on Peter Jansen’s work, communicates over SPI fast enough to track hand-moved magnets.

A Teensy 4.1 microcontroller runs the locking logic. Two software libraries were created: one to record a gesture to nonvolatile storage, and another to compare a new gesture against the stored one. A servo physically unlocks the box by rotating a beam out of a notch glued inside the lid. The 3 cm² sensing area made it difficult to repeat gestures from outside the box.

To solve this without exposing the mechanism, Cass added a Celtic knotwork pattern on the front, serving as a hidden reference grid. If the battery dies while locked, the box remains secured and requires physical force to open. The project is documented in an article on IEEE Spectrum and its code is available on GitHub.
