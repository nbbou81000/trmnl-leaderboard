Andromeda: Open-Source VR Headset Reaches First Working Prototype

A developer unveils Andromeda, an open-source VR headset built from scratch, featuring high-resolution LCD screens and integrated motion tracking.

Developer Mañolo has built Andromeda, an open-source VR headset that has reached its first functional prototype. This DIY project tackles multiple technical challenges: high-resolution displays, optics designed for a few centimeters from the eyes, low-latency motion tracking, and SteamVR-compatible software.

The headset features two LCD screens, each about 2 inches diagonally, with a resolution of 1600 × 1600 pixels per eye. For the optics, Mañolo designed a custom system using four Fresnel lenses. Two lenses are stacked in front of each display to create a pseudo-pancake assembly, optimized for a reduced field of view. The lenses, protected with Kapton tape, were trimmed using three 3D-printed jigs and adjusted to accommodate tiny cameras mounted inside the lens housings. These cameras, pointing toward the user’s eyes, will later enable integrated eye tracking.

The displays and their optics are mounted on 3D-printed carriages connected to the main chassis by smooth metal guide rods. This mechanism allows manual horizontal adjustment, paving the way for a future motorized interpupillary distance (IPD) adjustment system. Each screen connects to a central driver board via a 51-pin, 0.3 mm-pitch FFC cable, while video input comes from a PC through a Micro DisplayPort.

Motion tracking relies on a stereoscopic USB-C camera mounted at the front of the headset, providing synchronized images at up to 120 FPS for real-time inside-out SLAM positional tracking. A USB gyroscope adds rotational data, and an internal USB hub combines the IMU and two eye-tracking camera connections. The display driver board is powered by the 5 V supply from the stereo camera board, reducing the number of cables entering the headset.

The entirely 3D-printed chassis is attached to a halo-style headband, adjustable via a rack-and-pinion mechanism and a rear tightening knob. With custom drivers installed, SteamVR recognizes Andromeda as a valid VR headset, and the prototype demonstrates real-time 6-DoF tracking. While motorized IPD adjustment and eye-tracking features are still in development, the core display, optical, tracking, and software systems are already operational.
