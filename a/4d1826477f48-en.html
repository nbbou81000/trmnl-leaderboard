Wearable Scentronic Devices Could Beam Smells Over the Internet

Researchers are developing a system to transmit smells in real time via wearables, enhancing immersion in telepresence.

Researchers from the Ohio State University and the Zhejiang Laboratory have developed Scentronic, a technology designed to transmit smells over the Internet of Things (IoT) to enhance telepresence and teleoperation experiences. Currently limited to sight, sound, and limited touch, these systems could soon include the sense of smell.

The system combines an electronic nose sensor (e-nose) at the remote location with a wearable scent emitter worn by the user. When the e-nose detects a specific scent, the wearable releases the same odor in real time. In tests, the system successfully transmitted five distinct scents—coffee, beer, red wine, orange, and chocolate cake—with a recognition rate of about 73%. This rate remains close to 71% when two scents are emitted simultaneously.

Beyond entertainment, the researchers highlight potential medical and accessibility applications. Prototypes like a wristband or necklace could serve as an olfactory aid for people with olfactory disorders, similar to hearing aids for hearing loss. Jinghua Li, co-author of the study, emphasizes the importance of these technologies in unraveling and reproducing olfactory mechanisms, particularly in neurological diseases.

The project, published in the open-access journal Science Advances, uses Espressif ESP32 microcontrollers to control both the e-nose and the wearable emitters. Early results are promising, suggesting that digitized smells could soon reach consumer devices.
