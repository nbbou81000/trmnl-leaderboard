Hacked Nixie Clock Displays Bus Wait Times

An enthusiast repurposes a Nixie clock to display bus wait times by hacking its firmware and linking it to a Raspberry Pi Zero.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Nixie clocks, with their vintage cathode-ray tubes and warm glow, have become a popular retro aesthetic choice. While many commercial models exist—often as bare PCBs—most are limited to displaying time. [Justin Scott] took this a step further by repurposing a two-digit Nixie clock board to show how many minutes were left before his next bus arrived.

To achieve this, he first modified the board’s firmware using an ST-LINK V2 programmer and traced the pins of the STM8 microcontroller to understand how they controlled the Nixie tubes. After identifying unused I/O pins, he set up a serial link to a Raspberry Pi Zero via a level converter to handle the data processing. A Python script on the Pi queries the local bus API, retrieves real-time schedules, and calculates the remaining wait time.

The Nixie display then shows this wait time in minutes, while the decimal points blink to indicate background activity. Though most commercial Nixie boards lack native I2C support, this project proves they can be hacked for more dynamic uses. The author highlights how this approach combines retro charm with practical functionality.
