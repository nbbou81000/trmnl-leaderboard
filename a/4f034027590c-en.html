Prusa Core One+ INDX: A Troubled Multi-Extruder Upgrade?

The Prusa Core One+ INDX promises multi-material printing with minimal waste, but calibration and filament compatibility issues mar the experience.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Prusa Core One+ INDX is marketed as an advanced upgrade for the Prusa Core One+ FDM printer, featuring eight independent extruders (INDX). This setup aims to minimize purge waste during material or color changes, with a claimed target of just 0.013 grams per transition. Each extruder is equipped with dual Bondtech gears, designed to enhance extrusion precision.

However, tests conducted by [Thomas Sanladerer] highlight significant issues. The mandatory automatic calibration sequence at print startup is exclusively compatible with Prusa’s own filaments. This restriction prevents the use of third-party filaments, such as Voxel PLA, without manual g-code adjustments. Even after calibration, settings are not saved, forcing users to repeat the process for every print. Users also report failed prints with Prusa filaments, indicating broader compatibility problems.

Visual access to the print chamber is severely obstructed by the extruder assembly, making it impossible to manually check filament loading or monitor the printing process. These limitations make the INDX less practical compared to alternatives like the Bambu Lab H2C or Snapmaker U1, or even Prusa’s own XL toolchanger models, which performed more reliably in comparative tests.

User feedback echoes these challenges. Some note that third-party filament compatibility is achievable via manual settings in Prusa Slicer or the printer’s interface, but these workarounds bypass persistent warnings. Others share similar struggles with Prusa’s multi-material systems, describing disappointing results despite the company’s technical support.
