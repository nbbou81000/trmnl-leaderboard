Iomega Zip Drive: High-Capacity Floppies Before USB Sticks

Before USB sticks, Iomega’s Zip drive offered 100 MB of portable storage with a distinctive design and massive marketing.

In the 1990s, before USB sticks and broadband, Iomega revolutionized portable storage with its Zip drive, a 100 MB high-capacity floppy disk system. Founded in 1980 in Utah, the company initially sold the Bernoulli Box, an expensive professional solution, but new CEO Kim Edwards shifted focus to consumers in 1994. The Zip drive was born from a clear demand: simple, affordable, and fast storage.

Released in 1995 at $199 for the drive and $19.95 per disk, the Zip stood out with its indigo design, rare in an era dominated by beige. It connected via a PC’s parallel port, with a printer pass-through, and featured a window to view the handwritten label on the inserted disk. Aggressive marketing—including TV, radio, and mainstream magazine ads—drove sales from $326 million in 1995 to $1.2 billion in 1996.

Its success was marred by the “click of death”, a recurring noise indicating a disk could not be read, leading to a class action and partial refunds. Despite this, sales peaked in 1999 with 9.6 million units sold. Production ended in 2004 after 50 million drives and 310 million disks were sold.
