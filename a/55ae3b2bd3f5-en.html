DSPi Turns Raspberry Pi Pico into USB Sound Card with Built-in DSP Engine

An open-source firmware turns Raspberry Pi Pico or Pico 2 into a USB sound card with advanced audio processing.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The DSPi project, developed by Weeb Labs and released on GitHub, turns a Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) or Pico 2 (RP2350) into a USB sound card with an integrated digital signal processor (DSP). The firmware handles advanced audio features such as room correction, active crossover filters, parametric equalization, time alignment, loudness compensation, and headphone crossfeed. Both microcontrollers run at a fixed 307.2 MHz system clock, optimized for integer sample rates (44.1 kHz, 48 kHz, and 96 kHz).

The RP2040 requires a small voltage increase to reach this frequency, while the RP2350, thanks to its hardware floating-point unit, offers more headroom and supports up to 11 channels and 9 outputs, compared to 7 and 5 for the RP2040. Each output can dynamically switch between S/PDIF (digital audio) and I2S (serial audio interface) protocols, and a single device can mix both. Output pins are reassignable on the fly without reflashing, and assignments are stored in flash with up to 10 presets available.

A delta-sigma modulator running on the second core of the microcontroller drives a PDM subwoofer output through a simple RC low-pass filter. The firmware is released under the GPL v3 license and builds with the standard Pico C/C++ toolchain.
