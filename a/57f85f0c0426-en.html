A DIY Camera Built on Raspberry Pi 5 with Open-Source Software

A Reddit user builds a fully customizable digital camera around a Raspberry Pi 5, featuring a 3D-printed case and dedicated OS.

The R1 Mini project repurposes a Raspberry Pi 5 into a fully functional digital camera, addressing the constraints of closed commercial models. The 3D-printed enclosure, measuring 100x100x100 mm, houses an Arducam IMX477 HQ camera module with a C/CS lens mount for flexible optics. Power is supplied by a 10,000 mAh Mophie Powerstation battery, while a 4-inch Waveshare display (720x720) serves as the user interface for previews and settings.

Physical controls include dedicated buttons for shutter speed, ISO, and white balance adjustments, along with a sleep or safe shutdown button. An MPU6050 accelerometer enables tilt detection, and a USB 3.0 port handles video storage. Battery life reaches five to six hours during recording or eight to nine hours in idle mode. Active cooling maintains operating temperatures between 50 and 55 °C, preventing deformation of 3D-printed parts.

The camera runs ApertarOS, an open-source operating system supporting multiple sensors, physical controls, Wi-Fi management, and a web interface. Custom configurations include button mappings, startup graphics, and camera identification in image metadata. The sensor supports video resolutions up to 2028x1520 at 30 FPS (60 FPS at lower resolutions) and still images in RAW DNG, PNG, TIFF, or JPEG formats. Videos are recorded as CinemaDNG image sequences, compatible with software like DaVinci Resolve. A vibration motor salvaged from an Xbox controller provides haptic feedback, disabled during recording to avoid interference.

The project is still a work in progress but fully functional. The STEP design files for the enclosure are shared upon request, offering a foundation for makers to replicate or adapt the device.
