A Retro Bluetooth Phone Built with ESP32 to Escape Your Smartphone

A maker builds a retro Bluetooth handset to reduce smartphone use, without a cellular modem but with an 80s-inspired design.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

To escape the grip of his smartphone, Kevin Noki designed a retro Bluetooth handset inspired by 1980s phones. Unlike a traditional phone, this prototype lacks a cellular modem, as adding a VoLTE module is often impossible without regulatory approval.

The device initially used an ESP32 microcontroller for its simplicity and Bluetooth compatibility. However, poor audio quality led to switching to a dedicated Hands-Free Profile (HFP) Bluetooth module, purpose-built for voice calls. The case design, crafted from XPS foam cut with a hot wire, allowed quick iterations before finalizing a 3D-printed version. A black acrylic sheet, bent using a jig, covers the display for an authentic retro look.

The project is available as open source on the author’s website, with detailed instructions for replication. The STL file for the 80s-style phone case is also provided, though a 3D-printed version isn’t mandatory to build the handset. The goal remains to reduce reliance on a primary smartphone without fully abandoning communication.
