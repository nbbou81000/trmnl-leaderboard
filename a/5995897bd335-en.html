TWEACMAN: A Tactile WAV Player Inspired by Reel-to-Reel Decks

An open-source project recreates the manual control of reel-to-reel decks to manipulate digital audio files.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

To recreate the tactile feel of reel-to-reel tape decks, maker Mykle built TWEACMAN, a portable WAV player featuring two 360° rotary controls inspired by TEAC decks and Walkman players. Unlike standard rotary encoders, these components, Taiwan Alpha RV112-FF potentiometers, include two 90°-offset potentiometers each. By reading their analog outputs and applying trigonometry, the system calculates the shaft’s absolute angular position with high precision, enabling smooth and continuous audio scrubbing at speeds ranging from 0.001x to 1000x, in both directions.

The project’s core is a SparkFun Thing Plus RA6M5 board, powered by Renesas’ RA6M5 microcontroller. It provides six 12-bit ADC channels—critical for reading the two potentiometers (each requiring two channels)—and includes output amplifiers to generate stereo audio without an external DAC. Additional features like a floating-point unit, capacitive touch sensing, and a DA14531MOD Bluetooth Low Energy module enhance functionality. Power comes from a rechargeable 3.7 V lithium-polymer battery.

The open-source Arduino libraries developed for this project handle potentiometer interfacing, calibration, and multi-rotation tracking. Mykle also demonstrated their versatility with an example controlling audio oscillators. Beyond digital audio playback, TWEACMAN showcases how affordable, unconventional components can create intuitive interfaces for digital devices.
