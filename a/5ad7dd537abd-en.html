Arduino Adapter Lets You Use Sega Genesis Controllers on Philips CD-i

An Arduino-based adapter enables Sega Genesis controllers to work with the obscure Philips CD-i console.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Philips CD-i, released in 1991, remains a niche and poorly supported system, especially regarding its controllers. MattKC developed an adapter using an Arduino Pro Micro to bypass the original controllers’ limitations, which are often rare and use proprietary connectors. The solution leverages Sega Genesis controllers, which feature widely available DB-9 connectors and a wiring scheme compatible with Atari joystick ports.

The technical hurdle lies in the protocol mismatch: Genesis controllers send simple parallel signals, while the CD-i expects a serial data stream, similar to mouse inputs. The Arduino acts as a translator, continuously polling the Genesis controller to read button states and then generating the serial commands the CD-i requires. This adaptation demands a deep understanding of the CD-i’s unconventional input system, designed more for mouse-like devices than traditional gamepads.

The project doesn’t transform the CD-i gaming experience, given the console’s limited library. However, it provides an affordable workaround for CD-i owners seeking more common controllers. BlueRetro, another open-source project, offers a similar alternative for other retro consoles.
