Zigbee: Why a Mesh Network Outperforms Wi-Fi for Smart Home Reliability

A journalist explains why he chose Zigbee for his smart home setup, despite the higher upfront cost, and how this protocol outperforms Wi-Fi in reliability and scalability.

After two years of building his smart home, a technology journalist made the deliberate choice to base his entire setup on Zigbee, despite its higher cost compared to Wi-Fi. His key argument: the creation of an independent mesh network that avoids overloading the home Wi-Fi and simplifies automation.

Unlike Wi-Fi devices, which all rely on a central router, Zigbee-compatible gadgets communicate through a dedicated coordinator. Permanently powered devices—such as Philips Hue bulbs—also act as repeaters, extending the network’s range without additional hardware. This results in near-unlimited coverage, even in large homes, without sacrificing responsiveness. The journalist highlights his Hue bulbs installed at the far end of his house, whose commands travel through the mesh rather than directly to the Hue Bridge.

Energy efficiency is another major advantage. Zigbee sensors (motion, doors, etc.) run on batteries for years because they only transmit data when an event occurs. In contrast, Wi-Fi sensors—often limited to a few days of battery life—must constantly poll the network to stay active. A real-world example: a Zigbee remote powered by a CR2032 coin cell lasts several years without replacement.

Seamless integration with Home Assistant

The Zigbee protocol integrates smoothly with Home Assistant, an open-source home automation platform. Through the ZHA (Zigbee Home Automation) integration, all Zigbee devices in the home become controllable and automatable entities from a single interface. The journalist even tested third-party coordinators (such as the Sonoff Zigbee 3.0 USB Dongle Plus) before settling on the Hue Bridge, without losing compatibility with his relays and sensors. The Matter standard also allows these devices to be exposed to other ecosystems, offering full interoperability.

The journalist acknowledges that Wi-Fi remains suitable for one-off automations or isolated devices (like a Govee LED strip in his bathroom). But for a comprehensive, robust, and scalable smart home, Zigbee stands out as the superior choice—combining reliability, low power consumption, and brand-agnostic flexibility.
