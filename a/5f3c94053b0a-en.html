SPIDriver Quattro: A USB-to-SPI Debugging Tool with Live Monitoring

A new USB-to-SPI debugging tool featuring a color display and power monitoring for real-time transaction analysis.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Excamera Labs, led by James Bowman, is unveiling the SPIDriver Quattro, a USB-to-SPI bridge designed to streamline the development and debugging of SPI-based projects. Unlike traditional tools, this device features a built-in color IPS display that shows live SPI traffic, providing immediate visibility into data exchanges.

The gadget connects without drivers on Windows, macOS, and Linux, appearing as a standard serial device. It supports three operation modes: driver (USB-to-SPI bridge for controlling devices), passive monitor (non-intrusive bus listening), and capture (logging transactions for later analysis). A current-sensing feature, accurate to 0.1 mA, helps diagnose abnormal power consumption during debugging.

The SPIDriver Quattro handles standard SPI, dual-SPI, and quad-SPI modes, making it suitable for flash memories, displays, or analog-to-digital converters. Its integrated screen allows real-time tracking of device responses and current spikes, a valuable asset during development phases. Interactive apps and software bindings simplify integration into automated scripts.

Two configurations will be available: a base model with a color-coded cable set, and an Expert Pack bundle including a dual-flash ZIF-socket adapter with in-circuit clip connectors, a 64Mb QSPI flash chip, and a 160×128 SPI LCD panel. Hardware and software design files will be released under the BSD 3-clause license upon shipment. A crowdfunding campaign is planned soon via Crowd Supply.
