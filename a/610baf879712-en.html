Comparing Three Edge AI Solutions for a Robotic Lawn Mower

A hands-on test evaluates hardware requirements for basic embedded AI on an autonomous lawn mower.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Autonomous lawn mowers often rely on expensive RTK GPS for centimeter-level navigation, but a camera-based local AI system could offer a cheaper alternative. GreatScott tested three edge AI platforms to determine how much hardware is truly needed for a simple vision task: distinguishing grass from obstacles like gravel or wooden walkways.

Three configurations with varying performance

The most affordable option is the Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32S3 Sense, priced around $20. It features an ESP32-S3 microcontroller (dual-core at 240 MHz, 8 MB PSRAM, 8 MB flash), an OV2640 camera, a microphone, and a microSD card slot. To fit within its limited resources, AI models were quantized and converted into lightweight TensorFlow Lite Micro formats, enabling basic image classification and object detection.

The mid-range solution, the Sipeed MaixCAM (about $80), runs Linux and includes a dedicated neural network accelerator rated at 1 TOPS. It also comes with a MIPI CSI camera and a small touchscreen, providing more power and flexibility for complex models.

At the high end, the Raspberry Pi 5 paired with the Raspberry Pi AI Kit (around $200) delivers top-tier performance. Its Hailo-8L accelerator offers up to 13 TOPS, enabling low-latency processing at over 30 frames per second.

The experiment revealed that while the Raspberry Pi 5 excels in raw performance, the ESP32-S3 is sufficient for a simple task like grass detection. The findings suggest that hardware selection should prioritize real-world application needs over theoretical specs like TOPS.
