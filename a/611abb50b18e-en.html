PhoneBot: A Humanoid Robot Powered by an Old Android Phone

An open-source bipedal robot uses an Android phone as its brain, for under $400 excluding the phone.

Researchers at UCLA have built PhoneBot, an open-source humanoid robot whose brain is a standard Android smartphone. The project proves that even an old, low-end phone can replace expensive robotic components. The robot stands about 48 cm tall, weighs 1.8 kg, and costs around $400 to assemble, excluding the phone.

Its lower body features 13 motorized joints powered by affordable DYNAMIXEL XL430-W250-T servos. Three joints per hip, one per knee, and two per ankle enable stable walking, while a thirteenth joint rotates the torso to orient the phone’s camera without moving the feet. The phone, mounted above the hips, doubles as the central unit and sensor: its IMU (inertial measurement unit) tracks movement, and its camera handles object detection and human tracking.

A custom Android app, based on TensorFlow Lite, runs the neural networks controlling locomotion. The robot communicates over Wi-Fi with an onboard microcomputer that relays commands to the servos. This wireless setup allows swapping phones without altering the robot’s electronics. Tests confirmed its operation with four phones, including a 2017 model and three budget devices under $80.

Walking required reinforcement learning training in simulation, tailored to the motors’ limitations. A sagittal mirroring technique corrected leg imbalances, reducing joint-angle asymmetry from 0.254 to 0.003 radians. The prototype has limitations: it only walks on flat ground, has low payload capacity, and conversational AI processing still relies on an external laptop. Future plans include adding lightweight arms, improving uneven terrain walking, and integrating AI directly on the smartphone.
