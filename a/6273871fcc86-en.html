A Portable DIY Vis Spectrometer Inspired by Newton

A researcher recreates Newton’s experiment with a portable, open-source visible spectrometer.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A science and electronics enthusiast, known as Curious Scientist, has built a portable visible spectrometer inspired by Isaac Newton’s light decomposition experiments. Unlike traditional spectrometers, this device uses an optical fiber to capture light, which is then aligned by a collimating mirror before reaching a diffraction grating. The grating splits the light into its color components, much like Newton’s prism did.

The resulting spectrum is focused by a second mirror onto a linear CCD sensor, a TCD1304, similar to those once used in digital cameras. Each bright spot on the sensor corresponds to a specific wavelength, enabling analysis of the light’s spectral composition. Initially, the signal was processed by a computer running custom software, but Curious Scientist simplified the system by replacing the computer with a STM32F401CCU6 development board.

A 4-inch display shows the results as a graph, while a rotary encoder allows for parameter adjustments and spectrum peak exploration. An 18650 lithium battery and charging module provide power, all housed in a 3D-printed enclosure. This portable spectrometer now enables spectroscopy anywhere, without relying on fixed equipment.
