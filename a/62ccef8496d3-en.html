Picotronix: Modular Oscilloscope with Two Raspberry Pi Pico 2 Boards

An open-source modular oscilloscope based on two Raspberry Pi Pico 2 boards, featuring a color display and expansion modules.

Picotronix, developed by Thort Werx Pty Ltd, is a modular oscilloscope, logic analyzer, and frequency meter built around two Raspberry Pi Pico 2 boards. One Pico 2 acts as a signal processor, while the other drives the display and handles wireless connectivity. The main module features a 320 × 240 color LCD and three expansion ports for plug-in picoPods modules.

Features and specifications

The Picotronix offers a two-channel digital storage oscilloscope, an eight-channel logic analyzer expandable to fourteen channels, and a frequency meter. The logic analyzer supports up to 150 MHz sampling. Analog inputs accept 3 Vpp or 16.6 Vpp signals at 1 MΩ, with BNC connectors for AC and DC modes. Timebases range from 5 ms to 10 µs per division, and the system captures using the RP2350’s 12-bit ADC. An auxiliary input can function as a logic probe or voltmeter.

Expansion modules include: Single-ended analog (500 mV to 5 V), differential (±25 V), Wave-05 (12-bit DAC with programmable offset), 12-bit Logic State Analyser (150 MSPS), MSO-12 (100 MSPS ADC and logic analyzer), Electronix Lab (educational module), and ADC-100 (dual 100 MSPS ADC).

The Picotronix is programmable in MicroPython, with direct access to the RP2350’s PIO state machines for optimal performance. A PC emulator will enable MicroPython script development outside the device. The project is partially open-source: part of the code will be released, but neither the hardware nor the full firmware is currently available.

Availability and pricing

The project launched on Kickstarter with a funding target of $10,000 AUD (about $7,000 USD). The main module is priced at $134 USD (including cables), with picoPods modules available as extras. Shipping costs $20–40 AUD within Australia and $21–42 USD internationally. Deliveries are expected to begin in March 2027.
