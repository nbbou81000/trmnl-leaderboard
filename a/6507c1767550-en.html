Modular 3D-Printed Train Whistle for Enthusiasts

An open-source 3D-printed train whistle project lets enthusiasts recreate historical sounds with an adjustable resonant chamber.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The open-source 3D-printed train whistle project, shared on Thingiverse, targets rail sound enthusiasts. Unlike modern horns that rely on vibrating diaphragms, this whistle uses a resonant chamber whose size dictates the pitch. Adjustable caps allow users to modify the chamber and replicate the tones of historic steam locomotive whistles.

Designed by [AeroKoi], the 3-inch model is more compact than previous iterations, and a spreadsheet helps fine-tune notes based on cap measurements. The accompanying video tutorial covers assembly, tuning, and the acoustic principles behind the design, including the challenges of mimicking brass whistle sounds with plastic and compressed air.

Practical use depends heavily on the air source: a small compressor only allows short, spaced-out blasts, while a larger tank is needed for extended sequences. The project also includes configurations to emulate specific whistles, such as the Santa Fe 5-chime or B&O 3-chime models.
