FLEX: Correcting Robots in Natural Language for Safer Tasks

An experimental system teaches robots hidden priorities through natural language corrections, without complex reprogramming.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Domestic robots struggle to interpret the intentions behind instructions. For example, asking a robot to bring coffee without specifying to avoid fragile objects can lead to accidents. Researchers from MIT and NYU have developed FLEX, a system that analyzes natural language corrections to adjust robot behavior.

FLEX relies on three operations: ADD (add a constraint), GENERALIZE (extend a rule to similar cases), and MASK (ignore irrelevant details). If a user says, "Don’t spill coffee on my laptop," FLEX can add the laptop as an object to avoid, then generalize this rule to other electronic devices. It can also mask superfluous details, like a bowl on the table.

Tested in simulation for 2D navigation tasks and manipulation with a seven-degree-of-freedom Franka robotic arm, FLEX outperformed existing methods. In seven navigation scenarios, it achieved the highest normalized rewards. Successive corrections allowed the system to understand intent after one or two instructions, even with unpredictable human feedback. Twelve participants confirmed its superiority in task performance, intent understanding, and ease of use.

For now, FLEX is limited to static tasks with rigid objects and may fail with highly personal preferences. However, this approach paves the way for more intuitive robot learning, where mistakes become opportunities for correction rather than bugs to anticipate.
