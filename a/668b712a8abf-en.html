BeagleBoard Unveils Two Open Source Modules for Up to 4 Motor Control

BeagleBoard simplifies multi-motor control with two open source modules: a controller and a 4-motor brushless board.

Developing robotic or industrial projects with multiple brushless motors often struggles with the complexity of simultaneous control. BeagleBoard offers an open source solution with two new modules: the BeagleConnect Torque and the BeagleConnect QuadESC. The first acts as the system’s brain, while the second directly manages up to four motors.

The BeagleConnect Torque is built around a Texas Instruments AM13E23019 microcontroller, featuring a 200 MHz Arm Cortex-M33 core, 512 KB of flash memory, and 128 KB of SRAM. It includes a dedicated trigonometric math unit for motor control algorithms and a TinyEngine neural network accelerator for tasks like fault detection. The module offers three 12-bit analog-to-digital converters, dedicated PWM resources for motor control, and various communication interfaces (CAN FD, UART, SPI, I²C). A CAN transceiver (TI TCAN844) and a Qwiic connector simplify sensor or peripheral integration.

The BeagleConnect QuadESC combines the Torque module with four independent brushless motor driver circuits. Each channel uses a gate driver (TI DRV8323SRTAR) and three half-bridge devices (CSD88599Q5DC) to control a brushless DC motor. The QuadESC provides three-phase current and voltage measurements per channel, along with a temperature sensor and battery voltage monitoring. These data enable advanced control techniques, such as field-oriented control, for precise torque and speed regulation. Powered by a 3S to 6S battery (9 V to 25.2 V), it delivers up to 40 A continuous and 70 A peak per motor.

These modules target developers looking to implement custom firmware. BeagleBoard provides open source KiCad design files, schematics, and development resources, allowing hardware modifications or integration into existing projects. Pricing and availability have not yet been announced.
