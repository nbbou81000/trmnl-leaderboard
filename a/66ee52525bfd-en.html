Arduino Launches $34 USB Camera for Computer Vision

A standard USB webcam tailored for Arduino UNO Q and VENTUNO Q boards to streamline computer vision integration.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Arduino has introduced a USB webcam designed specifically for its UNO Q and VENTUNO Q development boards, aiming to simplify the integration of computer vision in electronic projects. Unlike a standard webcam, this camera is optimized to work without additional driver installations or complex configurations, thanks to native integration with Arduino’s ecosystem.

The camera features a Sony IMX376 sensor capable of capturing video up to 4K at 30 frames per second, with autofocus to automatically adjust focus based on subject distance. An integrated HD microphone records audio alongside video, while a touch-sensitive LED ring with adjustable color temperature ensures consistent lighting in varying environments. Three mounting options are included: a monitor clip, a flat desk stand, and a standard tripod thread.

The camera is compatible with Arduino App Lab, a platform offering AI models for local image processing. Potential applications include object detection with YOLOX, image classification via MobileNet, or gesture recognition using MediaPipe. On the more powerful VENTUNO Q board, vision-language models like Qwen VLM can describe a scene in natural language. The built-in audio also enables applications combining voice and visual recognition, such as interactive robots or voice-controlled interfaces.

Priced at $34 and available from the official Arduino Store, this camera prioritizes ease of use over groundbreaking technical features.
