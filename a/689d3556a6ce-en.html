Restoring a Polaroid 630 SL Lightmixer Instant Camera: A Retro Repair Project

A 1990s Polaroid 630 SL Lightmixer, bought in poor condition, is restored after hours of disassembly and repair of corroded components and fragile plastic mechanisms.

The Polaroid 630 SL Lightmixer, a late-80s instant camera produced between 1986 and 1992, represents the pre-digital era of consumer photography. Unlike earlier 1970s models, this camera features an all-plastic construction, designed for easy assembly using plastic clips, which also makes repairs or restorations more accessible.

[Odd Tinkering]’s project begins with the purchase of a broken unit for just $10. The observed issues include an unreliable trigger button, spontaneous photo-taking, and a non-functional flash. After hours of careful disassembly, the root cause is identified as corrosion on certain connectors, along with a damaged internal component: a flash switch made from a bent copper strip, broken during the process.

The restoration involves cleaning the corroded contacts, repairing the switch by soldering, and touching up the exterior paint. Once completed, the camera functions almost identically to its original state from around 1990. It remains possible to use 600-series instant film, still available due to the enduring nostalgia for these retro devices.
