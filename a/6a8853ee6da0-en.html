Building a Homemade Induction Soldering Iron: Two Detailed Designs

Two open-source projects let you build an induction soldering iron, more responsive and stable than resistive models, without temperature sensors.

Induction soldering irons heat the tip directly via an internal coil, offering better responsiveness and stability than resistive models with the same power rating. Their main advantage is the lack of temperature sensors: the tip’s temperature is regulated by the material’s Curie point. Once this threshold is reached, heating efficiency drops sharply, providing natural self-regulation.

Two open-source designs to adapt

The first project is inspired by SergeyMax, who reverse-engineered a Metcal soldering iron. Frank Zhao adapted this design to use components available in America, operating at 13.56 MHz with an F-type connector. This version is compatible with handpieces from several manufacturers like Hakko or Metcal. It supports power input via XT30 connector or USB-PD and fits in a compact case.

The second, more affordable design is based on the open-source RadioThermal project. Frank Zhao added similar power options (XT30 or USB-PD), an external jumper-based power level selector, and fuse protection. Operating at 470 kHz, it uses a custom handpiece built around a commercial soldering iron tip.

These projects prove that building a high-performance induction soldering iron is feasible without relying on rare or expensive components, while benefiting from passive thermal regulation and improved responsiveness.
