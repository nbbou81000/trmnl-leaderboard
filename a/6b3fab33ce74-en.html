NINASENSE nRF: Ultra-Low-Power Wireless Sensor Board with Natural Language Programming

An open-source wireless sensor board powered by two AAA batteries runs for months without maintenance thanks to its energy-efficient design.

A platform designed for long-term autonomy

NINASENSE nRF, developed by NearMeter Technologies, stands out for its ability to operate for months or even years on just two AAA batteries. This autonomy is achieved by omitting power-hungry components such as USB interfaces, USB-to-serial converters, or always-on voltage regulators, which are common on most development boards.

Technical features and connectivity

The board is powered by a Nordic Semiconductor nRF52832 microcontroller, featuring a 64 MHz Arm Cortex-M4F processor and Bluetooth Low Energy connectivity. An LLCC68 radio chip adds support for LoRa and sub-GHz communication, providing both short-range and long-range wireless options. Users can select between a helical antenna or an SMA connector for the LoRa module. The board also includes a three-axis LIS2DH12 accelerometer for motion or vibration detection, as well as inputs for external sensors like DS18B20 temperature probes or Hall-effect sensors. Eight GPIO pins are available, three of which support analog inputs, and dedicated circuitry monitors battery voltage.

NINASENSE nRF can interface directly with Home Assistant via Bluetooth to transmit sensor data or trigger automations. For remote deployments, a LoRa configuration relays information to a dedicated bridge, which then connects to Home Assistant. The board can also act as a bridge for existing FSK/GFSK wireless sensors, with planned integrations for devices from brands like VEVOR, Ambient Weather, and Ecowitt.

The project’s most distinctive feature is nRFClaw Studio, a companion software that allows programming the board using natural language. Users describe their application in plain English, and the software generates the corresponding firmware, which can be wirelessly flashed via Bluetooth. Once programmed, the board runs its tasks locally without requiring a permanent cloud connection.

NINASENSE nRF is an open-source project: its hardware schematics and bill of materials will be published during the crowdfunding campaign. The nRFClaw Studio software is already available on GitHub, with additional documentation, application examples, and integrations planned for release. Pricing and campaign launch details have not yet been announced.
