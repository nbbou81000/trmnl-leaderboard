Simplifying Trigonometric Calculations with a Slide Rule

A method to use a slide rule with trigonometric functions and complex calculations.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Slide rules, iconic tools before the electronic calculator era, rely on a simple principle: adding logarithms and then converting to antilogarithms. This method enables precise multiplication and division, provided you estimate the order of magnitude of the results. For example, 2 × 3 yields the same result as 2 × 3000 or 200 × 300 on a slide rule, requiring prior estimation.

However, trigonometric functions like sine, cosine, or quadratics complicate the use of these tools. Bob the Science Guy, who has created a series of videos on the topic, offers a workaround. He explains how to convert addition or subtraction problems into trigonometric problems, making them easier to solve with a slide rule. While counterintuitive, this method leverages the tool’s full potential.

For those eager to practice or rediscover how to use a slide rule, an interactive simulator is available online. Simply open it in full-screen mode to view both sides of the tool and manipulate the virtual sliders.
