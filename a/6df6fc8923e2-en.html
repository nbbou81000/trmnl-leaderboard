A Giant, Fully Functional 3D-Printed N64 Controller

A fully functional, oversized N64 controller, 3D-printed and assembled with precision, redefines gaming ergonomics.

To turn a standard Nintendo 64 controller into a massive, fully functional version, ChaosBench merged 3D printing, meticulous assembly, and custom electronics. The project starts with a 3D model of the original controller, hollowed out and sliced into printable sections. The PLA parts, printed separately, are joined using plastic welding and glue, then smoothed with body filler before being primed and painted in N64 gray. Despite these steps, minor cracks appear at the seams due to the PLA’s properties.

The oversized buttons posed a mechanical challenge. ChaosBench designed custom assemblies with microswitches placed beneath each 3D-printed button. Anti-rotation guides keep the buttons aligned, but early versions suffered from binding. After adjusting tolerances by fractions of a millimeter, the issue persisted: the original microswitch springs were too weak to push the massive buttons back up. Heavy-duty springs solved the problem, though they require significantly more force to press.

Electronically, a zero-delay USB encoder translates button presses into signals recognized by a computer as a USB gamepad input. The controller’s size meant the original wiring harnesses were too short, so ChaosBench built extension cables using crimped connectors and heat-shrink tubing. However, the encoder only supports 12 buttons, leaving the Z-trigger and one C-button non-functional. Worse, the arcade-style joystick only offers four directional inputs without analog control, preventing diagonals and making games like Super Mario 64 difficult to play. Mario Kart 64 remains partially playable, but the giant accelerator button demands considerable effort.
