A DIY AI-Powered Lockpicking Robot Using ESP32

A researcher automates pin tumbler lockpicking with a DIY robot, an ESP32, and real-time AI feedback.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Traditional pin tumbler lockpicking relies on tactile sensitivity and patience, but Dr. Kun Dániel’s DIY robot offers an automated alternative. His machine, designed to mount directly onto a lock cylinder, uses sensors and artificial intelligence to replicate the techniques of an experienced locksmith.

The device is built around an ESP32-C3 Mini development board, which connects via Wi-Fi to Anthropic’s Claude to process real-time data. The mechanical assembly, mostly 3D-printed, includes a stepper motor, two servos, and two load cells. These sensors continuously measure pin resistance, allowing the system to adjust its movements dynamically. An OLED display provides live feedback, while a rechargeable lithium-ion battery powers the electronics.

Unlike rigid mechanical approaches, the AI analyzes sensor feedback to determine the optimal picking strategy. The robot probes each pin individually, adapting pressure and position based on collected data. A demonstration video shows the system successfully opening a standard lock, though the project remains a work in progress. The researcher has already spent over 200 hours refining it and plans to release the source code, STL files, and full documentation soon, enabling other makers to build and experiment with the technology.
