Teen Improves Brain-Computer Interface with AI and EEG

A brainwave-controlled system, enhanced by AI, enables users to type full sentences in under five seconds.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

At just 14 years old, Aarav Sharma built a brain-controlled robotic arm. Since then, he has expanded the project into a broader communication system based on brainwave detection.

His system, called SSVEP (Steady State Visually Evoked Potential), works by detecting brain responses to visual targets flashing at different frequencies. When a user focuses on a target, an EEG headset records the corresponding brain activity and converts it into a command. Sharma developed an interface capable of handling six simultaneous commands, enhanced by word suggestions from an OpenAI language model. Tests showed users could type full sentences in under five seconds.

To refine the system, Sharma conducted offline EEG data collection, asking participants to focus on six flashing targets. He analyzed command accuracy based on the amount of EEG data collected, using TFR graphs (Time-Frequency Representation) to visualize detected frequencies and a confusion matrix to identify misclassified targets. His long-term goal is to move the user interface to AR glasses for a more portable and practical experience.

More details about his brain-computer interface projects are available at aaravinnovate.com/bci-neurova.
