A Modular ESP32-S3 Flight Controller for DIY RC Projects

A maker designed a scalable ESP32-S3 flight controller, inspired by Pixhawk designs, to streamline custom RC projects.

A maker going by The Engineer has created a modular flight controller based on the ESP32-S3, aimed at custom RC (radio-controlled) projects. Unlike traditional setups that rely on messy wiring and multiple modules, this design consolidates core functions while maintaining expandability.

The system is built around an Espressif ESP32-S3 microcontroller, which includes built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, along with numerous GPIO pins. An onboard inertial measurement unit (IMU) featuring an accelerometer and gyroscope communicates over SPI to provide real-time motion data. Two independent 3.3 V voltage regulators power the ESP32-S3 and the IMU separately, reducing electrical noise.

The controller includes multiple JST-GH connectors, compatible with Pixhawk standards, to simplify the addition of sensors or modules via UART, I2C, and SPI interfaces. The board, designed using KiCad, is compact thanks to components placed on both sides. Assembly was done manually using a hot plate for reflow soldering, while plastic JST connectors were soldered separately to avoid heat damage.

After successful electrical tests, the maker programmed the ESP32-S3 with MicroPython and verified the IMU’s functionality. A React application was then developed to display a 3D model of an airplane or drone, whose movements mirror the controller’s orientation in real time via WebSockets. While not yet used to pilot a real aircraft, the prototype is functional and expected to expand with the addition of GPS sensors and LoRa modules.
