An AI-Powered Interactive Halloween Pumpkin with Local Processing

A plastic pumpkin turned into a smart Halloween decoration using Arduino and embedded AI models, with no internet required.

Halloween just got a little creepier with Spooky Sassy Pumpkin, a project by maker Naveen Kumar that turns a plastic pumpkin into an interactive AI-powered decoration. Unlike traditional motion-triggered props, this pumpkin listens to visitors’ questions, processes images from its camera, and responds with eerie or sarcastic remarks—all while running locally without internet access.

The project is built around an Arduino VENTUNO Q board, featuring a Qualcomm Dragonwing IQ8 processor and an STM32H5 microcontroller. This setup delivers 40 TOPS of neural processing power and 16 GB of RAM, enabling multiple AI models to run simultaneously. A Logitech C270 webcam provides both video and audio input, while a 3-watt speaker outputs the pumpkin’s responses. An M5Stack FlashLight Unit illuminates the pumpkin from within, with lighting effects that change based on the AI’s activity: candle-like flickering when idle, steady glow when listening, slow pulsing while thinking, and rapid flashing while speaking.

Four AI models work together in the software stack. A face detection model identifies approaching visitors, a quantized Whisper Small model converts speech to text, the Qwen3-VL-4B vision-language model generates sarcastic or spooky responses based on the question and image, and Melo handles text-to-speech conversion. Responses are limited to under ten words to keep interactions snappy. The project still faces limitations, particularly delays during speech recognition initialization that can cause it to miss the start of questions.
