TeethMark: A Dental Retainer to Control a PC with Your Tongue

A transparent dental retainer fitted with electrodes lets users control electronics through tongue gestures, including tactile feedback.

Researchers at Aarhus University have developed TeethMark, a transparent dental retainer equipped with electrodes that turns the tongue into an input device for controlling electronic devices. The system uses a flexible printed circuit board embedded in the retainer, featuring capacitive sensing electrodes to detect three types of gestures: tapping a single tooth, sliding the tongue across multiple teeth, or touching several teeth at once.

Unlike standard touch interfaces, TeethMark incorporates electrotactile feedback. Small electrical pulses are delivered via electrodes placed on the inner surfaces of the teeth, perceptible to the tongue. Additional electrodes on the outer surfaces stimulate the inside of the lips to deliver notifications without requiring tongue movement. An ESP32 microcontroller handles capacitive sensing and controls the stimulation circuitry, while a MOSFET driver generates the signals and a relay switches between sensing and feedback modes to prevent interference. The four-layer flexible PCB includes shielding to improve sensing reliability and can be customized using a 3D modeling tool to adapt electrode positions to the user’s dental structure.

In tests with 12 participants, TeethMark achieved recognition rates of 82.92% for tapping, 92.78% for multi-tooth touches, and 94.92% for sliding gestures. Directional electrotactile feedback was correctly identified 99.17% of the time. The researchers suggest potential applications such as language learning assistance, tactile sensations in virtual reality, tongue rehabilitation exercises, or controlling tools like a UV curing light. However, the prototype remains experimental and faces challenges, including distinguishing intentional gestures from natural tongue movements during speech or swallowing.
