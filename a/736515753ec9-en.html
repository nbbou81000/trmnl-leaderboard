Teensy 4.0 and 4.1: Ultra-Fast Microcontrollers for Your Projects

Arm Cortex-M7 development boards running at 600 MHz, perfect for demanding projects.

The Teensy 4.0 and Teensy 4.1 are development boards designed by Paul Stoffregen and sold by SparkFun. Visually similar to an Arduino Nano or Pro Micro, they stand out with exceptional performance. Their core is a 600 MHz Arm Cortex-M7 microcontroller, a speed far exceeding most comparable boards, which typically run at just a few dozen MHz. This power allows for dynamic clock scaling, with the option to overclock up to 1.008 GHz with proper cooling.

Both models share common features: 1 MB of RAM, an integrated RTC (battery-powered), a USB host port, and up to 40 or 55 GPIO pins depending on the model. The Teensy 4.0 offers 1.984 MB of flash memory and a compact size (1.4 x 0.7 in), while the Teensy 4.1 provides 7.936 MB of flash, an elongated form factor (2.4 x 0.7 in), and a 10/100 Mbit Ethernet PHY (without an integrated RJ45 jack).

Pricing remains affordable: $23.80 for the 4.0 and $31.50 for the 4.1. While cheaper alternatives exist, these boards serve as an "insurance policy" for projects requiring high computing power from the start, avoiding costly redesigns. Their mature ecosystem and active community also make them a strong choice for advanced applications.

Notable projects leveraging their capabilities include a VTOL drone flight controller, an interactive lighting control system for a geodesic dome sculpture, and a touch-based musical instrument driven by 18 capacitive buttons and 16 potentiometers. Accessories like Qwiic shields (for easy I2C module connections), wired Ethernet kits, and lockable versions (to protect code) complete the lineup.
