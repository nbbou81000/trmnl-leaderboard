Wi-Fi MediaTek MT7921AU Turned into an SDR Receiver

Two projects leverage a hidden feature in the MediaTek MT7921AU Wi-Fi chipset to turn it into a software-defined radio receiver.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A previously undocumented I/Q sampling feature, originally intended for debugging, has been discovered in the MediaTek MT7921AU Wi-Fi chipset, commonly found in USB adapters. Two independent projects now leverage this capability to repurpose the adapter as a software-defined radio receiver (SDR).

Two distinct approaches

The BirriaSDR project extracts raw I/Q samples at 40 Msps with 14-bit resolution using libusb, without altering the firmware. The adapter returns to its original state after use. The supported frequency range spans from 2156 to 2610 MHz, with an API compatible with RTL-SDR.

In contrast, Mtk79ICAP uses a SoapySDR interface and applies minor firmware modifications. It achieves a maximum bandwidth of 80 MHz and covers the 5 GHz bands. The adapter can operate simultaneously in Wi-Fi monitor mode and stream SDR data, provided automatic gain control is enabled.

These projects follow a recent trend, after a similar discovery on the MediaTek MT7996. Wi-Fi adapters, often equipped with external antennas, simplify integration with frequency mixer circuits. While these solutions are not yet mature, hobbyists have already used an ESP32 to transmit arbitrary signals.
