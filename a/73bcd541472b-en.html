Generating QR Codes with Halftone Images for Aesthetic Rendering

A method to embed halftone images in QR codes without compromising scannability.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

While QR codes are traditionally monochrome and functional, they can be customized for aesthetic or marketing purposes. A basic approach involves embedding a regular image in the center and relying on the scanner’s error correction to bypass disruptions. However, a more advanced method, first demonstrated in a 2013 paper by Hung-Kuo Chu et al., allows halftone images to be seamlessly integrated into QR codes.

Jorge C. Lucero has implemented this technique as a client-side JavaScript tool. It works by dividing each QR code data module into a 3×3 grid of submodules. Only the central submodule determines the module’s color, while the others are used to create grayscale or color gradients, forming a halftone image. The result is a visually appealing QR code where the embedded image remains visible without compromising scannability.

The tool includes an ‘enhancement’ mode that improves image clarity but may introduce scanning errors requiring manual correction. This method delivers better visual quality than simple binary image embedding, especially with high-resolution QR codes.
