Geolocating a Video Using Aircraft Doppler Shifts

A surprising method uses aircraft sounds captured in a video to pinpoint its exact location.

Photos and videos often contain unintentional clues about their recording location, even when such details are deliberately obscured. A recent method developed by colsto uses Doppler shifts generated by nearby aircraft to pinpoint a video’s location with high accuracy.

Aircraft emit sounds audible over long distances, even if their presence isn’t perceptible to the human ear. By analyzing these sounds in a spectrogram (a visual representation of frequencies), characteristic Doppler curves can be extracted. These patterns must then be matched against real aircraft flight paths, available via public ADS-B (Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast) data, which provide altitude, speed, heading, and position at regular intervals.

The method becomes more precise when multiple Doppler curves are present in the recording: it involves searching for locations where the curves match the expected intervals and characteristics. A filtering step eliminates areas where too many Doppler curves would have been heard, narrowing down potential candidates. The shape of the curve itself provides additional clues: its height reflects the aircraft’s speed, while its length depends on distance. By reconstructing theoretical Doppler curves from aircraft trajectories, it becomes possible to compare them with real data to identify the exact recording location. In a test, even a single Doppler curve was enough to locate a video without any other clues.
