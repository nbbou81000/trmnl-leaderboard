NASA Tests Pressure-Sensitive Paint for Aerodynamics Research

NASA employs fluorescent paint that reacts to pressure to study airflow over aircraft wings in wind tunnels.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

NASA has conducted its first large-scale tests of an unsteady Pressure Sensitive Paint in a wind tunnel. This special coating glows under ultraviolet light, with brightness varying according to the pressure applied by airflow over a surface. High-speed cameras record these changes, generating data for aerodynamic modeling around aircraft wings or rockets.

Developed over several years by researchers at Langley and Ames centers, the technique had previously been tested mainly on fixed models in standard wind tunnels. This experiment involved a large-scale, freely moving model in a low-oxygen environment, made possible by the unique capabilities of the Transonic Dynamic Tunnel. Researchers consider this paint a valuable tool with potential for broader wind tunnel applications.
