Researchers Integrate a Touchscreen Directly into Fabric

Electronic fibers embedding LEDs and sensors enable a flexible, washable touchscreen integrated into clothing.

Touchscreens are now common in many devices, but integrating them into clothing has long been impractical due to their rigidity and sensitivity to mechanical stress. Researchers at the National University of Singapore (NUS) have overcome this challenge by developing electronic fibers that combine touchscreen functionality and sensing capabilities directly into fabric.

Each fiber contains individually addressable LEDs and a touch detection system. To assemble them, the team created a manufacturing process using a laser to expose specific points on insulated wires, followed by controlled wetting to form electrical connections only at those spots. This method connected over 5,000 chips with more than 20,000 connections. The resulting fibers, just one millimeter thick and up to ten meters long, are assembled into layers using a thermal drawing process.

A sleeve prototype, woven with 54 fibers each containing 96 LEDs, forms a 168×105 mm display with a resolution of 96×54 pixels in 8-bit color at 30 frames per second. The system supports multi-touch detection, and each fiber has its own control channel, preventing a single failure from disrupting the entire display. In addition to the screen, specialized fibers include a microphone, speaker, motion sensors, ambient light sensor, temperature sensor, and magnetometer. These components enable functions like running a calculator, displaying photos, playing videos, receiving images via Bluetooth, or acting as a compass. A temperature-sensing fiber under the arm can also measure the wearer’s body temperature.

Tests showed that the fabric’s light output and touch response remained nearly unchanged after 50 machine washes and 1,000 bends with a five-millimeter radius. Breathability remains comparable to common fabrics like cotton, polyester, and denim. The researchers are now focusing on higher-resolution displays, additional sensing features, and on-device AI integration.
