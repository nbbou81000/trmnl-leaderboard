A Standalone AI-Powered Calorie Tracker with DIY E-Ink Display

A DIY project leverages local AI and a camera to estimate food calories without relying on a smartphone or cloud services.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

To overcome the hassle of mobile nutrition tracking apps, a maker has built a standalone system that analyzes food on a plate. The device is powered by a D-Robotics RDK X5 single-board computer, featuring an octa-core Arm Cortex-A55 processor running at 1.5 GHz, up to 8 GB of RAM, and a dedicated Brain Processing Unit (BPU) delivering 10 TOPS of AI performance. This setup allows the system to run computer vision models locally, without relying on an external GPU or cloud server.

A D-Robotics binocular stereo camera, connected via the MIPI-CSI interface, captures images of meals. The creator initially used a YOLOv9 object detection model but switched to a more precise segmentation model. This model identifies pixels belonging to each food item, distinguishing between items like a sandwich, banana, broccoli, or cake slice while estimating their size. The data is then used to calculate calorie estimates, displayed on a 4-inch Waveshare screen through a custom web-based dashboard.

The entire setup is housed in a 3D-printed enclosure, inspired by a loaf of bread and colored in beige and chocolate-brown. While calorie estimates based on images have limitations (misjudged portions, hidden ingredients), this project proves that autonomous, cloud-free nutritional tracking is achievable with accessible tools.
