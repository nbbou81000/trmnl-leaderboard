A 1990s Credit Card Reader Repurposed as a Solar System Guide

A 1990s credit card reader, the TRANZ 330, is turned into an interactive solar system guide through hardware and software hacks.

The TRANZ 330, a 1990s credit card reader, hides a far more ambitious architecture than its keypad and display suggest. Beneath its surface lies a complete Z80-based computer, a legendary chip from the early days of personal computing. This makes it a prime candidate for retrocomputing projects, as demonstrated by [CharlesD].

By leveraging the device’s UV-erasable ROMs, he replaced the original firmware with a new program. His goal? To turn the reader into an interactive solar system guide, usable with a telescope. To achieve this, he first mapped out the keypad and display’s functionality, then added a GPS module and a thermal printer compatible with the era. The system then calculates the azimuth and elevation of planets to display their real-time sky positions.

The project, fully documented and accompanied by source code, showcases the ease of repurposing retro hardware. The UV-erasable ROMs allow for straightforward reprogramming, while the addition of modern peripherals (GPS, printer) extends the device’s capabilities. A practical demonstration of the hidden power in everyday devices.
