Tilefinch: A Modern Browser for Retro PSP

A lightweight web browser brings modern browsing to aging PSP consoles.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Sony PlayStation Portable (PSP), released in 2004, included a web browser, but its performance was severely limited. Steve Janovitz has now developed Tilefinch, a modern browser tailored for the PSP, leveraging its hardware efficiently.

The PSP features a 333 MHz MIPS32 processor and, depending on the model, 64 MB of RAM. While impressive for its time, these specs are now dwarfed by the demands of contemporary websites. Tilefinch addresses this by using lightweight software components: the Lexbor rendering engine for page display and the QuickJS JavaScript interpreter for dynamic content. These choices maximize the PSP’s built-in capabilities, including hardware video decoding, to deliver a smoother browsing experience.

User feedback highlights that Tilefinch enables access to websites previously unusable on the PSP, such as basic pages or image services. However, challenges remain: modern, memory-intensive pages still struggle to load properly. Some users suggest complementary solutions, like deploying a DNS sinkhole (e.g., Pi-hole) to reduce request loads, or even running Tilefinch on newer microcontrollers like the ESP32-P4, whose theoretical performance could offer promising results.

Tilefinch represents a significant step forward for retro gaming and electronics enthusiasts, demonstrating that older devices can be modernized with the right tools.
