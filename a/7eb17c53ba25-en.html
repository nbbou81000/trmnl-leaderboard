Linux 7.3: VRAM Management, Btrfs/Ext4 Boosts, and ARM/RISC-V/MIPS Support

Major Linux kernel update featuring memory optimizations, filesystem improvements, and expanded support for ARM, RISC-V, and MIPS architectures.

Linus Torvalds announced the release of Linux 7.3 after a week of development without major surprises. The final version is now available, while the merge window for Linux 7.4 opens with a slight delay due to family vacations. No late modifications will be accepted during this phase.

Key technical improvements

This release introduces better video memory (VRAM) management for low-end GPUs, preventing conflicts between applications by prioritizing protected buffers. The Btrfs and Ext4 filesystems receive significant optimizations: Btrfs regains its lost Direct I/O performance, with up to 5x improvements in latency and throughput for non-SSD disks, while Ext4 enhances parallel writes and fallocate() performance.

User-space filesystem (FUSE) performance with io_uring is also boosted through optimized memory pool management and the addition of zero-copy transfers. Additionally, the full implementation of sched_ext now allows assigning BPF-based task schedulers to control groups (cgroups), offering greater flexibility in process management.

Updates for ARM, RISC-V, and MIPS architectures

Linux 7.3 brings fixes and extensions for multiple architectures. For ARM SoCs, improvements include clock driver cleanups, MMC, RTC, and NMI controller support, along with fixes for devices like the PinePhone or TV boxes. New devices are now supported, including the EmbedFire LubanCat 4 (RK3588S) and Anbernic RG Vita-Pro (RK3576).

Rockchip SoC updates cover clock driver fixes, PHY, and IOMMU controllers, while Amlogic SoCs gain support for models A1 and T7. Samsung introduces a new high-end SoC, the Exynos 1580 (or Exynos 8855), with improvements to its PHY and power management drivers. Finally, Qualcomm adds support for a new mysterious SoC based on Cortex-A78C cores, named "Shikra."
