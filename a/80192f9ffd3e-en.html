LocalDot: Open-Source PCB Turns 3rd-Gen Echo Dot into Local Home Assistant Voice Assistant

An open-source PCB replaces the mainboard of a 3rd-gen Echo Dot to turn it into a local Home Assistant voice assistant, without relying on the cloud.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

LocalDot, an open-source project by AAElectronics, offers a replacement mainboard for the 3rd-generation Amazon Echo Dot (Donut and Crumpet models only; Clock version excluded). The goal is to repurpose the smart speaker into a fully local voice assistant compatible with Home Assistant, eliminating dependency on Amazon’s cloud services.

The board features two ESP32 chips: the ESP32-S3 handles Wi-Fi connectivity and device control, while the ESP32-P4 manages audio processing. It retains the original hardware, including the four-microphone array with beamforming, speaker, buttons, LEDs, and enclosure, as well as the 12V power supply. No enclosure modifications or soldering are required for installation.

Key local features include on-device wake-word detection, voice command processing, timer management, notifications, and intercom experiments via Home Assistant. Once a wake word is detected, voice commands are sent to the configured Home Assistant server for processing, enabling smart-home control or task execution without an internet connection. The project also supports custom wake words and audio experimentation.

Design files (schematics, BOM, ESPHome configuration, and ESP-IDF firmware) will be released under the CERN-OHL-S-2.0 license on GitHub. The Crowd Supply crowdfunding campaign has already exceeded its $7,500 funding goal. Pricing is set at $50 for one board, $98 for two, and $145 for three, with shipping costs of $8 within the US and $18 internationally. Shipments are expected to begin on April 17, 2027, and the campaign ends on December 17, 2026. Orders will be fulfilled by Mouser Electronics.
