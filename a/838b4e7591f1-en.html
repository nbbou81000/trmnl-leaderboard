A Soft Robotic Gripper That Rotates Objects Without Dropping Them

Spanish researchers develop a soft gripper that can rotate objects while keeping a firm hold on them.

Researchers from the Universidad Carlos III de Madrid and the Universidad Pública de Navarra have developed a soft robotic gripper that can manipulate an object between its fingers without releasing it. Unlike existing grippers, which focus on grasping and holding, this system enables more complex movements, such as rotating an object to read a label or unscrewing a light bulb, without breaking contact.

The device features three soft fingers arranged in a triangle, each with three degrees of freedom: two tendon-driven bending modes and rotation around its base. Electric motors pull internal tendons to bend the fingers in different directions, depending on whether one or two tendons are activated simultaneously. A third motor rotates the entire finger. The structure, 3D-printed in NinjaFlex 85A TPU with a 30 % gyroid infill, mechanically limits excessive deformation through physical contact between its sections.

Tests on 11 diverse objects (bottle, tissue package, fidget spinner, toys, etc.) achieved a 98.2 % success rate over 55 grasping attempts. The only failure involved an artificial flower slipping. The key demonstration shows the gripper’s ability to rotate an object while maintaining a firm hold, a feature useful for component assembly, handling fragile items, or real-time grip adjustment.
