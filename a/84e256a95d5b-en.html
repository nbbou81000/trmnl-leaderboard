AI at the Speed of Light: Two Photonic Computing Prototypes

Turkish researchers leverage light to cut AI power consumption, achieving high accuracy in classification tasks.

As AI models grow in complexity, their computational demands and power consumption soar. Researchers at Koç University in Turkey propose a radical alternative: offloading some calculations to optical systems. Two experimental prototypes, led by Assistant Professor Uğur Teğin, demonstrate that light can perform complex computations more efficiently than traditional processors.

Optical neural network preserves color data

The first system employs a spatial light modulator (SLM), a programmable device that controls light phase. Instead of converting images to grayscale to simplify processing, the team splits data into red, green, and blue channels and encodes them onto the SLM. Optical transformations—based on interference and diffraction—then combine this information. A camera captures the resulting light patterns, which feed into a minimal electronic classifier. On medical images (HAM10000), accuracy jumps from 68 % to 98 %, and from 34 % to 79 % on natural images (STL-10).

Multimode fiber laser as a physical processor

The second prototype leverages complex interactions between light modes inside a multimode fiber laser cavity. Images are first encoded as phase patterns using an SLM, then injected into the laser. Interference and nonlinear gain saturation transform these inputs into distinctive intensity patterns, classified by a simple algorithm. Tests on recyclable waste, satellite imagery, and retinal scans achieve success rates between 85 % and 99 %, using just 2,500 to 10,000 trainable parameters—far fewer than the millions required by conventional deep neural networks.

Both systems remain experimental and still rely on electronic components for control and interpretation. Yet they show that computationally intensive AI operations can be handled by light, paving the way for faster, more energy-efficient hardware.
