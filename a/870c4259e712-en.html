Littelfuse Discontinues All Microcontrollers, Including the Z80

Littelfuse is ending production of all its microcontrollers, including the historic Z80 family, after 50 years.

Manufacturer Littelfuse has announced the permanent discontinuation of its entire microcontroller (MCU) product lineup, ending a technological era that began in 1976 with the launch of the Zilog Z80. Designed by Federico Faggin and Masatoshi Shima, this microprocessor shaped embedded computing and consumer electronics, powering devices such as the Sinclair ZX81, Tandy TRS-80, and Namco’s iconic Pac-Man arcade machine.

After discontinuing the Z84C00 family and Z180 line in 2024, Littelfuse is now extending this decision to all its MCU products, including the still-active eZ80 range. Customers have until December 21, 2026, to place orders, with final shipments scheduled through June 2027. The company notes that remaining stock is limited and fulfillment cannot be guaranteed due to potential supply or factory capacity constraints.

Littelfuse has not provided reasons for this decision. Users of the Z80 platform will need to explore alternative solutions, such as Renaldas Zioma’s free and open-source reimplementation, to sustain their projects.
