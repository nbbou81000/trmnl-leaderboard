A $5 ESP32 Fixes Bluetooth Range Issues in Home Assistant

A $5 ESP32 DevKit V1 extends Home Assistant’s Bluetooth range without extra sensors or components.

A temperature sensor in the living room kept dropping out of Home Assistant, disrupting a smart fan automation. After confirming the sensor and its batteries were fine, the issue was traced to the limited Bluetooth range of the Home Assistant server’s built-in adapter, located in the bedroom. The author solved it with a ESP32 DevKit V1 board costing $5, configured as a Bluetooth proxy using ESPHome.

Home Assistant’s Bluetooth advertisement monitor tool helped pinpoint devices at the edge of coverage. Problematic devices like the LG C2 and Govee Table Lamp 2 showed readings of -86 dBm and -85 dBm, while stable devices near the server registered between -67 and -77 dBm. Lower negative values indicate stronger signals.

Two ESP32 proxies were deployed: one near the opposite bedroom, the other between the router and the first proxy. Their placement was optimized to avoid interference with the router’s 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi while ensuring reliable Bluetooth coverage. A tested ESP32-C6 offered no range advantage despite Bluetooth 5.3 support, confirming that antenna design and environment are critical factors. The $5 DevKit V1 boards proved sufficient for covering dead zones.

The solution requires no additional sensors or circuit modifications. After setup, previously unstable Bluetooth devices are now consistently accessible, with stable signal readings. The author advises checking Wi-Fi signal strength at the proxy location, as a loss of Wi-Fi connectivity renders the relay ineffective.
