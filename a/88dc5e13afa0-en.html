PlugMate and PlugClaw: Tiny Android-Based Private Computers with Optional Ubuntu/OpenClaw Support

Two secure, modem-free Android sticks for private, portable computing.

TrustKernel, known for secure execution technologies, has launched two tiny standalone computers in USB-C form factor: the PlugMate and PlugClaw. These devices, the size of a USB flash drive, feature a MediaTek Helio G80 octa-core processor, 4 to 6 GB of RAM, and 128 GB of encrypted eMMC storage. They run PlugOS, a hardened Android 14 system, without a modem or battery, designed for private and secure computing.

Operation and security

The PlugMate (199 USD on sale, 299 USD MSRP) and PlugClaw (149 USD on sale, 239 USD MSRP) connect to a host device (smartphone, tablet, or PC) to share its screen, keyboard, and network connection. No user data is transferred to or from the device. The PlugClaw adds an AI agent (OpenClaw) running in an Ubuntu VM, capable of automating tasks or responding to messages. Both devices leave no trace after disconnection, according to the manufacturer.

A system update is mandatory before any use. TrustKernel warns that beta builds may cause data loss, and any reset regenerates encryption keys, making data permanently inaccessible. A local backup system exists but requires prior configuration on the host via the PlugOS client.

Hardware and accessories

Both models measure 50 x 19 x 8 mm and weigh around 15 grams. The PlugMate ships with an aluminum case acting as a heatsink, while the PlugClaw includes mesh sleeves and a U-shaped USB-C adapter for flat mounting behind a smartphone. Both come with a quick start guide and warranty cards.

The PlugOS client, available on Android, iOS, Windows, macOS, and Linux, requires no account or registration. Boot time ranges from 20 to 58 seconds depending on the device state and host. Tests confirmed multi-platform compatibility, with an exception on Windows where screen recording may be blocked by a default protection setting in PlugOS.
