ESP32-C5: A 5 GHz Drone Video Receiver Using SDR

An open-source project leverages the hidden software-defined radio (SDR) capabilities of the ESP32-C5 to receive analog 5 GHz video feeds, commonly used in drones.

The ESP32-C5 microcontroller from Seeed Studio now includes an undocumented software-defined radio (SDR) feature, enabling the reception and demodulation of analog signals. Maker [Twotoz] has leveraged this capability to build a 5 GHz video receiver tailored for drone feeds.

Unlike digital transmissions, analog FM signals at 5 GHz offer ultra-low latency and robustness against interference, making them ideal for real-time control. The project uses a Xiao ESP32-C5 board paired with a resistor-ladder DAC to reconstruct the video signal. The interface includes a channel-switching button and a serial link for advanced settings.

This work highlights the potential of SDR on ESP32-C5 devices, though the impact of software latency on usability remains to be assessed. It joins other SDR-based video projects, underscoring the versatility of these microcontrollers.
