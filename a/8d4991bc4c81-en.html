Banana Pi Unveils BPI-CM7 Compute Module Based on Allwinner H618

Banana Pi is readying the BPI-CM7 compute module featuring an Allwinner H618 SoC, LPDDR4 RAM, Wi-Fi 5, and Bluetooth 5.0, aiming to rival Raspberry Pi's Compute Modules.

Banana Pi is launching the BPI-CM7 compute module, built around the Allwinner H618 system-on-chip. The module includes up to 2GB of LPDDR4 RAM and 32GB of eMMC storage, alongside a 256-byte EEPROM. It features an Arm Mali-G31 GPU and a K019 connectivity module from Kertong Polytron Technologies, based on the Cypress CYW43455 chipset, delivering dual-band Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5.0.

The module offers HDMI video and audio output, one USB 2.0 OTG port, three USB 2.0 Host ports, a microSD card reader for storage expansion, and a Fast Ethernet (10/100) port. Its general-purpose input/output (GPIO) capabilities include UART, TWI/I2C, SPI, I2S/PCM, DMIC, SPDIF, IR, and pulse-width modulation (PWM). Notably, it lacks an onboard analog-to-digital converter (ADC).

To unlock its full potential, Banana Pi provides a dedicated carrier board, the BPI-CM7IO. This board adds a mini-PCIe-style expansion slot with USB 2.0 interface, a SIM card slot for cellular modems, composite video and analog audio outputs, and a UART debug port. Pricing and availability details have not yet been announced, but Banana Pi suggests the module will be priced below an entry-level Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5.
