BeetleBot: Tiny Soft Robot Walks and Grips Without Motors

A soft robot inspired by beetles moves and grips objects using heat-activated artificial muscles, without motors.

Inspired by beetle anatomy, BeetleBot is a tiny six-legged robot with a gripper that can walk, turn, and grasp objects without traditional motors. Its movements rely on liquid crystal elastomer (LCE) artificial muscles, a heat-responsive material that contracts when heated and returns to its original shape when cooled. These muscles, 3D-printed using direct ink writing, embed flexible metallic heating elements between layers. A mere 3 V electrical input triggers a contraction of about 45 % of their original length, enabling repeated movements through heating-cooling cycles.

The robot’s 3D-printed resin body mimics beetle leg structures: one muscle group lifts the body while another provides forward propulsion. Selectively activating muscles on one side allows the robot to turn. A soft gripper, also powered by heat-activated LCE actuators, can grasp objects, with an integrated capacitive pressure sensor measuring contact forces and distinguishing shapes.

For remote control, researchers developed a stretchable finger patch featuring a carbon nanotube-based strain sensor. Hand gestures produce distinct electrical signals converted into Bluetooth Low Energy commands sent to the robot. An onboard camera and the gripper’s pressure sensor provide visual and tactile feedback, enabling real-time adjustments by the operator. While promising for medical robotics, environmental monitoring, or remote manipulation in hazardous areas, BeetleBot faces challenges: its muscles require temperatures above 100 °C, and the speed of heating-cooling cycles limits practical use.
