Arduino UNO Q: A Kit for Custom Physical Interfaces

Arduino unveils a kit for physical interfaces with its UNO Q, a Linux SBC with dual-core architecture.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Arduino has released the UNO Q SBC Controller Bundle, a kit designed to leverage the capabilities of its UNO Q board, a unique combination of a microcontroller and a Linux single-board computer. Unlike traditional Arduino boards, the UNO Q features a Qualcomm Dragonwing QRB2210 processor for the operating system and an STMicroelectronics STM32U585 coprocessor for real-time tasks, forming a "dual-brain" architecture.

The bundle includes four solder-free Modulino expansion modules: Modulino Buttons (buttons), Modulino Joystick (joystick), Modulino Knob (rotary potentiometer), and Modulino Pixels (LED display). The goal is to allow users to design custom physical interfaces, such as tactile controls for software, without relying on a standard mouse or keyboard. Arduino highlights the Arduino App Lab development environment, which simplifies communication between the two chips and the creation of tailored programs.

The kit is available with the 4GB version of the UNO Q, priced at $107.76 on the Arduino Store, a discount from the planned retail price of $119.73. A 3D-printable case, released under a Creative Commons license, is also offered on Printables. Note that the USB-C hub required for standalone use (additional HDMI, Ethernet, and USB ports) is not included.
