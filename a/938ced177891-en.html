Banana Pi BPI-M8: An Allwinner A733 SBC as an Alternative to Raspberry Pi 5

A compact Allwinner A733 SBC featuring PCIe via FFC connector and up to 128GB eMMC storage.

The Banana Pi BPI-M8 is a credit-card-sized single board computer (SBC) powered by the Allwinner A733 octa-core SoC, featuring two Cortex-A76 cores at 2 GHz, six Cortex-A55 cores at 1.8 GHz, and a RISC-V E902 real-time core. Designed as a Raspberry Pi 5 alternative, it offers enhanced connectivity options.

The board comes with 4GB of LPDDR5 RAM and storage options including up to 128GB eMMC (BPI-M8A model) or 16MB SPI NOR flash (BPI-M8B model). Display outputs include an HDMI 2.0b 4K@60 Hz port and a 4-lane MIPI DSI connector, while dual 4-lane MIPI CSI connectors support camera modules. Networking is handled by a Gigabit Ethernet port (with optional PoE), WiFi 6, and Bluetooth 5.0.

Expansion features a 16-pin FFC PCIe connector (multiplexed with DisplayPort) for adding external peripherals like graphics cards, and a UFS module socket for high-speed storage. The 40-pin GPIO header is Raspberry Pi-compatible, and three USB ports (1x USB 3.0, 2x USB 2.0) are available. Power is delivered via a 5V/4A USB-C connector, managed by an AXP318 PMIC.

The manufacturer claims support for Android, Linux, and Armbian, though system images and source code are not yet available. Documentation currently focuses on hardware details and basic setup guides. Direct competitors include the ArmSoM Sige6, Orange Pi 4 Pro, and Radxa Cubie A7A, all sharing the Allwinner A733 SoC.

Pricing starts at $109 for the BPI-M8A (64GB eMMC) and $73 for the BPI-M8B (16MB SPI) on AliExpress, or $103 and $69 on the official Banana Pi store.
