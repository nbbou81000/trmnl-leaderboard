Mathematics and Optimization: Squeezing Bytes in Firmware

Adafruit’s team explores mathematical methods to shrink firmware sizes, yielding tangible results across multiple boards.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Adafruit’s team tested mathematical data from OpenAI publications to optimize firmware memory usage, particularly for CircuitPython. Their goal was to free up bytes without compromising functionality. After analyzing 722 mathematical manuscripts, they identified known compression techniques, such as storing shared bytes once. For example, across 51 GFX fonts, this method saved 3,865 bytes—2.1% of bitmap data—without quality loss.

The most significant gains, however, came from human contributions. A Thumb-1 assembly routine for floating-point calculations, written eight years ago by Daniel Engel, freed up to 1.8 KB per board. Tested on a Feather M0, this optimization reduced floating-point multiply/divide loop times by 18%, though some conversions slowed down. Engel has not yet responded to the request to integrate his code, but Adafruit plans to submit it with full credit.

Between September and October 2026, 17 memory-related pull requests were merged. Savings varied by board: up to 2,972 bytes for the Trinket M0, 2,928 bytes for a Russian-language ESP32-C6 build, and 4,096 bytes of linker capacity reclaimed on the Pico W. Some optimizations, like ulab operator tweaks, prioritize size over speed. The reported figures reflect individual PR gains, not a combined measurement.
