A wristband detects finger gestures by tracking muscle pressure

A wearable bracelet tracks subtle pressure changes in the forearm to recognize finger gestures without cameras or electrodes.

Smartwatches and fitness trackers already monitor heart rate and activity, but they can’t tell what your fingers are doing. To solve this, a prototype wristband uses force myography (FMG), a technique that tracks muscle and tendon deformations in the forearm during finger movements. Unlike optical systems or EMG electrodes, this approach relies on pressure sensors pressed against the skin, detecting minute volume changes in muscles.

The device, entirely 3D-printed, features force sensors arranged around the wrist. Their precise placement captures pressure patterns unique to each gesture—such as clenching a fist or pinching fingers. A spring-tensioned buckle ensures consistent contact between sensors and skin, preventing loose fits (loss of sensitivity) or tight fits (data saturation).

A microcontroller gathers raw signals, while a machine learning model, pre-trained on known gestures, interprets the data in real time. The bracelet includes a rechargeable battery and wireless connectivity, making it fully portable. While challenges remain—like individual calibration or variations in wear—the project proves that a simple sensor setup can decode complex hand movements without directly observing the fingers. Future iterations could enable seamless control of electronics or robots using subtle gestures.
