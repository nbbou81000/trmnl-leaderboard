7 Underused Home Assistant Features You Should Be Using

From interactive notifications to probabilistic sensors, unlock Home Assistant’s hidden automation power.

Home Assistant’s vast feature set often hides powerful tools that can streamline smart home automations. Here are seven underused features to supercharge your setup.

Interactive notifications and probabilistic sensors

Actionable notifications turn basic alerts into direct controls. From a phone, users can trigger actions like turning off lights or adjusting thermostats without opening the app or using voice commands. The author uses them daily, including a daily medication reminder triggered by a notification with buttons.

Bayesian sensors merge multiple signals (phone presence, lighting, TV activity) to estimate probabilities, such as whether someone is home. Unlike single-signal detection, this method reduces false positives and improves the reliability of presence-based automations.

Centralized media control and automation modes

The Universal Media Player integration consolidates complex AV setups. It combines devices like set-top boxes, soundbars, and TVs into a single virtual controller. Volume controls automatically adjust to the active device, eliminating the need to switch between interfaces.

Home Assistant offers four automation modes: Restart (immediate restart), Queued (waits for completion), Parallel (runs multiple instances), or the default mode (ignores new triggers). These modes tailor behavior to specific needs, such as resetting a motion timer with each activation.

The Proximity integration enhances heating control by anticipating arrivals. By analyzing direction and distance from the home zone, it activates heating early enough to avoid a cold interior while preventing energy waste.

Since the 2026.5 update, duration-based triggers have expanded. Users can now activate automations after prolonged inactivity (e.g., 10 minutes of no motion) or monitor window openings for over 30 minutes before sending alerts.

Scripts centralize repetitive actions, avoiding duplicated code. A single update (e.g., adding a new phone) propagates across all relevant automations, simplifying maintenance.
