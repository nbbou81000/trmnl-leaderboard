DIY Guitar Effects Pedal with ESP32-S3 and Neural Amp Modeling

A standalone guitar effects pedal powered by an ESP32-S3 runs full neural amp models in real time.

A standalone guitar effects pedal built around an ESP32-S3 runs full-size Neural Amp Modeler A2 (NAM A2) models in real time. The 23-layer, eight-channel WaveNet model operates at 48 kHz, with processing split across both CPU cores using hand-written Xtensa kernels and 64-frame blocks.

Features and control

The user interface, developed in TSX and compiled to native C++, is displayed on an AMOLED or LCD touchscreen depending on the board. Controls include an effects chain (gate, compressor, chorus, drive, delay, and stereo spring reverb), an integrated tuner, and preset management stored on the pedal. A maintenance mode enables authenticated OTA updates, remote diagnostics, and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) discovery, while radios remain off during operation.

Amplifier models and configurations can be loaded from a microSD card or directly from a browser. A WebAssembly build allows using the pedal without hardware, with an interface identical to the embedded firmware.

Compatibility and setups

Multiple boards are supported, including the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-2.06 (default reference), the ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 (screenless), or bare ESP32-S3 modules. Boards with screens include a microSD slot for custom captures, while screenless versions require external power for the USB host port. USB Audio Class 2 (UAC2) interfaces at 48 kHz are compatible, such as the XTONE Pro or IK Multimedia iRig HD X models.

Impulse responses (IR) for speaker cabinets can be imported from WAV files (48 kHz, mono or stereo) placed on the SD card. Files are validated and processed directly by the pedal without prior conversion on a computer. A command-line tool also allows transferring IRs over the network in maintenance mode.
