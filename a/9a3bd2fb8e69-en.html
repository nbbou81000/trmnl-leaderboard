Turning a Smart Home into an Interactive Video Game

A developer turns a handheld Linux device and Home Assistant into a game where puzzles interact with real smart home devices.

A developer repurposed a R36 Ultra, a compact Linux handheld designed for retro gaming, into an interactive smart home controller. This device, featuring analog sticks and shoulder buttons, can emulate consoles from 8-bit to 64-bit systems. Its built-in Wi-Fi module made it ideal for connecting to home automation devices.

The creator first built Home Boy, an 8-bit recreation of his home where each room contained real smart devices. By navigating this virtual space, he could turn lights on or off, play music, or control the TV. Actions in the game triggered real-world changes through Home Assistant, an open-source home automation platform. While impractical for daily use, this approach turned home interaction into an immersive, almost magical experience.

Inspired by the initial project, he developed a puzzle game where solving clues required interacting with real devices. The story revolved around a mysterious character communicating through household gadgets, leaving clues scattered across the environment. For instance, a safe’s code was revealed by lighting a smart bulb in a sequence of colors, while a TV displayed a webpage with the next puzzle. All commands were sent via Home Assistant, bridging the game and physical devices.

The prototype, though imperfect, convinced the developer to refine the concept with more complex puzzles and a structured narrative. His goal is to create a polished version for his children, blending gaming and real-world interaction in a way that feels both fun and educational.
