6 insane homelab projects for an old laptop you don’t mind breaking

An old, underpowered laptop or PC gathering dust in a drawer can be repurposed into a geeky homelab playground. Unlike desktop PCs, their portability and low power consumption make them ideal for experimental projects without risking your main devices.

Portable offline internet

Using the free tool Kiwix, an obsolete laptop can become a self-contained digital library. By downloading offline archives of websites like Wikipedia or technical manuals in ZIM format, the computer acts as a local server accessible from any device on the network via a web browser. Perfect for areas with poor connectivity or travel.

Open-source experimental router

Installing OPNsense, an open-source routing and firewall solution, turns an old PC into a customizable router. This setup allows testing advanced configurations like VLANs, firewall rules, or VPN connections without risking the main network. A USB-to-Ethernet adapter may be needed to separate WAN and LAN connections.

Other projects include tracking aircraft via ADS-B signals using an RTL-SDR receiver, building a virtual mini data center with Proxmox VE and K3s, or running a deliberately vulnerable app like OWASP Juice Shop to learn cybersecurity. Additionally, tools like Kismet can analyze local Wi-Fi networks, detecting interference or hidden access points.
