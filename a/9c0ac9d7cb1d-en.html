A $10 ESP32 Turned into a USB OTG Macro Pad

A $10 ESP32-S3 board repurposed as a USB OTG macro pad to control volume, mute microphone, and take screenshots using an analog stick.

ESP32 microcontrollers are widely used in DIY projects due to their affordability, connectivity, and ease of programming. Models like the ESP32-S3, which include native USB support, open up new possibilities for custom PC accessories. One journalist repurposed a $10 ESP32-S3 board into a USB OTG macro pad by combining it with a KY-023 analog joystick sensor and custom firmware.

The setup maps directional inputs from the joystick to various system commands. Tilting the stick up or down adjusts the system volume, while left and right movements trigger shortcuts such as Ctrl+D to mute/unmute the microphone in Google Meet or Ctrl+E to enable/disable the camera. Pressing the joystick itself initiates a screenshot via Win+Shift+S.

To enhance functionality, the author implemented multiple command profiles switchable with the ESP32-S3’s BOOT button. A dedicated profile for video conferencing (Google Meet) coexists with a productivity profile for text editing and spreadsheets. Each profile assigns specific actions to joystick movements, reducing clutter on the desk.

This project highlights the versatility of ESP32-S3 boards, particularly their USB OTG mode, turning a sub-$10 microcontroller into a practical tool for automating repetitive tasks.
