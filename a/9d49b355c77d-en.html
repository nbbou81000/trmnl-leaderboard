A Glowing Purple 5-Segment Display Built Using Vacuum Deposition

A maker crafts a glowing purple 5-segment display using vacuum deposition, drawing inspiration from Nixie tubes.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Nixie tubes, those cold-cathode displays known for their orange glow, rely on a low-pressure gas mixture. Their production had nearly vanished, but small-scale manufacturers have revived them for hobbyists. A maker, going by Witchcraft and Alchemy, wondered what other types of displays could be created under similar conditions.

By experimenting with a vacuum chamber and a platinum physical vapor deposition (PVD) sputtering process, he found that applying a thin platinum layer to an anode plate and passing current through nearby cathodes in an argon atmosphere produced a uniform purple glow. Unlike Nixie tubes, where only the cathode lights up, this method illuminates an entire 2D surface, offering a unique aesthetic.

To build a functional display, he used a Nixie driver IC with 10 outputs. Since that wasn’t enough for two 7-segment displays, he opted for 5-segment ones, which are less conventional but fit the setup. The plate was masked and coated with titanium (as a primer) and then platinum before being placed in the vacuum chamber. Electrical connections were made through a sealed pass-through, linked to an Arduino and the driver circuit.

The final prototype took the form of a machined aluminum box, featuring a laminated glass window and a port for maintaining the vacuum. While some segments failed to work properly due to fabrication issues, the overall result is visually striking. As with Nixie tubes, the main challenge remains the complexity of artisanal manufacturing.
