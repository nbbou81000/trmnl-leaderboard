Tapo blocks third-party access by default with TPAP

Tapo plugs and bulbs now enforce a new secure protocol, TPAP, disabling third-party access by default.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Since October 2026, Tapo smart devices (plugs, bulbs, power strips, cameras, and hubs) reject third-party connections after a firmware update. The legacy KLAP protocol, based on a hash exchange, is replaced by TPAP, which uses the SPAKE2+ authentication mechanism (defined in RFC 9383). This change prevents offline replay attacks or password guessing, as each session requires a new negotiation.

,

Compatibility and configuration

,

Tapo devices now refuse third-party connections by default unless the "Third-Party Compatibility" option is manually enabled in the official app. Once activated, existing scripts can continue working without changes, either by targeting the device's IP address directly or via the discover_devices function. The tapo software client (available in Rust and Python) supports TPAP since version 0.11.1 and automatically detects the protocol used by the device.

,

The H200 and H500 hubs now support extended features, including listing and downloading recordings in MPEG-TS format, as well as managing schedules and timers directly on the device. The legacy AES protocol was removed in version 0.11.0 of the client.
