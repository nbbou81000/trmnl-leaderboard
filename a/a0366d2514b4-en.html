E-Paper at 30 FPS: Power Draw and Lifespan Tested

An experiment measures power draw and wear on an e-paper display running at 30 frames per second.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A project tested the impact of running an e-paper display at 30 frames per second (FPS), a technology known for low power consumption and longevity. The experiment focused on a Waveshare ESP32-S3-ePaper board, previously used to emulate a Game Boy at this refresh rate.

Measurements showed an average power draw of 115 mA for the entire system, with 90 mA consumed by the electronics alone. The display module itself drew only 7.4 mA at 30 FPS, confirming that e-paper’s low-power claims hold even at high refresh rates. However, lifespan depends on refresh type: manufacturers typically rate displays for one million updates, translating to about 9.3 hours at 30 FPS for full-screen refreshes. Tests revealed that smart refreshes, which update only changed pixels, significantly extend this duration.

After displaying 2.16 million frames of repeating patterns, some ghosting appeared but disappeared after additional refreshes. No permanent damage was detected within the test limits, though the manufacturer explicitly advises against such use, excluding warranty coverage in case of damage.
