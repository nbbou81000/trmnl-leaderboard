A Solar-Powered Robot Pet Built Without a Microcontroller or Code

An analog robot inspired by animals moves using solar power and simple circuits.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

ALTco has built a tiny solar-powered robot that moves like a living creature without relying on a microcontroller or any code, drawing inspiration from BEAM robotics. This project mimics organic behavior using purely analog circuits, avoiding programmable components.

The robot features two N20 gear motors driving custom 3D-printed wheels fitted with rubber O-rings for traction. Two 74HC240 inverter chips, selected for their minimal standby power consumption, serve as low-power motor drivers. Power comes from a solar cell mounted on a brass frame, with a Miller solar engine storing energy in a capacitor to eliminate the need for a battery.

The chassis is designed for easy servicing, splitting into two sections: the top holds the electronics and solar panel, while the bottom contains the motors and sensors. JST connectors simplify disassembly, and spring-loaded contacts allow the solar panel to slide out. Sockets for resistors and capacitors enable experimentation with different component values.

Two photodiodes act as the robot’s eyes, detecting light variations. Their signals influence a pair of coupled oscillators: one controls motor activity, while the other adjusts speed and direction based on lighting conditions. Under bright light, the robot moves toward the source. In dim or uneven lighting, the oscillators drift out of phase, producing unpredictable movements reminiscent of a small animal exploring its surroundings.
