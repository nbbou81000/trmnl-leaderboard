TAKTO ONE: An Open-Source Hand Exoskeleton

A robotics student builds an open-source hand exoskeleton that can move, enhance, and record finger movements.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Sebastian Molano, a robotics and embedded systems student at Anhalt University of Applied Sciences in Germany, took on an ambitious challenge for his master’s thesis: designing a fully open-source hand exoskeleton. Named TAKTO ONE, the device is a compact and stylish wearable bracer that uses motors and cable "tendons" to actuate finger joints.

The current prototype limits actuation to some fingers, but the final version aims to control all four fingers and the thumb, totaling ten motorized joints. Each finger features two actuated joints driven by Dynamixel XC330 servomotors, controlled by a Teensy 4.1 board via a dedicated bus. Additional sensors include IMUs (Inertial Measurement Units) and a MyoWare 2.0 EEG system to detect muscle activity. A built-in round LCD displays system status and real-time data.

The project goes beyond hardware: Molano developed a full software suite, including a virtual reality digital twin simulation and multiple control schemes. The exoskeleton can assist finger movements, compensating for lack of strength or dexterity. A standout feature allows recording and playback of movement sequences. This could aid in learning technical gestures, such as playing the piano, by mimicking an expert’s motions to build muscle memory.

Available in three colors, TAKTO ONE is entirely open source. Construction files are accessible on GitHub, though the device is not sold as a finished product.
