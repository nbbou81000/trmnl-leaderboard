Turning PET Bottles into PETG Filament for 3D Printing

A maker calculated the number of PET bottles required to produce 1 kg of recycled PETG filament, a lengthy and labor-intensive process.

In countries without a bottle deposit system, PET bottles pile up as waste. A YouTube channel focused on recycling, PETCraft, has turned them into PETG filament for 3D printing. The process, detailed in a recent video, begins with heat-smoothing the bottles to make them easier to cut into thin strips. These strips are then fed through a homemade extruder—built from salvaged parts—before being wound onto spools.

According to PETCraft, it took thirteen 5-liter bottles, one 10-liter, two 8-liter, and a single 1.5-liter bottle to produce 1 kg of filament. A time-consuming and labor-intensive task, especially since the recycled material’s quality can vary based on bottle thickness, transparency, or color. Commentators note that commercial PETG offers a stable composition, while recycled bottles may introduce inconsistencies like bubbles or weaker layer adhesion during printing.

Despite these challenges, the maker plans to use the filament with a DIY 3D printer assembled from salvaged parts and controlled by an Arduino board. While the process saves between $10 and $20 per kilogram compared to store-bought filament, its real-world feasibility remains questionable given the effort involved.
