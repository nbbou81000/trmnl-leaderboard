A Lightbulb-Shaped Computer for Spatial and Ambient Computing

A design project envisions a bulb-shaped computer that projects interfaces onto walls and surfaces.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A design project called Lightbulb Computer proposes a bulb-shaped alternative to smart glasses and headsets. The concept merges a projector with computer vision, allowing installation in standard Edison sockets on walls, ceilings, or portable lamp bases. The system responds to voice commands, tracks pointing gestures, analyzes its surroundings, and projects content onto real surfaces.

Demonstrations include recipe widgets or timers displayed on a kitchen counter, an interactive wall-sized map that two people can pan and scroll together, photos transferred from a smartphone, a family checklist in a hallway, or smart home controls activated by pointing directly at a device. The videos shown come from a functional but bulky prototype, with no editing or AI-generated content.

The designer notes that consumer hardware isn’t quite ready yet, but projectors are becoming brighter and smaller, while computer vision models are fast enough to scan a bookshelf in milliseconds. Privacy would be handled through on-device processing, a physical indicator light, and a mechanical camera cover.
