Hackaday Supercon 2026: 20 Must-See Talks

Hackaday Supercon 2026 reveals 20 technical talks covering software-defined radio, open-source rovers, and hardware hacks.

The Hackaday Supercon 2026, taking place in just over a week, has now revealed its full lineup of 20 technical talks. The topics range from software-defined radio to open-source rovers and hardware hacks, catering to electronics and DIY enthusiasts.

Diverse topics and hands-on projects

One talk dives into software-defined radio (SDR), exploring how affordable RF chips like the TI SimpleLink Wireless MCU can be repurposed. Speakers will explain how they reverse-engineered the chip’s RF subsystem to unlock undocumented modes, enabling reception of AM, FM, and raw IQ samples. Another project, the Charlie SAO module, will demonstrate real-time reception of analog airband and digital signals.

The Open Source Rover community will share insights on the practical and philosophical challenges of maintaining an open-source robotics project. Another session will focus on optimizing PCB enclosures by integrating 3D-printed designs with FDM techniques for better precision.

Security, interfaces, and fabrication

A talk will dissect bank dye pack systems, explaining the hidden electronics in stacks of cash and remote detonation triggers. Another presentation will examine the constraints of embedded user interfaces, particularly on Raspberry Pi, where hardware-driven sensor inputs replace traditional server-based architectures.

Additional talks will cover PCB fabrication using fiber lasers, as well as tactile human-machine interfaces (HMI), with live demonstrations of custom-built prototypes.
