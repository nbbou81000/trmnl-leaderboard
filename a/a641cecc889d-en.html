Custom RISC-V Processor Powers Retro Gaming System in 2026

A DIY retro gaming system leverages a custom RISC-V CPU on an FPGA, showcasing open-source flexibility for vintage emulation.

The RISC-V open-source architecture is gradually replacing proprietary solutions like ARM or x86, even in microcontrollers. [M_Jagadeesh97] is getting ahead of the trend by building a retro gaming system around a custom-designed RISC-V processor.

Without access to chip fabrication tools, the creator uses a Terasic DE0-Nano FPGA board, an affordable and hobbyist-friendly platform. The system includes 32 MB of RAM and boots to a launcher with 32 preloaded games. To address hardware limitations, the display resolution is capped at 64×32 pixels, preserving the original aspect ratios of games like DOOM while achieving 45 frames per second.

The technical write-up covers every aspect of the project: bare-metal CPU design, video signal timing, and the implementation of a Wolfenstein-style raycaster. Paired with a small display, the setup remains compact and portable, demonstrating how powerful inexpensive FPGA boards can be with the right effort.
