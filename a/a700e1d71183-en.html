ESP32-C3 Transmits DVB-S2 Video Without a Satellite, But Not at 12 GHz

A project leverages the ESP32-C3’s RF transmitter to broadcast DVB-S2 video, though frequency remains capped at 2.4 GHz.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The ESP32-DATV project by [SP8ESA] uses the ESP32-C3’s built-in radio module to transmit video in DVB-S2 format, a standard typically reserved for satellite television. Unlike traditional setups requiring an orbiting satellite, this method enables direct broadcasting from a microcontroller without additional hardware.

A PC-side application converts the input video signal into a DVB-S2-compliant stream. The ESP32-C3 then processes the samples and transmits them via its RF block. While this simplifies the setup, hardware limitations apply: the DVB-S2 frequency range (10.7 to 12.7 GHz) far exceeds the ESP32-C3’s 2.4 GHz cap for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.

Lower frequencies (down to 100 MHz) might be achievable with hardware tweaks, but higher bands would require a complete RF block redesign. Some users speculate that transmissions at 5.5 GHz could unintentionally produce harmonics at 11 GHz, detectable by standard LNBs, though this remains unconfirmed.
