Maker Faire Rome 2026: Europe’s Premier Maker Event

Three days of creativity and open innovation in Rome, featuring robots, 3D printing, and open-source projects.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Maker Faire Rome 2026, held from October 23 to 25 at the Gazometro, stands as Europe’s largest maker event after the Bay Area edition. Supported by the Italian government, the fair highlighted open innovation, digital fabrication, and themes like circular economy and sustainability.

Standout projects and innovations

Among the featured projects, the B-Shell, a flat-pack, bistable gridshell developed by the Visual Computing Lab, drew attention. Designed for easy transport, it could even have space applications. Another notable innovation was research on optimal light spectra for plant grow lights, presented by Italy’s National Research Council.

The event also showcased quirky projects like a miniature Geiger counter (Doze) or robotic lamps combining 3D printing and mechanics. Robots like StackChan from M5Stack and the open-source Aelox 5-axis 3D printer, still in development, impressed visitors. In digital fabrication, InMachines unveiled the Piccola, an open-source desktop CNC mill, while their OpenLab Starter Kit now includes a laser-sintered metal printer.

Agriculture and food were also highlighted with projects like GrainLab, a micro-maltery for sprouting grains, and Robby Minichef, a culinary robot capable of boiling and draining pasta precisely. Sustainability was represented by plastic recycling initiatives, such as those by Davide Venturini of Precious Plastics Romagna, who redesigned DIY machines for shredding and molding.

Finally, prototypes like the Mentime Drink Machine, a beverage machine slated for 2027, and the Orion PDA, an offline handheld computer with a sunlight-readable display, showcased the diversity of possible applications.
