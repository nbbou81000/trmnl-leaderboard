Birduino: Arduino-Powered Bird Call Player with NFC Cards

An Arduino-based device plays bird calls on demand using NFC-enabled cards. Build details and tips included.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The Birduino, built by Hannah Robertson, is an Arduino-powered device that plays bird calls when an NFC card is tapped. It combines an Arduino Nano, an RC522 RFID reader module, and an MP3 player module to handle audio playback. The cards are standard playing cards featuring North American bird illustrations, modified with NFC stickers cut from an online roll. While the RC522 reader isn’t fully NFC-compatible, it successfully reads the sticker IDs.

Each card triggers the playback of a specific bird call, sourced from the Cornell Guide to Bird Sounds of the United States and Canada (20 USD), which includes vocalizations for 908 species. The project required manual work to link each NFC ID to its corresponding sound, but the outcome is functional. The enclosure, made of laser-cut clear acrylic, is assembled using Clear Gorilla Glue Gel to prevent clouding at the edges.

The creator shares practical advice: using pre-built modules simplifies wiring and allows leveraging existing libraries. She also warns about Gorilla Glue product names, as similar labels can hide different formulas.
