SmallTV Hacked Without Firmware or Soldering

A SmallTV display repurposed for custom visuals using its built-in photo viewer, no firmware hack or soldering required.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The SmallTV displays from GeekMagic, powered by an ESP8266 or ESP32 microcontroller and featuring a compact screen, are marketed for displaying time or weather. Their professionally made plastic enclosures sometimes make them look like finished products, though they likely draw inspiration from existing DIY projects. Until now, repurposing them usually required replacing the stock firmware—a process often complex.

A new method, shared by Yongha Kim, bypasses this step by leveraging the SmallTV’s built-in photo viewer function. The screen receives a remotely generated image over HTTP, without any hardware or software modification. The process relies on a local server (PC, Raspberry Pi, etc.) that produces a 240×240 pixel image, sent to the SmallTV. The Python code used here relies on the Pillow library to create dynamic visuals, such as AI tool usage data, but the system accepts any type of content.

The screen also supports animated GIFs, allowing for the integration of motion. The possibilities are nearly endless: displaying personal data, real-time information, or even artistic works. The simplicity of this method opens the door to various projects, like a presence or availability status display, without requiring advanced electronics skills.

For those eager to dive deeper, it remains possible to fully replace the SmallTV’s firmware, offering complete control over the device’s behavior.
