Doom Runs on a $30 Clamp Meter

A developer runs Doom on a basic digital clamp meter, proving that a measuring device can double as a retro console.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The 1990s game Doom has been ported to many unexpected devices, from calculators to printers. Now, Artur Bieniek has added a digital clamp meter to that list: the FNIRSI DMC-100, priced around $30. Designed for measuring current, voltage, and resistance, this tool seems far from a gaming console. Yet it includes key embedded system components: an LCD screen, a microcontroller, buttons, internal flash storage, and a USB-C port.

The main challenge was the device’s limited memory—just 32 KB of RAM, compared to megabytes in the original PCs. Despite this, Bieniek managed to run Doom natively on the meter without relying on another computer to stream the display. The process involves putting the meter into firmware update mode via USB-C, flashing custom Doom firmware, and transferring the necessary files. After verification, Doom can be launched directly on the device.

The built-in controls are functional but awkward: Bieniek uses the meter’s front-panel buttons, with the flashlight button serving as the trigger. To improve playability, he connected a DJI drone controller via USB-C, enabling smoother directional controls. The game’s audio is played through the meter’s piezoelectric buzzer, producing a high-pitched, distorted sound. Despite these limitations, Doom runs well on the meter’s small screen, showcasing the creative potential of repurposing basic embedded hardware.
