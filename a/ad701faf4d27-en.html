Everyday Objects Turned into Tamagotchi-Style Companions Using AI

Researchers turn ordinary objects into expressive virtual companions using e-ink displays and AI-generated characters reflecting their state.

Traditional smart devices often rely on raw data displays or notifications, but researchers at Carnegie Mellon University are exploring a more engaging alternative. Their Object-Agent Twins (OATs) system gives everyday objects personalities through AI-generated animated characters. Inspired by virtual pets like Tamagotchi, these avatars reflect the object’s state using built-in sensors and emotional expressions.

Each OAT is built around an ESP32-S3 development board paired with a 1.54-inch two-color e-ink display. The device includes a temperature and humidity sensor, a microphone, and an expansion board for additional modules like motion detection, occupancy, light intensity, or soil moisture. A 1,100 mAh lithium-polymer battery provides roughly one week of runtime. The 3D-printed enclosure and electronics cost less than $20 to assemble, with optional sensors priced under $5 each.

Users configure their OATs via a web interface by describing the object, selecting relevant sensors, and defining desired or undesirable conditions. Google’s Gemini AI then generates a pixel-art character and its behaviors, complete with 12 emotional expressions. Every two seconds, the ESP32-S3 checks sensor data and only communicates with the cloud when an anomaly is detected, optimizing power consumption. The server evaluates the information and sends back the appropriate emotion, displayed on the e-ink screen.

In a study with 12 participants, OATs were created for objects like refrigerators, plants, dumbbells, and coffee makers. Results suggest this approach influences interactions: some users adjusted their routines to keep their objects "happy," while others felt guilty when their objects appeared unhappy. However, the system has limitations, including occasionally ambiguous emotional expressions and users manipulating sensor data to please their characters.
