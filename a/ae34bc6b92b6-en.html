A $5 HDMI adapter fixes display issues in headless homelabs

In a headless homelab (without a display), some systems behave differently when disconnected from a monitor. Resolutions vanish, remote desktop sessions glitch, and certain GPUs refuse to enable features without detecting a display. A $5 HDMI dummy plug solves these issues by faking a connected monitor.

The concept is straightforward: the adapter feeds the system display capabilities (resolution, refresh rate, etc.) as a real monitor would. This prevents the GPU driver from disabling useful options. NVIDIA cards are particularly prone to these quirks, though AMD and Intel may also be affected depending on firmware settings.

Two adapter types are commonly used: HDMI and DisplayPort. The choice depends on the available port. DisplayPort adapters are ideal for high-resolution or high-refresh setups, as they allow specifying parameters like 1440p at 144 Hz or 4K at high refresh rates. Cheap adapters may report incomplete or incorrect capabilities, degrading remote desktop performance. Always check specifications before purchasing.

Premium adapters can also simulate audio or HDR support, letting the system expose these features even without a physical monitor. Once plugged in, the adapter requires no additional drivers or software tweaks—it works silently in the background, fixing the issue effortlessly.
