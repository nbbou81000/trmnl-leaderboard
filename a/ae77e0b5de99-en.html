Vintage CPU Replacements with tranZPUter: Open-Source Upgrades for 6809 and More

An open-source project offers modern replacements for vintage CPUs using FPGAs or MCUs, adding network and virtual storage options.

The tranZPUter project, led by Philip Smart, provides modern replacements for obsolete microprocessors (MPUs) using FPGAs or microcontrollers. The goal is to keep vintage systems functional without relying on unobtainable components.

One of its variants, the Pico6809, targets the Motorola 6809, an 8-bit MPU released in 1978. While less common than the Z80 or 6502, the 6809 featured 16-bit capabilities and outperformed the 6502 in several benchmarks. Its higher cost limited its use to systems like the TRS-80 Color, Dragon 32/64, and arcade machines.

The Pico6809 board uses an RP2350B microcontroller, leveraging its PIO (Programmable I/O) peripheral to emulate the 6809. It also integrates an ESP32 as a co-processor, adding features like virtual floppy and cassette drives, as well as optional network connectivity. Currently, the board is optimized for Dragon 32/64 systems, with potential adaptations for other platforms in the future.

This project aligns with a broader movement to preserve vintage computing by enabling enthusiasts to extend the lifespan of their machines without sourcing original, often unavailable parts.
