MicroManipulatorStepper: Open-Source Nanometer-Precision Motion Platform

An open-source 3D manipulator achieves 50 nm resolution using mechanical and electronic optimizations.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Developer 0x23 has released an open-source motorized 3D manipulator on GitHub, tailored for high-precision tasks such as optical alignment, electronic component probing, and microscopy. Its parallel kinematic structure, featuring miniature ball joints, ensures high stiffness and a wide range of motion.

The system uses off-the-shelf stepper motors driven by a 30 kHz closed-loop controller. A magnetic gearing technique boosts the resolution of low-cost magnetic rotary encoders by 30 times, achieving steps as small as 50 nanometers. The creator notes that resolution does not equate to accuracy, with absolute accuracy remaining significantly lower.

Control is handled via G-Code over USB serial, making integration into other projects straightforward. The firmware, written in C++, includes look-ahead path planning and 6DOF pose interpolation. A Python API and a GUI with a live camera feed are provided. CAD files are available in FreeCAD, electronics in KiCAD, and the board uses only through-hole components, simplifying assembly.

Hardware version 4.0 introduces revisions to the ball joints and linkage manufacturing, while firmware 1.0.4 adds PWM tool outputs on two GPIO pins for applications like laser engraving.
