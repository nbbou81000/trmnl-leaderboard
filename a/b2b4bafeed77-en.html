SoundBubble: A Virtual Microphone That Tracks Your Fingertips

Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University have developed a system that captures finger interactions without physical sensors.

Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University have developed SoundBubble, a system that turns smart glasses or an augmented reality headset into a virtual microphone. The technology uses an array of microphones embedded in the device to capture and amplify sounds produced by the user’s fingertips without requiring physical sensors on the hand.

The system works by measuring the tiny time differences in sound arrival between microphones. With the headset’s cameras tracking finger positions in 3D space, SoundBubble calculates these delays and synchronizes the audio streams to isolate sounds from the fingertip. Unrelated background noise, which doesn’t align, is suppressed. This allows the system to detect subtle interactions like taps on a surface, micro-gestures between fingers, or the use of passive objects such as buttons, knobs, and sliders.

In tests with 10 participants, SoundBubble achieved an overall accuracy of 94.7% across three applications: contact detection (97.5%), interaction with passive widgets (97.1%), and activity recognition with handheld objects (89.4%). The system also performed well in noisy environments, such as cafes or with music playing, isolating target sounds up to 20 dB below ambient noise levels.

Simulations suggest the technology remains effective even with fewer microphones. An eight-microphone setup, suitable for AR glasses, maintained performance close to a 16-microphone array, while reducing the array to four microphones led to a more noticeable drop in accuracy. This approach could simplify interactions with augmented reality interfaces by eliminating the need for additional sensors on objects or hands.
