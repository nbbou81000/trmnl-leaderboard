DOOM Runs on a $30 Clamp Meter

A DOOM port on a cheap clamp meter demonstrates retrocompatibility knows no bounds.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The classic game DOOM, known for its ports on unlikely devices, now runs on a low-end clamp meter. The chosen device, the FNIRSI DMC-100, costs around $30, a fraction of the price of 1990s computers. It features an Artery AT32F415 microcontroller running at 150 MHz but limited to just 32 KB of RAM, a significant challenge for the port.

Despite this constraint, [Artur Bieniek] successfully adapted the doomgeneric engine to this hardware. The Freedoom: Phase 1 game, fully compatible with DOOM, runs entirely, including levels E1M1 to E1M9, intermission screens, the menu, and four save slots. Color graphics are displayed on the meter’s 320 x 240 screen, and sound effects are generated via an integrated buzzer. Controls are mapped to a DJI RC-N1 controller, an unconventional choice for such a project.

This port highlights the ingenuity of developers when faced with extreme hardware limitations. It joins the long list of unusual devices that have hosted DOOM, from desk phones to custom CPUs.
