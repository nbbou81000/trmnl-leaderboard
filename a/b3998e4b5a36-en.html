Drone Detection via Magnetic Fields: A Passive, Low-Cost Solution

Researchers use drones' motor magnetic fields to identify them without emitting detectable signals.

Drones offer many benefits, but their use for surveillance raises serious privacy concerns. Detecting these small aircraft is challenging, especially when they’re too tiny for reliable radar tracking. Researchers at Florida International University have developed a novel approach that identifies drones by analyzing the magnetic fields generated by their electric motors—without emitting any detectable signals that could reveal the detector’s location.

Most small drones rely on brushless DC motors, which unavoidably produce extremely low frequency (ELF) magnetic fields. Each drone model has a unique “magnetic fingerprint” based on its motor configuration. To capture these faint signals, the team built a sensitive circular loop antenna paired with a low-noise analog front end, which amplifies and conditions the signals for processing. Unlike active systems, this method is passive, meaning it doesn’t transmit anything an adversary could use to locate it.

The identification process uses a signal processing technique called spectral correlation function (SCF) estimation. This method analyzes repeating patterns in the magnetic emissions, generating maps that highlight motor and controller characteristics. These maps are then fed into a convolutional neural network (CNN) based on the VGG16 architecture, trained to recognize the magnetic signatures of different drones. In outdoor tests with three commercial micro-drones at distances up to 15 meters, the system achieved a detection accuracy of 98.52%, with correct drone model identification in 83.5% of cases. Smaller drones, with weaker magnetic emissions, proved harder to identify accurately.

The prototype, developed for a few hundred dollars, could be deployed in networks around sensitive areas. While its limited 15-meter range prevents it from replacing radar, it offers an inexpensive, discreet additional layer of protection. The team is now working to enhance the system’s performance further.
