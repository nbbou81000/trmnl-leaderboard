DOOM Rendered as Vector Graphics on Vectrex via Modern Multicart

A modder runs DOOM on the 1982 Vectrex console by leveraging a USB multicart.

DOOM, the 1993 first-person shooter, has been adapted to display as vector graphics on the Vectrex, a vector-based console released by Milton Bradley in 1982. The demo video shows the game’s levels rendered as animated vector lines instead of pixels. The project was created by modder Jason Kopp, known as ArcadeJason, who used the UVMC2, a USB multicart designed for the Vectrex.

Unlike a native port, the game does not run directly on the Vectrex hardware. The UVMC2 acts as an external mini-PC, processing the data and sending the output to the console’s screen. Audio is output via a 3.5mm jack. The DOOM build relies on an SDK written by another modder, Daniel Ferrer Guerrero.

This demonstration offers a glimpse into a vector-first branch of gaming history, reminiscent of titles like Tempest, Battlezone, and the 1983 Star Wars arcade cabinet. The video is available online, and additional details can be found on Time Extension’s website.
