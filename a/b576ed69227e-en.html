Understanding DC Motor Principles in 3D

A visual tutorial explains how a DC motor works using a 3D simulation and permanent magnets.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A project on Hackster.io by electroniclovers breaks down how a DC motor converts electrical current into rotational motion using magnets and a copper loop, with a 3D simulation to visualize the process.

The setup starts with two permanent magnets facing each other with opposite poles, creating a steady magnetic field. When current flows through a copper rod, it generates its own magnetic field. Reversing the current flips this field, causing the rod to move in one direction when placed between the magnet poles. Bending two rods into a rectangular loop on a shaft makes the sides push in opposite directions, resulting in rotation. However, at 90 degrees, torque drops to zero, halting movement.

To keep the loop spinning, a split-ring commutator with carbon brushes reverses the current every half turn, ensuring continuous force in the same direction. Adding a second coil at a 90-degree offset eliminates dead spots where torque vanishes.

The project uses 3D simulation tools like Blender, Unity, or a WebGL viewer to dynamically illustrate these steps. The full tutorial is available online for further exploration.
