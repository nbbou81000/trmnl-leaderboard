A $5 toy camera runs DOOM after firmware hack

A cheap toy camera gets DOOM ported via firmware hack, unlocking hidden capabilities.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A dirt-cheap toy camera has been turned into a DOOM-playing console by [Spode], who cracked open its firmware to unlock hidden potential. The device runs on an HX3302B SoC, a chip partially reverse-engineered by [SpriteTM], making the hack less of a shot in the dark.

The key vulnerability was the camera’s easily updatable firmware, which allowed new code to be flashed without much resistance. To analyze the hardware, [Spode] extracted its memory and embedded it into the EXIF data of captured photos—a clever trick that provided deep insights into the device’s inner workings. Performance benchmarks revealed the camera to be surprisingly capable, easily handling a 1993-era game like DOOM.

Memory constraints were overcome by keeping only the most frequently accessed parts of the game in RAM, while the rest stayed in flash storage. A custom audio synthesizer was added to recreate the iconic soundtrack, and the controls were adapted to the camera’s physical layout, turning it into a playable—if rudimentary—DOOM console. Surprisingly, it performs better as a game device than as a camera.
