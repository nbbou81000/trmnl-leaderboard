Battery Pal: A Smart Power Module for DIY Projects

PN Labs launches an all-in-one module to charge, monitor, and regulate batteries for electronic projects, inspired by smartphones.

PN Labs has launched Battery Pal, a compact, ready-to-use module that combines three essential functions for powering electronic projects: charging, monitoring, and regulation. According to Ethan Johnston, cofounder of PN Labs, the goal is to bring the smart battery management found in smartphones to a format accessible to hobbyists and small businesses.

The module supports multiple power sources: USB Type-C Power Delivery (PD), a 4.5–13.5 VDC power supply, or even a small solar panel. It provides a user-selectable regulated output of either 3.3 V or 5.1 V at 1.5–2 A. Battery charging current can be configured between 0.5 A and 3 A, with an integrated protection system managing voltage and temperature thresholds. A temperature probe is included to monitor the battery in real time. The module also features a fuel gauge accessible via I2C or Qwiic protocols, and a web-based dashboard can be set up using a Raspberry Pi RP2040 as a USB-to-serial bridge.

PN Labs also offers a stripped-down version called Battery Pal Lite, which reduces features for a lower price. This version omits the fuel gauge and 3.3 V output, limits the output current to 1 A and charging current to 0.2–2 A, and restricts input to a fixed 5 V USB Type-C or a 5.5 VDC power supply. The protection system is simplified with fixed thresholds and basic thermal cutoff. The Lite version is priced at $12.99, compared to $23.99 for the full version.

Early units are available for pre-order on Kickstarter, with rewards starting at CA$14 for the Lite version and CA$21 for early backers of the full version. Shipments are expected in January 2027. PN Labs states it has already produced 120 boards ready for dispatch and sells seven other hardware designs.
