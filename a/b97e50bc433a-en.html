The Forgotten Origins of Computer Pointing Devices

A journey through the early days of computer pointing devices, long before the PC era.

The history of computer pointing devices, such as mice, stretches back much further than most realize. At the Vintage Computer Festival Berlin, Dmitriy Kostiuk delivered a deep dive into their evolution, tracing their development from early prototypes to the 1990s, when these tools became widely available.

The first known example dates back to the 1950s, with a trackball designed for a Canadian warship. Unlike common assumptions, this system already featured a modern-looking optical encoder. In 1966, Douglas Engelbart unveiled his iconic wooden prototype, equipped with wheels and potentiometers. However, he wasn’t alone: that same year, Telefunken introduced a sleeker, lesser-known competitor.

The lecture then examines the various iterations that shaped the modern mouse’s form factor. For instance, mice with patterned mouse pads, often linked to Sun Microsystems, were actually produced by Mouse Systems. The first mice from Microsoft and Apple are also highlighted, alongside clunky designs for 1980s 8-bit computers. These commercial flops underscore how long it took for pointing devices to gain mainstream acceptance.
