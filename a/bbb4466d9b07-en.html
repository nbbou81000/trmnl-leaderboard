Hack Your Tamagotchi Paradise with a DIY UART Adapter

A maker bypasses Tamagotchi Paradise’s limits with a DIY USB-to-serial adapter, enabling customization without altering the original device.

The Tamagotchi Paradise, the latest iteration of the classic virtual pet, remains constrained by its manufacturer’s choices. A maker using the handle Squalius has developed a workaround: a DIY USB-to-serial adapter that connects the device to a computer, unlocking new possibilities without altering the original hardware.

The adapter uses a CH340G module, a common and affordable component for serial communication between microcontrollers and computers. A 3D-printed enclosure, available in two designs (one without heat-set inserts), holds three metal contacts aligned with the Tamagotchi’s existing connection interface. These contacts, made from nickel strips soldered to wires, enable data transmission. Squalius recommends printing the enclosure in PETG rather than PLA, as the latter is too brittle for this purpose.

After assembly, the CH340G must be configured for 3.3 V operation to avoid damaging the Tamagotchi. A preliminary test of the connections is advised before finalizing the build. Users then access a browser-based serial interface (compatible with Chrome) to send custom gifts, foods, or other items directly to their virtual pet. The adapter can also simulate connections between multiple Tamagotchi Paradise devices, unlocking features that normally require additional units. Save files can be backed up to a computer to preserve progress or experiment safely.

For more adventurous tinkerers, ROM modifications open the door to custom sprites, mini-games, and even emulators, turning the closed virtual pet into a versatile platform for hardware and software hacking.
