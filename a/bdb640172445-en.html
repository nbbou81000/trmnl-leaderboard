Raspberry Pi Monitors Energy Use with Its Own Boards and Home Assistant

The manufacturer uses its SBCs and Home Assistant to analyze and optimize energy consumption in its quest for carbon neutrality.

Raspberry Pi has released an update on its progress toward carbon neutrality goals, following the installation of solar panels and a push to encourage employees to switch to electric vehicles. To measure and reduce its energy consumption, the company is leveraging its own single-board computers (SBCs) paired with Home Assistant, an open-source home automation platform.

The team deployed Raspberry Pi 4 and 5 boards to monitor every electrical circuit in the building using Emporia current-sensing clamps. This setup provides detailed snapshots of energy consumption in kilowatt-hours (kWh) for each device. Additionally, an outdated building energy management system (BMS) was replaced with a homegrown solution based on BASview and BASpi, two open-source tools running on Raspberry Pi hardware. The replacement, estimated at £7,800 compared to £150,000 for a comparable proprietary system, offers improved visibility into temperature, air quality, and energy data.

Enhancements include CO₂-based ventilation control, solar integration, and weather compensation. Raspberry Pi also plans to expand its solar capacity with a 219 kW carport installation. More details are available in a dedicated blog post.
