Xerxes Pi 2026 and Nano: Carrier Boards for Raspberry Pi and Jetson Modules

Rapid Analysis unveils two carrier boards for Raspberry Pi and NVIDIA Jetson modules, featuring NVMe storage, PoE, and OCuLink.

Rapid Analysis, which previously launched the Xerxes Pi in 2025, introduces two new carrier boards designed for home labs and edge AI applications. The Xerxes Pi 2026 and Xerxes Nano target Raspberry Pi CM4/CM5 modules and NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano or Turing RK1 boards, respectively.

The Xerxes Pi 2026 features an M.2 M-key slot for NVMe storage, a Gigabit Ethernet port with optional PoE+, and dedicated debug and boot jumper pins for headless setups. An I2S Qwiic connector enables digital audio peripherals, while a mezzanine connector exposes CSI and HDMI signals. Measuring 120 x 40 mm, the board supports active cooling and vertical 1U rack installation.

The larger Xerxes Nano (120 x 60 mm) is built for demanding AI workloads. It includes an OCuLink port to connect external GPUs or PCIe devices, alongside a Gigabit Ethernet port with an SFP+ cage. Three PCIe Gen 3 lanes are available via an M.2 slot beneath the module. Additional interfaces include SPI, UART, I²C, and I²S. Both boards support active cooling and power via USB Type-C or PoE.

Rapid Analysis offers open-source tools, including a macOS management app (with Windows and Linux versions planned) to monitor system status, manage Docker containers, and handle SSH keys. KiCad hardware design files for the Xerxes Pi 2026 are also available. 3D-printable enclosures and racks (1U, 6-slot, 12-slot) are provided for physical integration.

A companion module, the Xerxes Compute Module X, is presented as a lightweight alternative to the Raspberry Pi CM5. It integrates a Rockchip RK3566 processor with a 1 TOPS NPU and 8GB of RAM. While open-source, technical details remain confidential until the Kickstarter campaign concludes.
