Programmable Electronic Badges for Canada’s Largest Hackathon

Canada’s largest hackathon replaces paper badges with 1,750 programmable electronic badges featuring an ESP32.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

For the 2026 edition of Hack the North, Canada’s largest hackathon, organizers replaced traditional paper badges with fully programmable electronic badges. Each badge features an ESP32 microcontroller, a color screen, navigation buttons, an NFC reader, a WS2812B LED matrix, and power from two AA batteries. An online interface allows writing applications in Lua, while an embedded shell provides direct system access.

Development required four manufacturing iterations at JLCPCB. Several issues arose: a 10 µF capacitor placed where a 10 µH inductor should be, a screen ribbon deemed too dense for soldering, an NFC component delivered as a tag rather than a reader, and reversed batteries in 50 pre-series units causing shorts and destroying two chips. Of the first 12 tested badges, 10 were unusable. An untested reverse-polarity protection FET was later added to the production run without prior full-board testing.

A total of 1,750 assembled badges and 750 blank ones were produced at a cost of $20,300. The unit price per functional badge reached about $11.60, compared to an initial estimate of $5. Participants used the badges to create projects like a mesh-networked version of Among Us, an interactive Pokédex of attendees, and controllers for Tux Racer. All hardware designs, firmware, and a WebAssembly emulator are released as open source.
