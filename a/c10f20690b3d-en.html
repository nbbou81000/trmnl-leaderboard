A Satellite Tracker Turns Overflights into Ambient Music

A website turns real-time satellite overflights into musical notes, generating a soundscape based on their orbits.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The OrbitalWiki project offers a unique way to visualize and listen to real-time space traffic. Based on your approximate location, the site identifies satellites currently orbiting above you from a database of nearly 17,000 tracked objects. Each time a satellite reaches its highest point in the sky from your perspective, a musical note is played.

The instrument and octave for each note are determined by the satellite’s orbital parameters, giving each overflight a distinct sound. The result is a slow, ambient soundscape that remains surprisingly relaxing despite the objects’ incredible speeds. The site also allows users to speed up the timescale for a more energetic sequence.

This project aligns with broader artistic initiatives, such as Sam Scorsby’s Impermanence Series A, which stitches together snippets of global radio broadcasts into ephemeral songs.
