Self-sufficient Mobile Weather Station: A Raspberry Pi Zero W Solar-Powered Project

A student builds a portable, self-powered, modular weather station using a Raspberry Pi Zero W and environmental sensors.

A student project has resulted in a portable, self-powered weather station powered by a solar panel and a battery. Designed for transportability, it measures temperature, humidity, wind speed (via an anemometer), and light intensity. The system’s core is a Raspberry Pi Zero W, selected for its low cost, minimal power consumption, and built-in networking capabilities, enabling smooth data collection and transfer to an external server.

The enclosure, rated IP65 for dust and water resistance, required multiple iterations to perfect its sealing and weight. Electronic components, protected by gaskets and cable grommets, underwent rigorous soldering tests before final assembly. Once the Raspberry Pi was installed, the system’s startup was signaled by a blinking blue LED, confirming the code was running correctly.

Tested in real-world conditions on the Gower Peninsula (Wales), the station revealed a few limitations: the anemometer stalled at low wind speeds due to bearing friction, later resolved by lubrication. The temperature sensor consistently overestimated readings by 1–2°C, likely due to internal heat buildup from electrical resistance with no escape path. Despite these issues, the station successfully established a local web server and stored collected data.

The creator plans improvements for future iterations: addressing the temperature sensor’s heat trap, simplifying sampling rate adjustments, and refining the station’s modular design. The ultimate goal is to bring the project to market for public use.

The project is featured in Issue 171 of the Raspberry Pi Official Magazine, available as a free PDF or in print (with a free Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W for 6- or 12-month print subscribers).
