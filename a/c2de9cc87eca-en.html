A "cottagecore" cyberdeck for autonomous fieldwork and nature observation

A rustic and portable cyberdeck, inspired by cottagecore aesthetics, eases field data collection with digital tools and local AI.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Jen Looper, a creative technologist with a love for nature and gardens, has built a cyberdeck called B.L.O.O.M. (Bridging Local Observations, Openly Mapped) to observe and document local wildlife and flora. The project aligns with the cottagecore aesthetic, blending rustic charm, sustainability, and "slow living."

Unlike typical cyberdecks, which often feature bulky, futuristic designs, B.L.O.O.M. takes a minimalist and practical approach: its enclosure is a train case, a traditional container for toiletries, providing ample space for paper notebooks alongside electronic components. Inside, it houses an Arduino® UNO™Q (4GB), a 5" HDMI screen, a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, a power bank, a USB camera, and a USB microphone.

The system is designed for field data collection and structured note-taking, compatible with methods used by naturalists like the Grinnell system. A companion app, Field Notes, streamlines this process. Additionally, the UNO Q can run local AI models to analyze captured sounds or images directly. For instance, Looper trained a model using Edge Impulse in the Arduino App Lab to identify bird species from their songs.

This project proves that modern digital tools and an eco-conscious approach can coexist in a portable, budget-friendly format.
