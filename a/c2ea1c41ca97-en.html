Geekworm Unveils Supercapacitor UPS HAT for Raspberry Pi 5

A supercapacitor-based HAT shields the Raspberry Pi 5 from sudden power losses, enabling clean shutdowns.

A sudden power loss on a Raspberry Pi 5 can corrupt an SD card or interrupt ongoing writes, especially when running critical tasks like a Home Assistant server, a 3D printer controller, or an edge AI application. To prevent these issues, Geekworm has introduced a supercapacitor-based UPS HAT designed specifically for the Pi 5. Unlike traditional lithium battery solutions, this module uses supercapacitors to provide backup power for a few seconds to several minutes, depending on the load.

Three versions are available, with capacities of 22F, 60F, or 100F. The 100F model, the most powerful, can power a fully loaded Pi 5 at 5A for about nine seconds, while a lighter workload extends runtime to over three minutes. The module handles the Pi 5’s proprietary power profile (5V/5A) via an integrated PD controller, avoiding low-voltage warnings or CPU throttling that generic power supplies often trigger. It accepts input voltages between 9V and 24V via a barrel jack or 12V via USB-C PD, making it compatible with a wide range of sources, from USB-C chargers to industrial power supplies.

Mounted beneath the Raspberry Pi, the module delivers a stable 5V/5A output through its USB-C and USB-A ports. It features a high-efficiency buck-boost converter, an INA219 current sensor for real-time voltage and current monitoring via I2C, and instant detection of external power loss through a pogo-pin connection on GPIO26. Geekworm provides example scripts to monitor supercapacitor charge, initiate a clean shutdown, and automatically restart the Pi once power is restored.

Supercapacitors offer a significantly longer lifespan than lithium batteries (over 500,000 charge cycles), rapid charging in about 15 minutes, and no risks of thermal runaway, swelling, or long-term capacity degradation. The 60F model is priced at $46.90, while the 100F variant is available for a few dollars more.
