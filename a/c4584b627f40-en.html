The Unexpected Challenges of Building a Hackathon Badge

A developer details the pitfalls encountered while producing 1,750 badges for an event.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Building a badge for a hackathon might seem straightforward—a microcontroller, some buttons, a battery, and a screen. Yet, scaling from a few prototypes to producing hundreds or thousands of fully functional units ready on day one of the event is a completely different challenge. Nicholas Ficara, who designed 1,750 badges for Canada’s Hack The North hackathon, knows this firsthand.

His method involved a carefully planned sequence of prototypes and release candidates to catch issues early. Despite this, errors still crept in: misplaced components, incorrect PCB footprints, power supply problems, and rotated parts. Each issue was addressed, and the badges were delivered on time, well-received and actively used during the event.

His story highlights the logistical and technical hurdles of mass-producing electronic devices, even for seemingly simple projects. A must-read for anyone considering creating a badge or a similar project.
