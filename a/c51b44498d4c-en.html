Raspberry Pi Pico: Halloween Projects to Spook Your Guests

The Raspberry Pi Pico powers spooky decorations and automatons with LEDs and sensors, requiring no advanced skills.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

This Halloween, the Raspberry Pi Pico serves as the brain behind eerie decorations and automatons, despite its compact size. It brings life to props using programmable LED strips from Pimoroni, compatible with a standard Pico. These kits feature a Plasma 2350 W board (a Pico 2 W optimized for LED control) and, optionally, a skull-shaped bottle to house the lights. The LEDs, adjustable in colors like green, orange, and purple, can be integrated into any existing decoration.

Other projects leverage LED matrices to craft faces inside pumpkins. Unlike traditional carved pumpkins, these versions allow unconventional patterns, such as moving eyes or custom expressions. A minimalist robot by Kevin McAleer uses ultrasonic sensors to detect movement and trigger articulated mouth and eyebrow animations, delivering a simple yet effective scare.

The most unsettling ideas repurpose everyday items. A store-bought "bag o’ bones" was upgraded with a PIR motion sensor, motors, and a speaker to trigger a bone-rattling dance and play Toccata and Fugue in D minor as unsuspecting guests pass by. Another project features a portrait of George Washington whose eyes follow viewers, using servos to move cardboard elements behind the frame. These low-tech approaches, paired with basic sensors, create chilling effects.
