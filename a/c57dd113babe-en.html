Gigaplex: A Tiny Retro Console with 165 Charlieplexed LEDs

A keychain-sized portable micro-console using 165 LEDs as a display, controlled via Charlieplexing.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Drawing inspiration from early electronic LED games, the Gigaplex project reimagines minimalist displays with a grid of 165 surface-mount LEDs arranged on a custom 11×15 PCB. Unlike traditional screens, this solution leverages Charlieplexing, a technique that allows controlling a large number of LEDs with few GPIO pins. Here, just 14 pins manage the entire display by cycling between high, low, and high-impedance states.

While not all LEDs can light up simultaneously, persistence of vision simulates a full display by rapidly refreshing them. The firmware also handles pixel brightness variations, requiring precise timing. For interaction, Gigaplex includes physical buttons, an inertial measurement unit (IMU) for motion-based input, an ambient light sensor for automatic brightness adjustment, and a piezo buzzer for music and sound effects. A battery and power management circuitry provide full portability.

The assembly process uses an automated pick-and-place machine to solder components onto the PCB after applying solder paste through a stencil. A resin-printed enclosure completes the device. To showcase Gigaplex’s capabilities, its creator compressed an animated Bad Apple!! sequence with synchronized audio, using just 2 bits per pixel (four brightness levels) and storing only modified rows between frames. The music, originally 41 KB in MIDI format, was reduced to 1.9 KB of note events. The combined firmware and assets occupy 51 KB of the available 62 KB flash memory. Classic games like Snake and Breakout were also adapted for the platform.
