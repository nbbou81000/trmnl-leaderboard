Open-Source eInk Bike Computer with 4.7" Display

An open-source project turns a 4.7" e-ink screen into a standalone bike computer with GPS and wireless connectivity.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

A new open-source project transforms a 4.7" e-ink display into a standalone bike computer. Built around an ESP32-S3 board, it includes 16 MB of flash memory and 8 MB of PSRAM, providing enough processing power for embedded applications.

The e-ink screen remains readable even in direct sunlight and is paired with a basic GPS module, Bluetooth 5 connectivity for external sensors (heart rate, power, cadence), and an SD card slot for offline maps, routes, and ride logs. A front light improves visibility in low-light conditions, while USB-C support allows firmware updates directly from a desktop Chromium browser.

However, the prototype has notable technical limitations. It lacks both a pressure sensor and a barometric altimeter, forcing climbs to be estimated from elevation data baked into map tiles. The GPS receiver, while functional, is less capable than modern multi-band modules, struggling with signal acquisition and retention in urban areas or under tree cover. Without a magnetometer (compass), the map can only orient based on travel direction, failing to show the correct orientation when stationary. Power and durability are also concerns: a 1,500 mAh battery delivers roughly 7.4 hours of runtime without the front light, and the board ships unsealed with no IP rating. Physical buttons are poorly designed, making interaction difficult with gloves or in wet conditions.

The creators recommend improvements such as adding a barometric altimeter, multi-band GPS, magnetometer, larger battery, and a weatherproof case. Contributions to the open-source project are welcome, including hardware adaptations and feature suggestions.
