4 Outdated Docker Containers to Replace to Avoid Security Risks

Abandoned or unmaintained Docker containers expose your systems to vulnerabilities. Here’s how to identify and replace them.

Unmaintained Docker containers pose risks for homelabs, even if they still work. Their abandonment exposes systems to unpatched vulnerabilities or future failures. Four popular tools are affected: Watchtower, NZBGet, Overseerr/Jellyseerr, and Requestrr.

Outdated maintenance tools

Watchtower, often recommended for automatic container updates, has been unmaintained since 2025. While its repository is archived, it retains sensitive access to the Docker socket. Several forks exist, such as nicholas-fedor/watchtower, but their reliability is uncertain. Alternatives like WUD, Dockwatch, or dockcheck are suggested.

Media management and download software to migrate

NZBGet, a Usenet download client, saw its original project abandoned in 2022. Two active forks exist: one maintained by NZBGet.com and another by LinuxServer.io, based on the former. For a simpler solution, SABnzbd remains a lightweight and maintained alternative.

Overseerr and Jellyseerr, media request tools for Plex or Jellyfin, were merged in 2026 under the name Seerr. The latter is actively maintained and includes critical security patches applied in early 2026. A migration is advised, especially if old containers are publicly exposed.

Finally, Requestrr, a Discord bot for media management, saw its original repository abandoned in 2024. The fork thomst08/requestrr offers a replacement solution. However, Seerr now includes Discord notifications, providing a more modern alternative.

To mitigate risks, check the last update date of your Docker containers and prioritize maintained solutions.
