Romu: A Tiny RP2354A USB Board in Crowdfunding

A 15.5 x 12 mm USB board powered by RP2354A, designed for embedded projects like security keys or MIDI controllers.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Bitmerse introduces Romu, a compact USB board measuring just 15.5 x 12 mm, built around the Raspberry Pi RP2354A microcontroller. This chip features two cores: a Cortex-M33 running at 150 MHz with Arm TrustZone support for secure operations, and a RISC-V Hazard3 core (RV32IMAC) with extensions like Zba, Zbb, Zbs, and Zcb.

The board includes a native USB 2.0 Full-Speed interface, no GPIO pins, and integrates 520 KB of SRAM and 2 MB of stacked flash memory. It also features a capacitive touch button, a boot button, and an RGB LED. Power is supplied directly via USB, regulated to 3.3V. A 3D-printed protective shim is included.

Romu ships preloaded with MicroPython, compatible with the Thonny IDE for immediate development. An optional programming and debugging jig (65 $) adds a 20-pin JTAG connector and UART access via USB-C. Schematics, PCB design files, BoM, and 3D enclosure STL files will be released as open-source on GitHub, along with examples like a dino game, a FIDO2 security key implementation, and a website launcher demo.

The Crowd Supply crowdfunding campaign targets $5,000. Pricing starts at $40 for the board alone, with shipping fees of $8 within the US and $18 internationally. Shipments are expected to begin by the end of April 2027.
