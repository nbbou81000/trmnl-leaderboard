Decoding QR Codes by Hand Without a Smartphone

A developer shares a method to manually read and decode QR codes from versions 1 to 5.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

QR codes make sharing links or contact details easy, but they usually require a smartphone to decode. Noel Friedrich has developed a way to read and decode these codes manually. His method is designed to be approachable, even for beginners.

The process starts with transcription: the QR code is copied onto paper, following the reference patterns. Friedrich provides a template where slashes mark error correction areas. These zones, crucial for reliability, must be processed by flipping the affected bits before decoding. Once this step is mastered, users can move on to the actual decoding phase.

The article then explains how error correction works—a key feature of QR codes that allows data recovery even if the code is slightly damaged. Friedrich demonstrates this with a practical example, showing how to exploit QR code generation and reading rules to create artistic versions. While these variants can be read by humans, their complexity makes them challenging to interpret without practice.

This method isn’t limited to standard QR codes: it also enables the creation of partially or fully human-readable codes without digital tools. A useful skill in situations where smartphone access is unavailable.
