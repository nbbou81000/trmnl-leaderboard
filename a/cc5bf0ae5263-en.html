A Vintage and Precise Kitchen Timer with an Analog Dial

A maker repurposes an old electrical meter into a precise retro kitchen timer featuring an analog dial and an intuitive tactile interface.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Frustrated by the limitations of touchscreen and mechanical kitchen timers, maker Michał Zalewski built Burninator, a custom timer that blends vintage aesthetics with modern precision. The device repurposes a Crompton panel meter—originally designed to measure electrical current—by replacing its scale with a custom adhesive-printed dial.

The timer’s scale uses a quasi-logarithmic layout divided into four sections: up to one minute in 10-second increments, one to five minutes, five to 30 minutes, and up to three hours. This design ensures fine control for short durations while simplifying adjustments for longer intervals. An illuminated button powers the device on, and a rotary knob lets users set the time. The countdown starts automatically three seconds after the last adjustment, and adjustments can be made while the timer is running.

Housed in a handmade maple enclosure, an 8-bit AVR DB-series microcontroller handles timing and controls the analog meter’s needle via pulse-width modulation (PWM). A small speaker, amplified by an AD8531 circuit, sounds an alarm every 15 seconds until the user presses the button or adds more time by turning the knob clockwise. Power is supplied through a 5 V adapter.

Burninator stands out for its blend of retro charm and practical functionality, offering a more intuitive alternative to clunky digital or imprecise mechanical timers.
