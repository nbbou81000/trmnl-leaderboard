SAWbot: A Simple Yet Efficient Amphibious Robot Using Continuous Waves

An amphibious robot inspired by eels moves on land and in water using a single motor and a flexible membrane.

Researchers at Ben-Gurion University of the Negev have developed SAWbot, an amphibious robot that moves seamlessly on land and in water using an unconventional propulsion method. Unlike traditional amphibious robots, which often combine separate locomotion systems, SAWbot relies on a continuous traveling wave generated by a flexible membrane. This design draws inspiration from anguilliform swimming, the locomotion technique used by eels and other elongated aquatic animals.

The core of the system is a single motor that converts rotary motion into a smooth sinusoidal wave traveling along the membrane. This membrane, stretched between two supports, provides fluid propulsion without requiring multiple synchronized actuators or complex control algorithms. On land, SAWbot uses treaded wheels for mobility, while rear floats keep the wave-generating assembly afloat in water.

In swimming tests, the robot achieved speeds of up to 34 cm/s, with an advance ratio of 0.73, meaning it traveled at 73% of the wave’s speed. Its energy efficiency, measured by a mechanical cost of transport ranging from 0.13 to 0.37, places it among the most efficient swimming robots reported to date. Real-world experiments confirmed its ability to overcome obstacles like grass, gravel, slopes, and even transition automatically between land and water.

This mechanical simplicity and adaptability open possibilities for applications in environmental monitoring or aquatic exploration, with potential scaling of the design for different robot sizes.
