RP2040 Drives HDA Audio Codec Directly Without FPGA or Chipset

An RP2040 microcontroller implements an HDA interface to drive an Intel audio codec directly without dedicated hardware.

A developer has demonstrated on habr.com the ability to operate an Intel High Definition Audio (HDA) codec—also known as Azalia—directly from an RP2040 microcontroller, without requiring a dedicated chipset or FPGA. This achievement leverages the RP2040’s PIO (Programmable I/O) blocks, which can handle high-speed signals.

The HDA interface uses four wires to transmit up to 15 audio channels in and out, far exceeding the limits of standard I²S (2 channels). The technical challenge lies in precise timing: the BCLK clock frequency must be exactly 24 MHz, while the SYNC and SDO signals change on every clock edge, enabling a 48 Mbit/s double data rate throughput. Audio frames, each 1,000 bits long, correspond to a base sampling rate of 48 kHz.

To achieve this, the RP2040 employs four PIO state machines: one for SDO, one for SYNC, one for SDI that also generates BCLK via a side-set, and a fourth to assign the codec’s bus address at startup. The main core (Core 1) handles the packing and unpacking of audio samples, which are not aligned to 32-bit boundaries. A demo successfully transmitted 192 kHz sine waves across all 10 DAC channels of an ALC889 codec. Overclocking the RP2040 to a 32 MHz BCLK achieved a 256 kHz sampling rate, though this caused the codec to overheat.
