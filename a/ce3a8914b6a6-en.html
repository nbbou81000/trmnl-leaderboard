CoughNet: Raspberry Pis Detect Coughs Without Recording or Identifying Individuals

Researchers deploy Raspberry Pis with microphones to detect coughs in a room, without recording or identifying individuals.

Researchers from Binghamton University (SUNY) have developed CoughNet, a privacy-preserving cough detection system. It uses listening stations made of three Raspberry Pi boards equipped with low-cost microphones in the same room. No cameras are involved, and no audio is recorded.

The system works by analyzing sounds in real time. When a cough is detected, a lightweight machine-learning model, CoughNet, processes the data from all three microphones to estimate the approximate location of the cougher. The microphone closest to the sound acts as a reference, while the two others, placed further away, confirm the source through correlation.

CoughNet can distinguish between successive coughs to determine if they come from one or multiple individuals, without ever identifying the people involved. Calculations are first performed locally on each Raspberry Pi. If the result lacks confidence, the data is sent via a LoRa transceiver — a low-energy communication technology — to a more powerful remote system for deeper analysis. LoRa was chosen for its energy efficiency, outperforming Wi-Fi in this regard.

The team highlights that their method avoids the heavy computational demands of traditional approaches. Their work was presented at the CHASE '26 conference (IEEE International Conference on Connected Health: Applications, Systems and Engineering Technologies 2026), but is not available as open source.
