56k.rip: Relive 1990s Dial-Up Internet Experience Online

A 1990s-style browser simulator recreates the authentic 56k dial-up experience, complete with lag and iconic sounds.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

56k.rip is a browser-based simulator that faithfully recreates the 1990s dial-up Internet experience. Upon loading, users are presented with a virtual desktop where they must open Dial-Up Networking to initiate a connection. The process includes realistic sound effects: the modem picking up, dial tones, and a 20-second handshake before the connection is established.

The loading speed matches the selected modem speed (14.4k, 28.8k, or 56k). Web pages load in text blocks, images render line by line, and downloads crawl at the chosen speed. Connections often fail, and calls can be dropped if another user picks up a phone. Behind this virtual modem lies a 1997-style mini-Internet, featuring period-appropriate content: a fishing page, recipes, a guest book, a webring, classifieds, missed connections, weather forecasts, a TV guide, and an article explaining what the internet is.

The simulator also includes 1990s-era tools and entertainment: an email client, a chat lobby, Minesweeper, a card game, a paint program, a command prompt, and screen savers. All sounds, except for the modem handshake (recorded from real hardware), are synthesized in the browser. Images are procedurally generated and reduced to the 216-color web-safe palette of the era. No account is required, and nothing is stored outside the user’s browser.
