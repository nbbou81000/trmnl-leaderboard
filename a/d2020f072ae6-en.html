Manual scooter converted into an electric scooter with a cordless drill

An enthusiast electrified a Razor A5 Lux scooter using a cordless drill for event work.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Eddie Abbondanzio converted a Razor A5 Lux push scooter into an electric model using a Hercules cordless drill as the motor. The goal was to avoid buying a second scooter while having a motorized option for his work at autocross events. The system uses front-wheel drive, with the drill powering the front wheel through a keyed shaft and one-way bearings, allowing the wheel to free-spin when the drill is off.

Operating in low-speed mode, the setup reaches about 10 mph with a rider. A 5 Ah battery provides around 2 miles of range. To improve usability, the drill’s original trigger was relocated to the handlebars, requiring the drill’s casing to be opened and additional wiring added to the trigger assembly.

The wheel and tire were 3D-printed in two materials: ASA for the wheel and TPU for the tire. The tire features teeth on its mounting surface to prevent slippage. The builder notes that this conversion did not save money compared to buying a new electric scooter. More details are available at eddieabbondanz.io.
