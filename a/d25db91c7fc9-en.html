Adafruit Metro ESP32-P4 Hydra: Arduino UNO-shaped board with ESP32-P4 and ESP32-C6

Adafruit launches an Arduino UNO-sized development board featuring dual ESP32 chips for HMI, IoT, and multimedia applications.

Adafruit has unveiled the Metro ESP32-P4 Hydra, an Arduino UNO-sized development board combining two ESP32 chips. The primary chip, the ESP32-P4, features a dual-core RISC-V high-performance CPU (up to 360 MHz) and a low-power core (up to 40 MHz), along with a 2D graphics accelerator and H.264/JPEG video decoding support. It is paired with 16MB PSRAM and 16MB NOR flash.

The connectivity is handled by a secondary module, the ESP32-C6, providing WiFi 6 (802.11ax), Bluetooth 5.3 LE, Thread, and Zigbee support. The board also includes a USB 2.0 High-Speed port, a microSD card slot, Raspberry Pi-compatible MIPI DSI and CSI connectors (22-pin format), and a STEMMA QT connector for I2C/I3C sensors. Standard Arduino UNO headers offer digital and analog pins, as well as SPI, I2C, UART, and PWM interfaces.

Targeted at HMI, camera, and IoT applications, the Metro ESP32-P4 Hydra supports Raspberry Pi 5-inch and 7-inch touch displays (720x1280 resolution) and Raspberry Pi Zero-style camera modules with the OV5647 sensor. It is programmable via Espressif’s ESP-IDF framework, supports TinyUF2 drag-and-drop firmware updates, and is compatible with CircuitPython 11.0.0-alpha.2, though Arduino support is still in development. The board is priced at $34.95 on Adafruit’s store but currently ships with the ESP32-P4 v1 chipset instead of the newer ESP32-P4X v3 revision.
