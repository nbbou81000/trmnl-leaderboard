Yeah Hand: A Low-Cost 3D-Printed Robotic Hand

An open-source 3D-printed robotic hand mimics human dexterity using servos and fishing-line tendons.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Designed by Vittorio Lumare, the Yeah Hand stands out for its minimalist and accessible approach. Mostly 3D-printed, this robotic hand relies on five servomotors per hand, mimicking the human structure: motors in the palm drive the fingers via fishing-line tendons running through PTFE (Bowden-style) guides. This design reduces friction and prevents overheating, as demonstrated by a 800-cycle open/close test without noticeable heat buildup.

The Yeah Hand delivers impressive performance for its cost: it can lift up to 8 kg and apply a maximum grasping force of 5 kg. For better grip, each finger features silicone pads, while the thumbs include "thumbnails" for fine object handling. Two versions are available: a robotic one with various mounting flanges and an ROS driver, and an experimental prosthetic version, differing mainly in its attachment system.

For intuitive control, Lumare created a glove with built-in flex sensors, mirroring human hand movements in real time. Demo videos showcase its abilities, such as handling smooth or delicate objects. This open-source project targets makers looking to explore humanoid robotics without heavy financial investment.
