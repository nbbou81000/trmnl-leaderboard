V-Control Unveils V-SoM for Power Electronics Prototyping

Italian startup V-Control introduces the V-SoM, a system-on-module for developing and scaling power electronic converters.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Italian startup V-Control is gearing up for a crowdfunding campaign for its V-SoM system-on-module, designed to streamline the development and scaling of power electronic converters. According to founders Federico Campanelli and Stefano Savio, the module addresses common gaps in power electronics design: deterministic PWM generation, real-time ADC acquisition, host PC communication, dynamic parameter tuning, data logging, and a clear path from prototype to production.

The V-SoM is built around STMicroelectronics’ STM32H755 microcontroller, featuring a Cortex-M4 core running at 240 MHz for user control code and a Cortex-M7 core at 480 MHz dedicated to communications. The latter handles Ethernet connectivity, variable exchange, and interfacing with V-Control’s proprietary VScope software. The module supports validation of algorithms written in C, PLECS, or Simulink, with real-time display of internal variables, live parameter adjustments, and data export for post-processing.

Hardware-wise, the V-SoM offers three 16-bit ADC converters (up to 3.6 MS/s on 14 inputs), 56 configurable GPIO pins, five high-resolution PWM channels, and three complementary channels. It also includes Fast Ethernet, CAN, SPI, and I2C interfaces. The shipped firmware supports 20 read/write channels at 20 kS/s, though the hardware can handle up to 60 channels at 50 kS/s. Technical documentation, schematics, bill of materials, and partial firmware source code are available on GitHub under the CERN Open Hardware License v2 and MIT license, but PCB design files and part of the custom Cortex-M7 firmware remain proprietary.

V-Control plans to launch its crowdfunding campaign on Crowd Supply in the near future, with no specific date announced yet.
