ESP32 Turned into 2.4/5 GHz Software-Defined Radio Receiver Without Extra Hardware

An open-source firmware leverages the ESP32’s ADC to turn it into a 2.4/5 GHz SDR receiver without extra components.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The open-source ESP-SDR firmware repurposes an ESP32 as a 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz Software-Defined Radio (SDR) receiver without requiring additional hardware. Instead of using the chip’s built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth modems, the firmware bypasses them to access the internal ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter) chain directly. It captures raw I/Q baseband samples and streams them to a computer over USB or UART. Once received, these samples can be processed and visualized as a radio spectrum or waterfall display, effectively turning supported ESP32 models into low-cost SDR receivers.

Supported ESP32 variants include the ESP32, ESP32-C3, ESP32-C5 (dual-band 2.4/5 GHz), ESP32-C6, ESP32-C61, ESP32-S2, ESP32-S3, and the preview ESP32-S31. Frequency coverage varies by chip: the 2.4 GHz ISM band (roughly 2.2–2.7 GHz) and the 5 GHz band (4.8–6.0 GHz for the ESP32-C5).

This solution offers hobbyists an affordable alternative to dedicated SDR hardware, leveraging the existing capabilities of ESP32 microcontrollers.
