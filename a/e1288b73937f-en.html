DIY Scientific Calculator with Sharp Memory LCD and STM32L431

An enthusiast replaces a discontinued scientific calculator with a fully customized, ultra-thin, low-power model.

A maker replaced a discontinued scientific calculator like the HP 35s with a fully custom-built model. The project uses an STM32L431 microcontroller, selected for its balance of processing power, memory, and low power consumption, including Stop2 mode which, according to the creator, could allow decades of operation on two parallel CR2032 coin cells.

The display is a Sharp Memory LCD with 400×240 pixels, whose state is retained without constant refreshing, significantly reducing power usage. Two boost converters step up the battery voltage (3 V) to regulated 3.3 V and 5 V rails, with the latter powering the display. The 43-key matrix keyboard includes anti-ghosting diodes to prevent errors when multiple keys are pressed simultaneously.

The most challenging part of the build was manufacturing the case and keys. The 40 resin keycaps were individually produced, engraved, painted, and fitted, while the case halves were 3D printed in resin using SLA. To engrave the legends, a rotary tool was mounted on a Prusa i3 MK3 3D printer, turning it into a mini CNC engraver. Assembly revealed several issues: residual flux under the switches caused intermittent presses, keycaps had excessive play, and surface-mount diodes interfered with the case fit. A future revision is planned to address these flaws.

The calculator supports Reverse Polish Notation and algebraic entry, graphing, matrix operations, equation solving, and a wide range of advanced mathematical functions. The 1.0 mm-thick PCB contributes to the device’s compact design.
