RHYTHM: A Retro Open-Source MP3 Player with High-End DAC

A compact open-source audio player without subscriptions or apps, featuring a 32-bit DAC and a 2-inch color screen.

Probably Harmless revives the standalone MP3 player experience with RHYTHM, an open-source device blending retro design with modern audio components. Unlike today’s solutions, it doesn’t rely on internet or subscriptions: music files (MP3, FLAC, WAV, AAC, OGG Vorbis, Opus) are stored on a microSD card and played up to 24-bit/96 kHz.

The compact unit includes a 2-inch color screen (320×240 pixels), a volume and navigation dial, and five physical buttons. Twelve RGB LEDs arranged in two vertical columns mimic a stereo VU meter, lighting up with the music. A rare feature: two 3.5 mm jacks allow two people to listen simultaneously, each with its dedicated amplifier (ESS ES9038Q2M 32-bit DAC, OPA1662, TPA6132A2) to prevent interference between headphones. Volume is shared but not independently adjustable.

The device also works as a USB DAC for computers or smartphones. Powered by an ESP32-S3 (16 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM), it uses ESP-IDF, FreeRTOS, and LVGL for easy software modifications. A 2,000 mAh lithium-polymer battery provides 12 to 15 hours of playback, rechargeable via USB-C. A "pocket mode" turns off the screen and LEDs to save power.

RHYTHM goes beyond music playback: it supports Retro-Go firmware to play NES and Game Boy games, and even Doom. Probably Harmless plans to release schematics, firmware, and 3D-printable case designs under open-source licenses during its crowdfunding campaign. The campaign hasn’t launched yet, but interested users can sign up for updates.
