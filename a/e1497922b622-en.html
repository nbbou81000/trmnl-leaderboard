Researchers Turn a 3D Printer Into an Electronics Pick-and-Place Machine

Scientists repurpose a standard 3D printer to automatically assemble electronic circuits during printing, with no hardware modifications required.

Researchers from the University of Bristol and the Hasso Plattner Institute have developed Print & Place, a software-based method to turn a standard fused filament fabrication (FFF/FDM) 3D printer into an automated pick-and-place machine for electronic components. Unlike traditional approaches requiring manual assembly or specialized conductive filaments, this solution automates the positioning, electrical connections, and encapsulation of parts directly during the printing process.

The technique leverages the existing print head to pick up components. A small amount of filament is extruded onto a component, which adheres to the head after cooling. The part is then positioned on the ongoing print and released either by heating the filament to its melting point or with a rapid tool head movement. No additional hardware is needed: tests were conducted using an unmodified Prusa XL, chosen for its large build volume allowing both a component tray and the printed model to fit simultaneously.

Results show the system can handle 0805 SMD components, conductive traces as thin as 1.25 mm, and printed circuit boards up to the size of an Adafruit Flora development board. However, 0603 resistors proved too small to manipulate. Demonstrated devices include a glowing articulated caterpillar, an LED fridge magnet, and a robotic firefly capable of vibration-based movement—all functional immediately after printing. The research was published in the Proceedings of the 39th ACM Symposium on User Interface Software and Technology (UIST '26) under open access terms.
