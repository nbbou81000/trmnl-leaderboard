VENTUNO Q Turned Into a Retro Gaming Rig via ARM Emulation

A hacker successfully runs Steam and games on the Arduino VENTUNO Q, an embedded board designed for edge AI, using x86-to-ARM emulation.

The Arduino VENTUNO Q, an embedded board powered by an Arm processor, was repurposed from its original use in edge AI and robotics into a functional gaming PC. Jim Bruges overcame the native incompatibility between the VENTUNO Q’s Arm architecture and the x86 binaries of Steam and games.

To solve this, Bruges employed FEX-Emu, an emulator that translates x86 and x86-64 instructions to ARM64 on the fly. The setup relies on an x86 Ubuntu root filesystem stored on a 1 TB NVMe SSD, while Mesa and the open-source Vulkan driver Turnip provide hardware acceleration via the Adreno GPU. Boot time improved from 9.5 seconds to 2.9 seconds after extracting FEX’s compressed root filesystem onto the SSD.

The hack successfully ran Half-Life 2 in its native x86 Linux version, achieving 40 to 50 FPS at 1080p, with drops to 20 FPS in demanding scenes. For Skyrim Special Edition, a Windows game, Proton was required to translate Windows APIs, in addition to FEX. However, the latest Proton failed due to a dependency on a Vulkan feature unsupported by the Adreno 623 GPU. Bruges reverted to Proton 10.0 with DXVK 2.6.2, allowing the game to launch. Performance, limited by the GPU, was optimized by lowering the resolution to 720p and disabling certain graphical effects, resulting in 20-30 FPS.

This project proves that the VENTUNO Q, despite being designed for embedded tasks, can run games—though performance remains far below that of dedicated gaming PCs.
