OpenStick at $10: A Minimalist, Silent Self-Hosted Homelab

A $10 microcomputer running Debian to host lightweight services, perfect for starting self-hosting.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The OpenStick, a $10 microcomputer, turns out to be a silent, energy-efficient home server. Though its performance lags behind a smartwatch, it runs Debian, providing a full environment with systemd for service management and Podman for lightweight containerization.

Several platform-optimized tools enhance usability. AdGuard Home blocks malicious domains at the DNS level, cutting up to 60 % of suspicious queries. A stripped-down version is available for cautious users. Zeroclaw, a lightweight AI agent, monitors the system via Telegram with a 3 MB memory footprint in idle mode—compared to over 1 GB for similar solutions. It allows remote system checks, even in remote areas.

For secure service exposure, Cloudflared creates an encrypted tunnel without opening ports. Beszel displays system usage graphs and sends Telegram alerts, while Gatus monitors service availability. Fusion replaces paid RSS aggregators, and Shiori automatically archives bookmarked sites to prevent dead links. A small script also notifies container updates via Telegram.

These lightweight, configurable tools turn the OpenStick into a functional homelab despite its bargain price.
