Banana Pi BPI-M8: An Allwinner A733 Rival to the Raspberry Pi 5 Starting at $69

Banana Pi launches a Raspberry Pi 5 clone powered by an Allwinner A733 SoC, available in two models starting at $69.

Banana Pi has unveiled the BPI-M8, a single-board computer designed to mimic the Raspberry Pi 5’s form factor but powered by an Allwinner A733 system-on-chip. The SoC features an octa-core configuration with two high-performance Arm Cortex-A76 cores running up to 2 GHz and six low-power Cortex-A55 cores at 1.8 GHz. It also includes an Imagination PowerVR BXM-4-64 MC1 GPU, a neural accelerator capable of up to three TOPS for on-device machine learning and computer vision tasks, along with support for LPDDR4, LPDDR4X, or LPDDR5 memory and UFS or eMMC storage options.

The board mirrors the Raspberry Pi 5’s layout, featuring a 40-pin GPIO header at the top, USB and Ethernet ports on the right, HDMI and power connectors at the bottom, and FFC connectors on the left. However, its port selection differs: one USB 3.0 port, two USB 2.0 ports, a single HDMI 2.0b output (supporting 4K60), a DSI connector for additional displays, a PCIe FFC port for DisplayPort, a gigabit Ethernet port (with optional PoE), two CSI connectors for cameras, and built-in Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.0 radios. Notably, it retains a 3.5mm analog audio jack, a feature absent from the Raspberry Pi 5.

Two models are currently available for purchase: the BPI-M8A (4GB RAM, 64GB eMMC storage) priced at $103, and the BPI-M8B (4GB RAM, 16MB SPI flash) at $69. Detailed specifications can be found on Banana Pi’s documentation website.
