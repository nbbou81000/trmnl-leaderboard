Rouse: A DIY Alarm Clock Without Spying for ESP32

An open-source firmware turns an ESP32 board into a smart alarm clock, with no user account or data collection.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Commercial smart alarm clocks from companies like Google or Amazon offer convenient features like weather updates and photo displays, but they often come with hidden costs: targeted ads, data collection, or reliance on third-party apps that may stop working. Rouse, an open-source firmware designed for ESP32 boards such as the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch or the Cheap Yellow Display, provides a privacy-focused alternative.

Unlike commercial solutions, Rouse requires no user account, subscription, or mobile app. Configuration is handled via a web interface accessible from any browser, and firmware updates only install with explicit user approval. The project prioritizes transparency and user control, a stark contrast to the practices of major tech companies.

To build a functional alarm clock, users need to add basic components like buttons and a speaker to the ESP32 board. A comprehensive build guide is provided, complete with 3D-printable enclosure files, though users can also choose a custom enclosure or existing case. Rouse fits into the tradition of DIY clock projects but stands out for its community-driven approach and accessible documentation, designed to encourage participation.
