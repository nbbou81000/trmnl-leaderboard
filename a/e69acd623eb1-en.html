Pico 2 W Displays 1080p HDMI Without Video Memory

A Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W generates a 1080p30 HDMI signal in real time, without a framebuffer or external video hardware.

The TV-Top Kiosk project, developed by Aaron Gayle and featured on Open Electronics, turns a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W into an HDMI console for TV-Top Games. This platform uses a television as the game board, with players interacting via their smartphones. The firmware connects to a WiFi network, pairs with a TV-Top server, and displays content sent by the current game.

The display reaches a resolution of 1920×1080 pixels at 30 frames per second, with 8 bits per color channel. Without a framebuffer or dedicated video hardware, the solution relies on a clever technique: scanlines are generated on the fly, six lines before the beam passes, using vector geometries stored in flash memory. The HSTX (High-Speed Serial Transmitter) protocol is used to send this data via the Pico 2 W’s GPIO pins, running at 372 MHz with a core regulator voltage of 1.30 V. The required bandwidth for 1080p30 (744 Mbit/s) stays within the Pico 2 W’s capabilities, despite its 520 KB of RAM being insufficient to hold a full frame.

Compatibility is ensured with HSTX DVI adapters, Pico DVI Sock, or even hand-soldered wiring, thanks to a match with libdvi’s pinout configuration. The source code and technical details are available on GitHub and the Open Electronics website.
