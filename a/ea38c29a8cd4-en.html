An Open Source Linear Capacitive Encoder for Ultra-Precise Measurements

An open-source linear capacitive encoder delivers ultra-precise measurements down to 40 micrometers, inspired by digital caliper sensors.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Linear capacitive encoders, like those found in digital calipers, enable highly accurate displacement measurements. [Nick] has developed an open-source implementation of this technology, making it accessible to makers and developers looking to integrate fine linear tracking into their projects.

The system uses two PCBs: an emitter board featuring a grid of eight etched antennas, and a reflector board fixed in place. The emitter board sends pulses through its antennas, while the reflector board bounces these signals back to a receiving antenna on the emitter board. The relative movement between the two boards alters the capacitive coupling, changing the intensity of the received signals. An STM32 microcontroller processes these variations to calculate the position with a precision of 40 micrometers.

Design files (PCB layouts, firmware) are available on GitHub, along with detailed documentation explaining how capacitive coupling translates into positional readings. This project is particularly useful for builders of machine tools, robotic systems, or components requiring ultra-precise linear tracking. Enthusiasts can also study previously featured digital caliper teardowns to deepen their understanding of capacitive encoders.
