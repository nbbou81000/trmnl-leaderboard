A Quadruped Robot That Takes to the Skies

An open-source quadruped robot merges walking and flying into a single lightweight 264 g device.

Maker Dorian Todd has transformed his open-source quadruped robot, Sesame, originally designed for walking, into a machine capable of taking off like a drone. The idea stemmed from the robot’s lightweight design, which originally cost less than $60 to build. By adding drone motors and optimizing its structure, Todd managed to reduce its weight to 264 g while maintaining enough rigidity for flight.

To lighten the robot, Todd replaced the original MG90S metal-gear servos with lighter SG90 plastic-gear models, saving about 35 g. The chassis was redesigned with weight-saving cutouts and 3D-printed using carbon fiber-reinforced nylon (PPA-CF). Additional carbon fiber components, machined with a desktop CNC, reinforced the structure without adding significant weight. A major challenge was stabilizing the drone motors during flight, while Sesame’s legs are designed to move freely. Todd solved this by adding mechanical stops that lock the legs at a 90-degree angle during takeoff, allowing the propellers to keep the motors properly oriented without overloading the servos.

The robot features two independent electronic systems. A LOLIN S2 Mini ESP32 board handles walking and facial expressions, just as it did in the original Sesame. Meanwhile, an all-in-one flight controller manages the drone motors, stabilization, and radio communications. This setup allows the robot to keep walking even if the flight system fails. After several prototypes, including indoor crashes, Sesame successfully demonstrated the full sequence: walking, extending its legs into flight position, taking off, hovering, landing, and returning to walking mode.
