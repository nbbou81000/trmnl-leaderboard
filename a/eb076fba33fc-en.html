Restoring a Soviet ES-4001 Mainframe Control Panel

A 1970s Soviet control panel linked to the Chernobyl accident is being restored to preserve a piece of computing history.

A Soviet ES-4001 control panel, designed for a 1970s mainframe, has been acquired by the Chornobyl Family for restoration. This device, built during the USSR’s push to match Western computing technology, is tied to the Chernobyl nuclear accident. A similar model, the ES-1060, was installed in the computer center of the Duga-1 radar system near the Chernobyl plant but was never operational after the 1986 disaster.

The ES-4001 panel, dating from the early 1980s, was sourced from a data center where it saw less critical use. While its condition is mostly intact, restoration work is needed to address damage from its abrupt removal from the mainframe. The project also aims to document how the system functioned, as it shares similarities with the IBM System/370 but includes unique Soviet-era quirks. With only about 300 units ever produced, every surviving component of this series holds historical significance.

The restoration highlights the technical challenges of reviving obsolete hardware while emphasizing the importance of preserving rare technological artifacts. Further details on the technical aspects and historical context are expected to be shared as the project progresses.
