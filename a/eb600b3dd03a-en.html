A Conference Badge Powered by a 1976 Microcontroller

A retro conference badge driven by a 1976 Intel 8048 microcontroller, featuring vintage LED displays.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

mwfeurtado’s project revives the Intel 8048, an 8-bit microcontroller released in 1976, to create a functional conference badge. This historic component, the first in its family, featured an 8-bit processor, 27 GPIO pins, an external data bus, and a clock speed of up to 11 MHz. Unlike modern microcontrollers, its program was masked into ROM during manufacturing, restricting modification options.

To bypass this limitation, the maker uses the EA (External Access) pin on the 8048, enabling execution of firmware stored in an external EEPROM. The circuit also includes an SN74HC573 (8-bit latch) for address demultiplexing, an Intel 8155 (RAM and I/O expansion), an 8 MHz crystal, and a 7805 voltage regulator. A 40-pin RCBUS connector provides additional expansion capabilities.

The badge stands out with its vintage OSRAM SLY2016 LED displays, two 5×7 yellow LED matrix modules each displaying four 7-bit ASCII characters. Connected directly to the 8155’s GPIO pins, they offer eight characters of output. The firmware, written in assembly, displays a scrolling welcome message: "Hello! My Name is MCS-8048 1976 Intel MCU".
