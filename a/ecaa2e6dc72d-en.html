Philips CD-i: USB-C Power Supply Replaces Original Brick

A hacker replaces the Philips CD-i’s 8P8C power supply with a modern USB-C solution for better reliability.

The Philips CD-i, a 1990s console, suffers from a major flaw: its power supply uses an 8P8C connector (similar to an Ethernet port), whose clip breaks easily. To address this issue, [MattKC] developed a solution based on USB-C, which is more robust and widely available.

The technical challenge lay in the CD-i’s electrical specifications. The console consumes up to 3 A at 5 V, but also requires a -5 V at 0.2 A line. Most standard USB-C chargers only provide 5 V/3 A, which is insufficient to generate this negative voltage. [MattKC] designed a dedicated board to negotiate a 9 V USB Power Delivery supply, then convert it to 5 V and -5 V. A switching circuit also replicates the original power brick’s functions.

The project uses several key components: a CH221K for USB-C negotiation, a TPSM84538 to step down the voltage to 5 V (up to 5 A), and an LT3580 to invert the voltage and obtain the -5 V. This solution avoids the fragile connector issues while providing a more reliable power supply.
