A Hidden Microcontroller in a LoRa Chip Paves the Way for Software-Defined Radio

Semtech’s LR2021 LoRa chip hides a programmable microcontroller, enabling advanced software-defined radio applications beyond its original design.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Unlike conventional LoRa chips, often relegated to peripheral roles for a host microcontroller, Semtech’s LR2021 embeds its own programmable microcontroller. This breakthrough, uncovered by researcher Alex Beale, enables firmware modifications to unlock advanced capabilities.

,

Through reverse engineering, Beale gained access to the hidden microcontroller and implemented software-driven features such as on-chip Fast Fourier Transforms (FFTs) and autonomous mesh network node management. These enhancements transform the LR2021 into a versatile tool for software-defined radio (SDR) projects, eliminating the need for additional hardware.

,

This discovery aligns with a recent trend where dedicated radio chips (WiFi, LoRa) are repurposed via reverse engineering. Artificial intelligence tools, including large language models (LLMs), played a crucial role in tackling the project’s complexity, Beale notes. Despite 15 years of programming experience, he admits he couldn’t have completed the work without their assistance.

,

The practical implications are significant: reduced power consumption by offloading tasks from the host microcontroller to the LR2021, or the ability to create standalone nodes for sensor networks. However, Beale highlights that current modifications rely on volatile memory, meaning changes do not persist after a reboot.
