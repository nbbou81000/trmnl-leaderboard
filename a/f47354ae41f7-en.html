Xerxes Home Lab Turns Pi and Jetson Modules into Blade Servers for Clusters

An engineer is crowdfunding a modular chassis to turn Raspberry Pi CM5 or NVIDIA Jetson modules into compact, customizable blade nodes.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Engineer and computational neuroscientist Rob Newport is crowdfunding the Xerxes Home Lab, a modular and affordable hardware platform designed for home lab enthusiasts. The system converts single-board computers like the Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 or NVIDIA Jetson Nano into compact blade nodes suitable for cluster setups.

Each blade node includes an M.2 PCIe slot for NVMe storage or accelerators, a microSD slot, I2S and debug serial ports, and optional Power-over-Ethernet (PoE) support on a Gigabit Ethernet port. Raspberry Pi versions feature an expansion connector for mezzanine cards providing MIPI CSI (camera) and HDMI interfaces. The Jetson version includes an OCuLink port for PCI Express signals. Newport also offers an in-house module based on a Rockchip RK3566, with four Cortex-A55 cores and a 1 TOPS ML accelerator.

The nodes fit into modular racks available in two sizes: a six-slot mini version or a twelve-slot full-size 19" variant. Rack design files are released as open source for 3D printing custom enclosures. Kickstarter rewards start at 49 AUD for a Raspberry Pi CM5-compatible blade carrier, while a bundle including a Jetson Nano carrier, four Pi 2026 carriers, PoE accessories, and a six-slot mini rack is priced at 399 AUD. Shipments are expected in December 2026.
