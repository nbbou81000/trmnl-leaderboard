Home Assistant Connect AUX-2: Sync Old Speakers with Modern Devices

A $69 box turns analog speakers into synchronized audio devices via Home Assistant, without cloud or extra accounts.

The Home Assistant Connect AUX-2, created by Nabu Casa, integrates old or non-smart speakers into a synchronized audio ecosystem. This 106 x 63 x 25 mm device features an ESP32-S3, a TI PCM5122 DAC, and a W5500 Ethernet controller. It connects to an existing speaker’s analog (3.5 mm) or optical (mini-TOSLINK) input. Power options include USB-C or Power over Ethernet, with Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz or Ethernet support (Wi-Fi is disabled when Ethernet is connected).

Setup is streamlined: after plugging in, the device is automatically detected by Home Assistant via Bluetooth (Improv) or the companion app. Once configured, it appears as an ESPHome device in Home Assistant’s interface. The system synchronizes multiple speakers, even incompatible ones (like a Sonos Era 100 and an older speaker), using Sendspin, an open standard for local synchronized audio. Music Assistant acts as the audio source, relaying streams to the Connect AUX-2 and other compatible devices without relying on cloud services.

The box does not connect directly to streaming services (Spotify, etc.) but relies on Music Assistant to send audio. Users can create synchronized groups, such as a Sonos Era 100 and a Huawei Sound X, all controlled from a single interface. Audio quality is capped at 16-bit/48 kHz, suitable for most uses but excluding high-resolution formats. Two of the three 3.5 mm ports are RS-232 outputs (5 V) and not audio inputs—misplugging could damage an amplifier.

The Connect AUX-2 is output-only: it cannot stream to Bluetooth speakers or record audio from a turntable. Compatibility with some devices (like Chromecast) remains experimental. The box ships without Ethernet or USB-C cables.
