DIY Solder Stencil from a Soda Can Using a Laser

A precise and cost-effective DIY solder stencil made from a soda can and a laser cutter.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

Solder stencils enable precise application of solder paste onto a PCB. While industrial stencils are made of steel, hobbyists often turn to soda cans as a budget-friendly alternative. The challenge lies in cleanly cutting the can and creating the tiny perforations required.

To transform a soda can into a flat aluminum sheet, the ends must be removed and the body sliced open without bending the thin metal. [Saheen Palayi] designed a 3D-printed tool, resembling a pipe cutter, which gradually presses a utility knife blade into the rotating can until it cuts through. Once the top and bottom are removed, the side can be easily snipped to flatten the sheet.

The hole pattern is derived from the PCB design file. The cutting is done using a fiber laser, whose wavelength is well-suited for marking and cutting thin metals. Fiber lasers, once exclusive to industrial workshops, are now available to consumers. [Saheen] uses an xTool F1 Ultra laser, which cuts the stencil in seconds. The same laser has previously been used to create PCBs by removing excess copper to leave only the traces.

This method is faster than machining or chemical etching of aluminum cans. A video demonstration is available in the article’s accompanying media.
