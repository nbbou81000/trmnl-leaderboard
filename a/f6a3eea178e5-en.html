BirriaSDR: Turning a USB WiFi Dongle into an Experimental SDR Receiver

An open-source project repurposes a USB WiFi dongle's chip into a wideband SDR receiver, with no hardware modifications required.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The BirriaSDR project, created by Luigi Cruz and published on GitHub, repurposes a standard USB WiFi dongle into an experimental SDR (Software Defined Radio) receiver. The tested hardware uses a COMFAST dongle featuring MT7961 (digital) and MT7972 (analog) chips, originally designed for WiFi. The key insight is leveraging the wideband receiver and I/Q capture engine built into these components, which are typically used for calibration and debugging.

The libbirriasdr software, written in C11, configures the front-end and capture engine via the chip's registers. A 1,632-byte program, loaded directly into the chip's boot ROM, streams I/Q samples to the host over USB. No vendor firmware modifications are required, and no writes to flash or eFuse occur—all configurations are lost on power-off.

The receiver supports two frequency ranges: 2,156 to 2,610 MHz and 4,500 to 6,200 MHz, with a 14-bit resolution and a sampling rate of 40 million complex samples per second. A CyberEther flowgraph example is included to display a live spectrum without additional hardware. However, sensitivity, frequency accuracy, and gain remain uncalibrated.
