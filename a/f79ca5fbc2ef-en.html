Automated Machine for Winding Flip-Dot Electromagnet Coils

An engineer builds a machine to wind flip-dot electromagnet coils, cutting costs and complexity.

Flip-dot displays offer a retro aesthetic and mechanical operation, but their production is rare and expensive. Larry, an engineer, tackled this challenge by designing an automated machine to manufacture his own electromagnets, the core components of these displays. Each dot in a flip-dot screen is a small pivoting disc activated by an electromagnet. To flip its position, the controller sends an electrical pulse that changes the magnet’s polarity, pulling the disc in one direction or the other. An 8×8 panel requires 64 electromagnets, making industrial solutions costly.

Instead of purchasing them, Larry chose to build his own by winding copper wire coils. His machine mimics a sewing machine: a stepper motor spins a bobbin while another moves a guide to wrap the wire evenly. A Teensy 4.0 controls both motors via driver modules, with an interface featuring an OLED screen and a rotary encoder to adjust parameters. Users can specify the coil length and number of turns.

Larry admits the coils aren’t yet perfect due to residual flex in the system, but they far exceed what a human could achieve manually. The machine saves significant time and standardizes components, paving the way for more accessible DIY flip-dot projects.
