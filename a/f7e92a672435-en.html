ESP-WiFi-Jam-Detector: A Standalone Wi-Fi Interference Detector on ESP32-C5

A standalone ESP32-C5-based device scans 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz Wi-Fi bands for interference in real time.

Read the article on the TRMNL Leaderboard →

The ESP-WiFi-Jam-Detector is an open-source project that detects Wi-Fi interference on the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands using a Waveshare ESP32-C5-Touch-LCD-3.5 board. Unlike solutions requiring a computer, this standalone device leverages the ESP32-C5’s built-in Wi-Fi receiver to scan frequencies between 2400 and 2483.5 MHz, and 5150 to 5895 MHz, at a rate of 80 MS/s.

At startup, the device establishes a 60-second baseline of the ambient radiofrequency noise. It then triggers alerts for elevated wideband noise, persistent narrowband carriers, swept signals, or suspicious pulsed wideband energy. The touchscreen displays a live spectrum and waterfall view of detected signals. With an SD card inserted, it logs state changes and saves a waterfall image centered on alarms.

The firmware, licensed under GPL-3.0, is available as open source on GitHub, with a prebuilt binary provided in the repository’s releases. The project notes that the detector can only indicate changes in the RF environment, not prove malicious intent. Additionally, a running microwave oven can mimic interference on the 2.4 GHz band, potentially leading to false positives.
