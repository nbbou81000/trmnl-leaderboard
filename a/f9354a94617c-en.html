ESP-FLUTTER: A Robotic Butterfly That Actually Flies

A remote-controlled ornithopter with illuminated wings, controllable via Wi-Fi or RC transmitter.

Inspired by the grace of natural butterflies, ESP-FLUTTER is a flying robot that replicates wing flapping to generate lift, a more complex approach than traditional propeller drones. Unlike ornithopters using a single motor and mechanical linkage for both wings, this project employs two independent servos for better control while keeping the design simple.

The system is built around a XIAO ESP32C3 board from Seeed Studio, which handles servo signals, wireless communication, battery monitoring, and wing lighting. The robot can be controlled via a smartphone by connecting to its built-in Wi-Fi network and using a web interface. An optional ExpressLRS receiver also allows control with a conventional RC transmitter. The interface includes advanced settings: flapping frequency, steering response, servo positions, and motion profiles. A sine wave produces smoother movements, while a triangle wave increases lift. Its creator, Max Imagination, found that a 4 Hz flapping frequency works best at full throttle.

To keep the weight at just 70 grams, the frame is 3D-printed in PETG with flexible TPU guards protecting the servos during crashes. Carbon fiber rods provide the necessary rigidity without adding significant weight. The lightweight wings are made from thin plastic produce bags stretched over carbon fiber frames and secured with flexible adhesive. Alternatively, lightweight kite fabric can be used. Power comes from a custom two-cell LiPo battery, while a pair of high-speed coreless digital servos provides the flapping power. The wings feature 50 tiny surface-mount LEDs connected with ultra-thin enameled copper wire, adding only about a gram to each wing.

Two versions of ESP-FLUTTER are available: a modular design using readily accessible components and a more compact version built around a custom four-layer PCB. Both share the same core principles, offering builders a choice between easier assembly and a more integrated design.
