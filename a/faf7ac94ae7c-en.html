Home Assistant: Free Smart Home Hub with Local AI and Old GPUs

Turn an old GPU and a $150 mini-PC into a smart home hub with local AI, no subscriptions required.

Home Assistant (HASS) isn’t just a smart home controller—it’s a versatile automation hub capable of managing servers, NAS units, 3D printers, and even unconventional device combinations. The open-source platform also supports local AI models, enabling voice control and chatbot features similar to paid services like Alexa, but without recurring fees.

Lightweight local AI for smart homes

Unlike cloud-based platforms that lock AI assistants behind paywalls, HASS includes a dedicated section for connecting local AI models. The Gemma 4 E4B model (8 billion parameters) runs smoothly on an older GPU like a GTX 1080, delivering 40–50 tokens per second. For basic tasks—such as querying device states or toggling automation—this setup is more than adequate. Integrations with llama.cpp or Ollama allow local hosting, while tools like faster-whisper (speech-to-text) and Piper (text-to-speech) complete the voice pipeline.

A cloud-free voice assistant

HASS 2026.03 introduced voice satellite functionality, letting any smartphone or tablet detect wake words like “Okay, Nabu” to trigger voice commands. These devices can act as secondary interfaces, while AI processing remains entirely local. The initial cost is limited to a used mini-PC (around $150) and an existing GPU, making this a budget-friendly and privacy-focused alternative.

The security risks of IoT devices and cloud platforms have driven many users toward local solutions. HASS, paired with self-hosted AI models, provides a customizable, cost-effective, and data-respecting alternative to mainstream smart home services.
