SBC Shortages in 2026: How to Pick Viable Alternatives

SBC shortages like those of Raspberry Pi boards in 2026 push makers to explore alternatives. Here’s how to pick the right one.

Single-board computers (SBCs) like the Raspberry Pi are facing unprecedented shortages in 2026, driven by soaring RAM prices and their widespread use in drones. Entry-level models, such as the Pi Zero 2W, are particularly affected. As a result, exploring viable alternatives has become essential to keep projects feasible.

How to choose the right SBC

To assess an SBC’s viability, prioritize models supported by Armbian or DietPi, lightweight Linux distributions. Their software compatibility ensures no major roadblocks. A quick check on their download pages reveals supported boards, helping avoid models with undocumented interfaces.

Hardware interfaces (GPIO, I2C, UART, SPI) are generally standardized, thanks to the Raspberry Pi’s 40-pin header popularity. Most SBCs offer I2C pins at positions 2/3 and 27/28, UART at 8/10, and SPI at 19/21/23/24. However, some boards like the Radxa Zero v1.5 have anomalies (duplicated or non-connected pins) due to hardware constraints. For projects requiring specific interfaces (parallel RGB, WS2812, GPS PPS), verify the presence of drivers or DeviceTree overlays in Armbian repositories.

HDMI and Ethernet ports (even non-Gigabit) are nearly universal, while USB 3.0 and PCIe via FFC connectors are becoming more common. Integrated WiFi modules can be problematic, especially SDIO-connected ones, but a USB dongle often resolves the issue. Camera (CSI) and display (DSI) interfaces vary by manufacturer: some, like Radxa, provide tested modules, while others leave compatibility to the user.

Compute Module-compatible boards offer a compelling alternative, with extended hardware compatibility for basic interfaces. Chinese-made models, often cheaper, replicate essential features without Raspberry Pi’s proprietary quirks.
