Arduino UNO Q: One Year of Community Projects and Embedded AI

Arduino UNO Q celebrates its first year with an active community and diverse projects leveraging its dual-core processor setup.

A year ago, Arduino announced its partnership with Qualcomm and the launch of the UNO Q, a board combining an STM32U585 real-time microcontroller and a Qualcomm Dragonwing QRB2210 processor. The latter features an AI accelerator, quad-core performance, and a GPU, enabling Linux and AI model execution, while the STM32 handles critical real-time tasks.

This dual-core setup allows a single project to integrate an Arduino sketch on the microcontroller and a Python program on the main processor, packaged via Arduino App Lab. This approach simplifies the development of complex applications that previously required multiple boards or separate systems.

In twelve months, the community responded massively: over 4,000 participants submitted 250 official projects, and nearly 850 discussions were created on the Arduino Forum. The number of projects shared on the Project Hub nearly doubled, while Arduino library downloads increased by 50%, reaching 244 million in a year. The Arduino IDE was also downloaded over 35 million times.

Notable projects include a DIY-ECG for real-time health monitoring, computer vision applications, local language models (LLMs), and tools for music and home automation. Arduino also opened underlying tools like App Lab, available on GitHub under open-source licenses, allowing developers to contribute and adapt the ecosystem.

The company expanded its offerings with new products like the Starter Kit R4, the Nesso N1, and eight Modulino modules (motors, joystick, relays, etc.), designed to enhance UNO Q-based projects. Additionally, the integration of Arduino Core on Zephyr reached version 1.0, providing a solid RTOS foundation for Arduino boards, including UNO Q and the new, more powerful VENTUNO Q.

Arduino and Qualcomm also launched the Works with Arduino program, enabling partners to certify compatible modules and deploy applications developed on UNO Q or VENTUNO Q without modifications. Tools like Edge Impulse and Foundries.io complement the ecosystem for production-grade pipelines and secure fleet updates.

Recent announcements, such as the VENTUNO Q, aim to meet more demanding needs while maintaining a familiar development experience. The community was showcased at events like CES, MWC, and the Mission44 Workshop, where students explored applications like AI-powered racing telemetry.
